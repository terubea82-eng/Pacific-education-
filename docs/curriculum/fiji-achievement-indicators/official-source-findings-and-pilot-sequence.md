### Source B — Year 6 and Year 8 Teachers Guide, Term 3 (2015): disaster-awareness content

The same guide includes a Red Cross / Girl Guides / Boys Scouts section with a 25-lesson progression. It provides achievement statements without the same coded PE indicator format. Keep these as cross-curricular safety-learning statements, not as a PE indicator or a substitute for a subject syllabus:

- Identify and discuss natural and human-made disasters that affect Fiji.
- Determine the type of disaster and state and practise relevant precautions.
- Explain safe procedures during a disaster and actions to take afterwards.
- Formulate and explain what people should do during and after disasters.

The guide sequences the 25 lessons in five blocks: lessons 1–5 discuss disaster types and effects; 6–10 collect and organise pictures; 11–15 practise precautions; 16–20 discuss safe actions during and after a disaster; 21–25 create posters, poems or essays and role-play preparedness. This is the guide's sequence, not a Ministry-prescribed 115-day distribution.

### Source C — Literacy Guide for Years 3 & 4 (Ministry of Education, Heritage & Arts)

- Official Ministry-hosted URL: https://www.education.gov.fj/wp-content/uploads/2022/06/Literacy_Guide_-for_years_34.pdf
- Use: practical teacher strategies and assessment support for Years 3 and 4 literacy.
- Verified teaching components: oral language, shared reading, sight words, phonics/word work, reading and writing.
- The guide recommends prediction, read-aloud/shared reading, comprehension questions grounded in the text, identifying where answers appear in the text, five new sight words, syllable work, word analysis and writing/comprehension tasks.
- The guide describes reading-development stages and ongoing observation/checklists. These are useful planning and diagnostic references, but should not be converted into invented official indicator codes.
- Day-by-day lesson design based on this guide must be labelled as a teacher-designed sequence until matched to the applicable current Year 3/4 English syllabus outcomes.



### Source D — Na Noda Mataniciva: ECE outcomes (Ministry-hosted guideline)

- Official Ministry-hosted URL: https://www.education.gov.fj/wp-content/uploads/2022/06/Na-Noda-Mataniciva.pdf
- The guideline says the FALD framework also informs Classes 1 and 2. It explains that teachers should plan from the more detailed sub-strand outcomes and unpack the concepts, skills and attitudes rather than treating the broad FALD statements as daily indicators.
- The six FALD and their broad outcome focus are recorded below as concise paraphrases:

| FALD | Broad outcome focus (paraphrased) | Example sub-strand focus |
|---|---|---|
| Physical Development, Health and Well-being | Build physical skills and begin healthy, active and safe routines. | Gross/fine/perceptual-motor development; nutrition; hygiene; safe practices. |
| Learning to Know | Observe, explore, investigate, communicate, question, solve problems and use logical thinking. | Inquiry and investigation; representing and symbolic thinking; early mathematics. |
| Language, Literacy and Communication | Communicate effectively in the child's first language and develop early literacy foundations. | Listening/responding; speaking/communicating; becoming a reader and writer. |
| Living and Learning Together | Contribute positively to personal and others' social and emotional well-being and learning. | Sense of self; relationships; culture and respect for diversity; emotions and behaviour. |
| Aesthetics, Creativity and the Arts | Develop observation and expression, and grow in describing, interpreting, appreciating, creating and reflecting. | Music, movement and drama; art and craft; aesthetics. |
| Moral and Spiritual Development | Develop awareness of beliefs and practices, a sense of right and wrong, and responsible behaviour towards people and the environment. | Spiritual awareness; moral development. |

**ECE example outcome:** the guideline's gross-motor sub-strand includes progressively improving throwing, catching and kicking accuracy. A teacher-designed activity may practise these through safe, age-appropriate play, but the outcome must be recorded as an ECE sub-strand outcome, not assigned an invented numerical indicator code.

**ECE source limitation:** The Ministry announced in September 2025 that Na Noda Mataniciva was being updated with UNICEF support for early literacy and numeracy. This register records the Ministry-hosted guideline located during this review; it does not claim that this is the completed revised edition. Confirm the current version before final ECE pilot approval.

## 3. Proposed 25-day pilot sequence — Year 6 and Year 8 cross-curricular disaster awareness

**Source basis:** Source B above. **Status:** teacher-designed day allocation derived from the guide's five lesson blocks; not an official Ministry day-by-day timetable. Have a Fiji curriculum/teacher reviewer approve the sequence before learner release. Do not attach it to a subject assessment unless the school verifies the appropriate subject syllabus outcome.

| Term 3 week | Day | Teacher-designed learning focus | Suggested evidence |
|---|---:|---|---|
| 1 | 1 | Name natural and human-made hazards familiar in Fiji. | Oral, signed, drawn or written examples. |
| 1 | 2 | Sort examples into natural and human-made categories. | Correctly classified picture or word cards. |
| 1 | 3 | Describe possible effects of one selected hazard on people and communities. | Short explanation, drawing or recorded response. |
| 1 | 4 | Compare effects of two different hazards. | Comparison chart or oral explanation. |
| 1 | 5 | Explain why preparation matters before a hazard occurs. | Short response or group discussion observation. |
| 2 | 6 | Find or select suitable pictures representing different hazards. | Teacher-observed selection and identification. |
| 2 | 7 | Label pictures with hazard names and categories. | Labelled picture cards. |
| 2 | 8 | Group pictures by hazard type and explain the grouping. | Categorised chart and explanation. |
| 2 | 9 | Present a chart of hazards that can affect Fiji. | Completed chart and presentation. |
| 2 | 10 | Explain one hazard shown on the chart and its possible effects. | Oral, signed, written or drawn explanation. |
| 3 | 11 | Identify precautions relevant to a selected hazard. | Precaution list or picture sequence. |
| 3 | 12 | Match hazards with suitable precautions. | Correct matching with explanation. |
| 3 | 13 | Demonstrate a safe preparation action using a teacher-approved scenario. | Practical observation checklist. |
| 3 | 14 | Explain why a selected precaution reduces risk. | Short reasoned response. |
| 3 | 15 | Practise and review a safe-preparation sequence. | Teacher observation and learner reflection. |
| 4 | 16 | Identify safe actions during a teacher-approved disaster scenario. | Ordered action cards or oral response. |
| 4 | 17 | Identify actions to avoid during the scenario. | Safe/unsafe sorting with reasons. |
| 4 | 18 | Explain what to do after the immediate danger has passed. | Sequenced response. |
| 4 | 19 | Role-play safe actions during and after a scenario. | Practical performance rubric. |
| 4 | 20 | Review and explain the complete before/during/after safety sequence. | Completed sequence and explanation. |
| 5 | 21 | Plan a poster, poem or short written message about preparedness. | Plan/checklist. |
| 5 | 22 | Draft the poster, poem or message. | First draft. |
| 5 | 23 | Improve the work for accuracy, clarity and safety. | Revised draft and teacher feedback. |
| 5 | 24 | Present or role-play the preparedness message. | Presentation/performance evidence. |
| 5 | 25 | Reflect on and explain key preparedness actions. | Exit response or accessible reflection. |

For Year 6 and Year 8, retain the same core learning focus but adjust depth, independence and explanation demand. Copper/Bronze/Silver/Gold versions must not change the underlying learning objective. Provide accessible oral, signed, tactile, drawing or written response modes where authorised and appropriate.

## 4. Curriculum and assessment safeguards

1. Keep the Fiji NCF as the framework reference, but map each class and subject against its current official syllabus, scope-and-sequence and teacher guide.
2. Record the issuing authority, exact title, edition/year, effective status, page/section, URL and indicator code (if one exists).
3. Distinguish an official indicator from a teacher-written objective, lesson focus, activity or assessment criterion.
4. Allocate the verified outcomes across T1 45 days, T2 45 days and T3 25 days only after prerequisites, scope, school calendar and assessment windows are reviewed.
5. Keep every unverified record PENDING_TEACHER_REVIEW; do not let it authorise formal assessment or a curriculum-alignment claim.
6. The Ministry assessment policy says assessment through Class 8 and Forms 3–4 is for learning and development, not student ranking or selection. Use diagnostic progress reports at these levels.
7. The Ministry's free educational resources policy limits school reproduction to classroom purposes and directs other use to the Permanent Secretary. PacEdu must obtain any permission needed for wider platform redistribution. This register paraphrases and records short indicator statements; it does not republish the teacher guides.
