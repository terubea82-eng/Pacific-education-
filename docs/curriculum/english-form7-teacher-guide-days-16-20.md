# Form 7 English — Week 4 Teacher Marking Guide (Days 16–20)

**Status:** PENDING_TEACHER_REVIEW and PENDING_OFFICIAL_INDICATOR_VERIFICATION. Original practice sources only; not prescribed texts, Ministry materials or past examination questions.

## Common marking principles
- Assess critical synthesis, method awareness, precision of evidence, evaluation of omission, qualification and sustained cohesion.
- Accept well-supported alternative interpretations and accessible oral, signed, AAC, dictated, Braille or written responses. Do not score accent, disability-related communication differences, handwriting speed or device access as errors.
- Record evidence, response mode, independence and prompts. Keep PENDING_TEACHER_REVIEW until educator review.
- Copper/Bronze/Silver/Gold are flexible scaffolds, not permanent learner labels.

## Day 16 — Oral communication
**Expected:** sustained recommendation, synthesis of both sources, qualification and response to whether submission numbers prove fair participation.
- Committee summary records 84 written submissions and grouped themes, but does not publish all submissions or explain duplicate counting.
- Vendor association identifies possible participation barriers caused by unclear feedback routes and meeting times that conflict with market hours. It represents members, not every trader or resident.
- Submission numbers alone do not prove that all groups were heard. Credit practical improvements such as varied meeting times, direct outreach, accessible feedback routes, publication of themes and a transparent response log.
- Next step: distinguish volume of participation from breadth, accessibility and influence.

## Day 17 — Close reading
**Expected:** comparative analysis of framing, voice, diction, authority and omission.
- Official profile uses abstract positive language (“transformed”, “shared vision”) and attributes success to leadership and partnerships.
- Personal account uses first-person detail and contrast: the walkway is easier for visitors, but a ramp is blocked and absent residents are rarely quoted.
- Credit analysis of the difference between institutional framing and situated experience.
- An omission is a textual gap; it does not alone prove deliberate concealment or intent.
- Next step: separate textual effect, author purpose and evidence of intent.

## Day 18 — Vocabulary and language
**Expected:** contextual analysis of connotation, register and certainty, with an accurate rewrite.
- “Real participation” asserts a standard of meaningful involvement; “tick-box” is dismissive and implies superficial compliance.
- “Completed in accordance with the published timetable” confirms procedure, not whether participants influenced the outcome.
- “Does not evaluate” identifies a gap in the report's assessment.
- A precise rewrite: “The committee completed three scheduled engagement meetings and recorded attendance; the report does not establish whether participants influenced the final design or whether accessible alternatives were available.”
- Next step: distinguish process evidence from impact evidence.

## Day 19 — Critical literacy
**Expected:** method-aware evaluation of numerical data and qualitative reports.
- Survey result: 71% of 312 respondents supported the redesign. It describes respondents, not necessarily the whole community.
- Missing method details include distribution channel, number invited, response rate, weighting, age/location/access profile and duplicate controls.
- Community note raises plausible access concerns for people without reliable internet, but four coordinators' comments do not quantify the gap or represent all groups.
- Further methods: accessible non-digital survey, stratified sampling, outreach through multiple groups, response-rate reporting and follow-up interviews.
- Next step: distinguish sample, respondents, target population and representativeness.

## Day 20 — Writing and shaping
**Expected:** sustained essay with thesis, integrated evidence, method evaluation, counterargument and accountability recommendations.
- Project review records 312 online responses and three meetings but lacks distribution method and a record of how concerns affected the final design.
- Coalition welcomes the contact address but asks for non-digital options, translated summaries where needed, a response log and follow-up review; it also acknowledges improvements to some facilities.
- Strong thesis: recorded activity is necessary but insufficient to prove meaningful participation; influence and accessibility must be demonstrated.
- Credit recommendations such as accessible participation channels, published response-to-feedback log, design-change rationale, follow-up review and independent evaluation.
- Next step: revise for thesis consistency, source integration, fair counterargument and evidence-based conclusion.

## Pathway evidence
- Copper: identifies accurate evidence from each source with labels, model and recorded prompts.
- Bronze: organises comparison using a grid and planning frame, reducing prompts.
- Silver: independently integrates evidence and evaluates method/omission.
- Gold: develops nuanced methodological critique and evaluates accountability implications while retaining the shared objective.
