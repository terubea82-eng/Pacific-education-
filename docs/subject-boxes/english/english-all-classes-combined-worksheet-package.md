## Mandatory worksheet quality and release rules

1. Each daily learner worksheet must stand alone and include learner name, class/level, subject, term, week, day/date and ability pathway.
2. Include the full passage, instructions, worked example, questions and generous answer spaces needed without another group's sheet.
3. Provide four genuinely differentiated pathways with a shared learning objective and appropriate Bloom progression.
4. Include a separate matching teacher marking guide for each pathway. Keep answer keys out of learner-facing downloads.
5. Accept equivalent wording and keyword/similar-word credit where the answer demonstrates the intended concept.
6. Use A/B/C/D multiple-choice options where a selected-response item is appropriate.
7. Provide accessible response options for Deaf, Blind and DeafBlind learners; do not make colour, audio, vision or camera the only route.
8. Use recent learner evidence to select support; ability pathway is not a permanent label.
9. Keep exact official Fiji syllabus wording separate from PacEdu-authored learning targets. Record source edition, outcome/indicator, page/section, reviewer and date before marking a worksheet source-verified.

## Worksheet A — Support (0–49%)
**Name/learner ID:** __________ **Date:** __________  
**Teacher:** __________ **Ability group:** Support  
**Learning goal:** Find a name and one object in a short sentence.

**Do this:** Listen to your teacher or use the audio button. Read the sentence. Circle or say the answer.

**Example:** “Mere has a bag.” Who has a bag? **Mere.**

**Read/listen:** “Jone has a red book.”

1. Who has a book?  
   ☐ Jone  ☐ Mere
2. What does Jone have?  
   ☐ a book  ☐ a ball
3. What colour is the book?  
   ☐ red  ☐ blue
4. Complete: “Jone has a ____.”  
   Word bank: **book / fish**

**Try by yourself:** Draw or choose a picture of a book.

**Teacher marking:** 1 mark each for Q1–Q4 (4 marks); Q4 accepts “book”. Picture task is participation evidence, not a literacy mark.  
**Next step:** If the learner answers with support, repeat with a new one-sentence text; reduce prompts gradually.

## Worksheet B — Developing (50–64%)
**Name/learner ID:** __________ **Date:** __________  
**Teacher:** __________ **Ability group:** Developing  
**Learning goal:** Find stated details in two simple sentences.

**Do this:** Read/listen. Tick or write the answer.

**Example:** “Ana has two pens. One pen is blue.” How many pens? **Two.**

**Read/listen:** “Samu walks to school with his sister. He carries a green bag.”

1. Who walks to school? __________
2. Who walks with Samu? __________
3. What colour is the bag? __________
4. Tick the correct sentence:  
   ☐ Samu carries a green bag.  
   ☐ Samu carries a red bag.
5. Write one word naming something you carry to school. __________

**Teacher marking:** 1 mark each (5 marks). Accept an oral answer for a learner who is being assessed orally, and record the response mode.  
**Next step:** Move to fewer prompts when the learner consistently finds details in new texts.

## Worksheet C — Expected (65–79%)
**Name/learner ID:** __________ **Date:** __________  
**Teacher:** __________ **Ability group:** Expected  
**Learning goal:** Answer detail questions and make a simple inference.

**Do this:** Read/listen twice if needed. Answer in a word or short sentence.

**Example:** “It is raining. Jo opens an umbrella.” Why does Jo open it? **To keep the rain off.**

**Read/listen:** “The class plants a bean seed in a pot. Ana waters it and puts it near sunlight. Every Friday, the class measures the plant.”

1. What does the class plant? __________
2. What two things does Ana do? __________
3. When does the class measure the plant? __________
4. Why does the class measure it each week? __________
5. Write one sentence about how to care for a plant. __________

**Teacher marking:** Q1=1, Q2=2 (both actions), Q3=1, Q4=1 for a sensible evidence-based reason, Q5=1 for a relevant sentence. Total 6.  
**Next step:** Ask the learner to explain an answer using a detail from the text.

## Worksheet D — Advanced (80–100%)
**Name/learner ID:** __________ **Date:** __________  
**Teacher:** __________ **Ability group:** Advanced  
**Learning goal:** Use text evidence, explain cause, and suggest a fair improvement.

**Do this:** Read carefully. Answer in complete sentences where asked.

**Example:** “The soil is dry, so Litia waters the plant.” What caused Litia to water it? **The soil was dry.**

**Read/listen:** “Class 1 planted two bean seeds in separate pots. Both pots received the same amount of water. Pot A was kept near sunlight; Pot B was kept in a dark cupboard. After one week, the class recorded each plant’s height.”

1. What did the class change between the two pots? __________
2. Name one thing the class kept the same. __________
3. Why is it useful to keep the water amount the same? __________
4. What should the class record before deciding which plant grew better? __________
5. The class repeats the test, but Pot B gets less water. Explain why this makes the sunlight comparison less fair. __________
6. Write a two-sentence conclusion using evidence the class would need to collect. Do not invent results. __________

**Teacher marking:** Q1=1; Q2=1; Q3=2 for fair comparison; Q4=1 for measured heights/observations; Q5=2 for identifying a second changed variable; Q6=2 for a cautious evidence-based conclusion. Total 9.  
**Next step:** Extend with a new short text and ask the learner to compare evidence, explain limitations, and justify a conclusion.

## Accessibility alternatives (same learning goal)
- **Blind/low-vision:** Supply a screen-reader-friendly text file and spoken directions; ensure audio can be replayed and stopped; offer braille/raised-letter cards or safe tactile objects where appropriate. Do not make color the only clue; name the colour aloud.
- **Deaf/hard-of-hearing:** Show the full written instructions and transcript; caption any video; use clear pictures/sequence cards and a sign-language video only where the learner's preferred sign language is known. Never make audio the only source of information.
- **Other access needs:** Allow pointing, typing, AAC, oral response, or a scribe where appropriate; note the response mode without lowering the learning goal automatically.

## Teacher quick guide
1. Select the learner's current evidence-based group for **English**; a learner may be in a different group in another subject.
2. Read the short instruction and show the worked example.
3. Let the learner attempt independently before giving a hint.
4. Record the score and the type of support used.
5. Use more than one task over time before changing a group. The 80% band is a pilot flag, not a permanent label.
6. Keep the answer key in the teacher view. Release the student worksheet only through the existing teacher approval flow.

# Class 2 English — four ability worksheets

Status: **original pilot material; official Fiji syllabus outcomes not yet mapped or teacher-verified.** Confirm the current Class/Year 2 English syllabus before classroom release.

**Teacher setup:** Write the date and current learning outcome on the student copy. Give one ability version based on recent English evidence; reassess regularly. Suggested bands: Support 0–49%, Developing 50–64%, Expected 65–79%, Advanced 80–100%. These are flexible support bands, not fixed labels.

## Worksheet A — Support (0–49%)

**Learning focus:** Read a short sentence, find key words, and write a simple sentence.

**Example:** “Mere has a bag.” Who has a bag? **Mere.**

Read: “A fish is in the pond. Joeli sees the fish.”

1. Who sees the fish?
2. Where is the fish?
3. Circle the correct word: fish / dish — “A ___ is in the pond.”
4. Fill the gap: Joeli ___ the fish. (sees / sleeps)
5. Put the words in order: **has / Mere / a book**
6. Write one sentence about something you see at school.

**Teacher marking guide (10 marks):** Q1 Joeli (1); Q2 in the pond (1); Q3 fish (1); Q4 sees (1); Q5 “Mere has a book.” (2); Q6 meaningful sentence, capital and full stop (up to 4). Accept equivalent clear wording.

**Next step:** Practise reading short sentences aloud or silently, then identify who/what/where.

## Worksheet B — Developing (50–64%)

**Learning focus:** Understand a short passage and use complete sentences.

**Example:** “Litia waters the plant.” What does Litia do? **She waters the plant.**

Read: “On Monday, Saki and Ana planted bean seeds near their classroom. They watered the seeds each morning. After some days, small green shoots appeared.”

1. What did Saki and Ana plant?
2. Where did they plant the seeds?
3. When did they water the seeds?
4. What appeared after some days?
5. Choose the best title: (a) The Lost Shoe (b) Bean Seeds (c) A Bus Ride.
6. Write two sentences about caring for a plant.

**Teacher marking guide (10 marks):** Q1 bean seeds (1); Q2 near their classroom (1); Q3 each morning (1); Q4 small green shoots (1); Q5 (b) (1); Q6 two relevant sentences (up to 5: meaning 3, sentence punctuation/capitals 2).

**Next step:** Answer who/what/where/when questions using evidence from the passage.

## Worksheet C — Expected (65–79%)

**Learning focus:** Infer information and explain an idea from a passage.

**Example:** If the ground is wet after rain, it probably rained recently. This is an inference supported by a clue.

Read: “Before break, the class put rubbish into the correct bins. When the wind blew, one empty wrapper escaped from an uncovered bin. Alipate picked it up and told the class that the lid should be closed.”

1. What did the class do before break?
2. Why did the wrapper escape?
3. What clue shows that Alipate cared for the school?
4. Suggest a better title.
5. What lesson can readers learn? Give one reason from the passage.
6. Write a short, four-sentence paragraph about keeping your school clean.

**Teacher marking guide (12 marks):** Q1 sorted rubbish into bins (1); Q2 wind blew it from uncovered bin (2); Q3 picked it up/told class to close lid (2); Q4 suitable title (1); Q5 reasonable lesson plus evidence (2); Q6 relevant paragraph (up to 4: ideas 2, order 1, punctuation/capitals 1).

**Next step:** Support opinions with a detail from the text.

## Worksheet D — Advanced (80–100%)

**Learning focus:** Compare ideas, evaluate a solution, and write a persuasive paragraph.

Read: “Two groups wanted to reduce litter. Group A placed a sign beside the bins. Group B placed a sign, explained how to sort waste, and checked the bins after lunch for one week. By Friday, Group B found fewer items on the ground, but the class had not counted the litter before the project began.”

1. Name one difference between the two groups' plans.
2. What evidence suggests Group B's plan may have helped?
3. Why can the class not be completely certain that the plan caused the improvement?
4. Propose one fair way to check whether the plan works next week.
5. Write a persuasive paragraph (5–7 sentences) encouraging learners to reduce litter. Include a clear claim, two reasons, and a practical action.

**Teacher marking guide (12 marks):** Q1 explanation plus checking/teaching difference (2); Q2 fewer items by Friday (2); Q3 no starting count / other factors possible (2); Q4 fair repeated count using same place/time/method (2); Q5 claim (1), two relevant reasons (2), practical action and clear writing (1).

**Next step:** Evaluate evidence, recognise limits, and propose a fair check.

## Accessibility and delivery

- **Blind/low vision:** Provide clean linear text for screen readers, large print as needed, verbal reading on request, and Braille/tactile materials through the school's support arrangements. Do not require color-based answers.
- **Deaf/hard of hearing:** All instructions and passage content are written; provide clear picture cues where useful. No task depends on hearing a recording.
- Allow oral, typed, dictated, or supported responses where the learning outcome permits. Do not change the assessed skill without documenting the adjustment.

**Teacher release check:** Confirm the current Fiji syllabus outcome, adjust local vocabulary, check reading level, retain this answer key in the teacher workspace, and approve before student release.

# Class 3 English — four ability worksheets

Status: original pilot worksheets; verify and record the current Fiji Class/Year 3 English syllabus outcome before classroom release. Ability bands are flexible, subject-specific guides: Support 0–49%, Developing 50–64%, Expected 65–79%, Advanced 80–100%.

## A. Support (0–49%) — Find details and write sentences

**Example:** “Tui has a pencil.” Who has a pencil? Tui.

Read: “Pita went to the library with his cousin. He borrowed a book about fish. At home, he read the book to his younger sister.”

1. Where did Pita go?
2. Who went with Pita?
3. What was the book about?
4. Who listened to the story?
5. Choose the correct word: Pita (borrowed / cooked) a book.
6. Write one sentence about a book you like.

**Teacher key (10 marks):** library (1); cousin (1); fish (1); younger sister (1); borrowed (1); relevant sentence with capital and full stop (up to 5).

## B. Developing (50–64%) — Sequence and vocabulary

**Example:** First means it happened before the next event.

Read: “Heavy rain fell overnight. In the morning, the school path was muddy. The class monitor placed a warning sign near the slippery part and told the teacher. The teacher arranged a safer path.”

1. What happened overnight?
2. Why was the path muddy?
3. What did the monitor do first?
4. What did the teacher arrange?
5. Put in order: teacher arranges a safe path / rain falls / monitor reports the path.
6. Write three sentences explaining how to stay safe on a wet path.

**Teacher key (10 marks):** rain fell (1); rain made the path muddy (1); placed a sign/reported it (2); safer path (1); rain → monitor acts/reports → teacher arranges path (2); relevant safety paragraph (3).

## C. Expected (65–79%) — Infer and summarise

Read: “Mila wanted to grow vegetables beside her home. The soil was dry, so she added compost and watered it. She planted bean seeds and marked the rows with sticks. Each afternoon, she checked the soil. When several days passed without rain, she asked her family to help save water for the garden.”

1. What problem did Mila notice?
2. How did compost and water help her prepare?
3. Why did she mark the rows?
4. What can you infer about Mila? Give a clue from the passage.
5. Summarise the passage in two sentences.
6. Suggest one question you would ask Mila about her garden.

**Teacher key (12 marks):** dry soil (1); prepared soil for planting (2); identify rows/seed places (2); reasonable inference supported by clue (2); accurate concise summary (3); relevant question (2).

## D. Advanced (80–100%) — Compare evidence and persuade

Read: “Two classes tried to save water. Class A put up a reminder sign. Class B put up a sign, checked taps at lunchtime, and recorded water-meter readings each Friday. After four weeks, Class B recorded lower water use. Neither class recorded a starting reading before the project.”

1. Name two differences between the approaches.
2. What evidence suggests Class B's approach may have helped?
3. Why is it difficult to prove the project caused the change?
4. Suggest a fairer way to compare the classes.
5. Write a persuasive paragraph (6–8 sentences) proposing one way your school could save water. Include a claim, reasons, evidence you would collect, and a respectful call to action.

**Teacher key (12 marks):** two valid differences (2); lower recorded use (2); no baseline/other causes possible (2); same baseline, period and recording method (2); paragraph: claim (1), reasons/evidence (2), clear respectful action and structure (1).

## Access and release

Keep all instructions written; captions or visual sequences may supplement but never replace essential text. Permit oral, typed or supported responses where they assess the same skill. Keep this key teacher-only. Confirm syllabus outcome and reading level before releasing.

## A. Support (0–49%) — Literal comprehension

Read: “Ravi and his grandmother visited the market. They bought taro, tomatoes, and fish. Ravi carried the bag while his grandmother paid the seller.”

**Example:** Who visited the market? Ravi and his grandmother.

1. Where did they go?
2. Name two things they bought.
3. Who carried the bag?
4. Who paid the seller?
5. Write one sentence about a market.

**Teacher key (8 marks):** market (1); any two listed items (2); Ravi (1); grandmother (1); relevant sentence with capital/full stop (up to 3).

## B. Developing (50–64%) — Sequence and main idea

Read: “The school garden looked dry after a week without rain. The gardening group loosened the soil, added compost, and watered the seedlings carefully. They covered the soil with dry leaves to slow water loss. The teacher asked them to record plant growth every Friday.”

1. What made the garden look dry?
2. List two actions the group took.
3. Why might dry leaves help the soil?
4. What did the teacher ask the group to record?
5. Write a suitable main idea in one sentence.
6. Write a four-step instruction for caring for a seedling.

**Teacher key (10 marks):** week without rain (1); any two correct actions (2); helps reduce water loss (2); plant growth (1); accurate main idea (2); logical steps (2).

## C. Expected (65–79%) — Fact, inference and summary

Read: “A community team planned a clean-up beside a stream. Before beginning, they checked the area for sharp objects and asked an adult to handle unsafe waste. They separated reusable items from rubbish and recorded the number of bags collected. The team planned to return a month later to see whether litter had returned.”

1. State two actions that made the clean-up safer.
2. Why did the team count the bags?
3. What can be inferred about the team's future plan?
4. Summarise the passage in two or three sentences.
5. Identify one detail that is a fact and one reasonable inference.

**Teacher key (12 marks):** checked for sharp objects and adult handled unsafe waste (3); measure amount/compare later (2); wants to monitor whether litter returns (2); accurate summary (3); valid fact/inference distinction (2).

## D. Advanced (80–100%) — Evaluate and write

A school newsletter says: “Our clean-up day solved the litter problem.” The report lists 12 bags collected on the first day but gives no later observations.

1. What evidence supports the claim that the team removed litter?
2. Why is “solved the litter problem” too strong a claim?
3. Give two pieces of evidence needed to test the claim.
4. Rewrite the statement so it is accurate and cautious.
5. Write a balanced newsletter paragraph (6–8 sentences) explaining the clean-up's achievement and what should happen next.

**Teacher key (12 marks):** 12 bags collected (2); no follow-up evidence (2); later counts and comparable locations/times (3); cautious rewrite (2); balanced paragraph with evidence and next step (3).

## Access and release
Supply screen-reader-friendly text, large print or tactile/Braille-ready materials as appropriate. Keep core information written; use captions and clear visuals as optional supports. ## Day 2 — Recognising and saying first sounds

## Teacher implementation and inclusion notes
- Accept spoken, signed, AAC, tactile selection, pointing, object matching, dictated or written responses according to the learner's communication access.
- Never score a learner down solely for not speaking, hearing, seeing, drawing or holding a pencil. Assess the English learning intention through an accessible response mode.
- Use real, safe familiar objects and large-print/high-contrast or tactile alternatives as needed.
- Give each learner only their own pathway worksheet. Do not require access to another group's paper.
- The pathway is a temporary instructional scaffold, not a permanent label. Reassess using current evidence.
- Teacher marking guides are not student handouts.
- Record teacher review and official source mapping separately. This pack's PacEdu indicators are authored for this draft and are not quoted official Fiji Ministry indicators.
