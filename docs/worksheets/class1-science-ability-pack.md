# Class 1 General Science — four ability worksheets

Status: original pilot activities. Verify the current Fiji Class/Year 1 Science syllabus and outcomes before release. Flexible subject-specific bands: Support 0–49%, Developing 50–64%, Expected 65–79%, Advanced 80–100%.

## A. Support (0–49%) — Living things and needs

**Example:** A plant is living. It needs water and light to grow.

1. Circle the living thing: stone / chicken.
2. Which does a plant need to grow: water / plastic?
3. Match: fish — water; bird — air.
4. Draw or describe one living thing near your home or school.
5. Name one way to care for a plant.

**Teacher guide (8 marks):** chicken (1); water (1); correct matches (2); suitable living thing (2); sensible care action (2).

## B. Developing (50–64%) — Materials and senses

**Example:** A stone is hard; a sponge is soft.

1. Name one hard object in the classroom.
2. Name one soft object.
3. Which material is usually transparent: clear glass or a wooden board?
4. Which sense helps you hear a bell?
5. Why should you never taste an unknown substance?
6. Sort these into natural or human-made: leaf, plastic bottle, shell, paper cup.

**Teacher guide (10 marks):** sensible examples (2); clear glass (1); hearing/ears (1); it may be harmful/unsafe (2); natural leaf and shell, human-made plastic bottle and paper cup (4).

## C. Expected (65–79%) — Observe and compare

Observe two leaves, or use teacher-provided pictures if real leaves are unavailable.

1. Write one way the leaves are alike.
2. Write two ways they are different.
3. What tools could help you compare their lengths?
4. Why should you avoid pulling leaves from a healthy plant unnecessarily?
5. Make a simple table with headings “Leaf A” and “Leaf B” and record two observations.

**Teacher guide (10 marks):** valid similarity (2); two differences (2); ruler/non-standard units with care (2); protects plant/avoids damage (2); table with two reasonable observations (2).

## D. Advanced (80–100%) — Fair test and explanation

A learner thinks a plant grows better near a window than inside a dark cupboard.

1. What is the question being investigated?
2. Name one thing to keep the same for both plants.
3. Name the one main thing to change.
4. What should the learner measure or observe each week?
5. Why should the learner not change water amount and light at the same time?
6. Predict what may happen and explain your reason.

**Teacher guide (12 marks):** light and growth question (2); same plant type/pot/soil/water amount (2); light exposure (2); height/leaf count/condition (2); only one main variable changes for a fair comparison (2); reasonable prediction linked to light (2).

## Access and release
Use real objects, tactile descriptions and large-print or screen-reader-friendly text. Do not require color identification or hearing as the only response mode. Use safe, non-toxic materials and adult supervision. Confirm current syllabus outcomes before release; keep the key teacher-only.
