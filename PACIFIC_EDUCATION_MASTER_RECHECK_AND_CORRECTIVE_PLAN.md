# Pacific Education — Master Recheck & Corrective Plan

## Purpose

This document consolidates the project-wide recheck requested by the owner: Pacific Education should be legally workable, achievable, sustainable, effective, efficient, professional and responsive to the needs of learners, teachers, parents/guardians, schools and authorized education stakeholders.

It is a **readiness and corrective-control document, not a legal certification**.

## 1. Current overall position

Pacific Education remains a controlled prototype/pilot project.

The GitHub Pages application is an untrusted client/prototype environment. It must not be represented as a secure production education service.

Production remains **BLOCKED** until the actual production controls, evidence, applicable professional reviews and explicit owner approval are completed.

## 2. Corrections made in this recheck

### A. Stage registry consistency

The Master Control registry now explicitly records Stages 20–30 and their actual contract status instead of stopping its numbered registry at Stage 19.

Stages 20–29 are recorded as in progress because their architecture/process contracts exist but implementation and verification remain outstanding.

Stage 30 is recorded as blocked until mandatory evidence and approvals are verified.

### B. Pilot-date consistency

The repository README defines the initial controlled pilot as **September 21, 2026–October 21, 2026**, with an owner-approved extension up to a total of three months if genuinely required.

The Master Control pilot transition date was corrected to match that current repository definition.

A pilot closing automatically must never mean automatic production approval.

### C. Production requirement coverage

The prototype production checklist has been expanded so the final gate does not omit:

- Privacy/data governance

- Monitoring and incident response
- Applicable legal/regulatory review

The checklist remains prototype-only and cannot itself create production authority.

### D. Master Control syntax/security correction

A malformed production-transition property in Master Control was corrected so the JavaScript object is syntactically valid.

The production transition remains fail-closed and requires server-side authority.

### E. Curriculum currency

Stage 26 was corrected to recognize that Fiji's curriculum framework and education policy are subject to review/change.

The production curriculum registry must therefore use versioned official-source records and distinguish current, superseded, historical, draft and under-review material.

### F. Legal-status wording

The README was corrected so Pacific Education does not claim legal compliance, Ministry approval or production readiness merely from repository implementation.

Appropriate Fiji legal/privacy, education-sector, cybersecurity, accessibility and child-safeguarding review remains an external prerequisite.

## 3. Legal and regulatory operating principle

Pacific Education should operate on a **compliance-by-evidence** model:

1. Identify the jurisdictions and services actually offered.
2. Identify applicable laws, regulations, policies, contracts and platform rules.
3. Obtain qualified advice where the issue exceeds technical design.
4. Convert applicable obligations into system and operational requirements.
5. Implement the controls.
6. Test them.
7. Record evidence.
8. Reassess when laws, policies, providers or product scope change.

Do not use a generic statement such as "GDPR compliant" or "Fiji compliant" without a qualified assessment of the actual processing and applicable requirements.

## 4. Fiji-specific current-context controls

The production curriculum and policy process must remain version-aware because Fiji's education framework is undergoing review.

The project should monitor official Ministry of Education publications and use the applicable authorized curriculum source for each class/form/subject.

Fiji's Online Safety Act 2018 must be considered for online communication and harmful electronic communications.

Privacy requirements must be reviewed against the law and policy framework actually in force when the relevant service launches. Government endorsed a National Privacy and Personal Data Protection Policy in 2025 as a foundation for proposed privacy legislation; this means the project must not assume that today's policy position is permanently static.

- Progress

- Curriculum references

### Layer 2 — Trust and safeguarding

- Identity
- Role

- Privacy

- Incident handling

### Layer 3 — Operations

## 6. User-needs acceptance criteria

### Learners

The service should provide:

- Clear daily learning
- Age/level-appropriate activities
- Understandable progress
- Safe interaction
- Low-bandwidth resilience
- Accessible presentation
- No unnecessary personal-data collection

### Teachers

The service should provide:

- Curriculum traceability
- Practical lesson support
- Progress visibility within authorized scope
- Assessment evidence
- Revision and coverage support
- Useful reporting without unnecessary administration

### Parents/guardians

The service should provide:

- Clear authorized visibility
- Safe relationship controls
- Understandable progress information
- Appropriate communication boundaries
- Clear support and privacy information

### Schools/authorized education stakeholders

The service should provide:

- Controlled access
- Role-based administration
- Auditable records
- Curriculum/version traceability
- Operational support
- Reliable reporting
- Clear data-governance responsibilities

## 7. Efficiency principles

To remain achievable and sustainable:

- Do not build production infrastructure before the product requirements are stable enough to justify it.
- Do not duplicate curriculum content when an authoritative reference/mapping is sufficient.
- Prefer reusable services and shared validation over repeated bespoke logic.
- Keep prototype-only code clearly separated from production authority.
- Automate testing and evidence collection where automation is reliable.
- Require human review where judgment, safeguarding, legal interpretation or curriculum authority is involved.
- Minimize data collection, storage and operational complexity.
- Use staged rollout rather than attempting a nationwide production launch immediately.

## 8. Sustainability controls

Sustainability requires an operating plan covering:

- Hosting and infrastructure cost
- Domain and service costs
- Payment-provider fees
- Support and maintenance
- Security monitoring
- Backups
- Curriculum updates
- Accessibility testing
- Professional review
- Incident response
- Tax/accounting obligations
- Staff/vendor responsibilities
- Funding runway
- Disaster recovery

Pricing must be tested against the real cost of delivering the service. Affordability and sustainability must be evaluated together rather than assuming a low price will automatically be profitable.

## 9. Professional quality system

Every production feature should move through:

**Requirement → Design → Implementation → Test → Review → Evidence → Approval → Release → Monitoring → Improvement**

For high-risk features:

**Requirement → Specialist review → Implementation → Security/safeguarding/privacy testing → Independent verification → Approval → Release**

## 10. Evidence hierarchy

Evidence should be weighted appropriately:

1. Authoritative legal/regulatory or Ministry source
2. Qualified professional/independent review
3. Production-system server evidence
4. Controlled test evidence
5. Pilot/user evidence
6. Prototype/client evidence

Prototype evidence cannot substitute for higher-level evidence.

## 11. Release gate correction

The production gate must evaluate a single authoritative readiness model containing, at minimum:

- Backend/database
- Authentication
- Authorization/relationships
- Infrastructure/hosting
- Cybersecurity
- Privacy/data governance
- Child safeguarding
- Accessibility/device/low-bandwidth
- Curriculum source validation
- Pilot/user testing
- Payments/business operations
- Backup/recovery
- Monitoring/incident response
- Applicable legal/regulatory review
- Owner approval
- Required specialist/independent reviews

A browser-side registry may display this status but must never be able to create production authority.

## 12. Change-control rule

Any material change to:

- Authentication
- Authorization
- Child data
- Curriculum
- Assessments/marks
- Payment
- Database
- Communication
- Safeguarding
- Privacy
- Infrastructure

must trigger an impact review and the appropriate testing/reapproval path.

## 13. Achievable implementation order

The practical sequence after the prototype is:

1. Freeze and document the pilot scope.
2. Execute controlled teacher/user testing.
3. Resolve critical usability and functional defects.
4. Validate official curriculum sources and mappings.
5. Obtain professional legal/privacy/safeguarding/security/accessibility input.
6. Build the minimum secure production backend.
7. Implement production authentication and server-side authorization.
8. Implement production database, audit and relationship controls.
9. Implement production hosting, backups, monitoring and recovery.
10. Implement verified payment operations only when needed for launch.
11. Conduct security, privacy, safeguarding, accessibility and device testing.
12. Complete independent/specialist review.
13. Assemble the release packet.
14. Obtain explicit owner approval.
15. Perform a controlled production launch.
16. Monitor, support, measure and improve.

## 14. Success measures

The project should measure:

- Learning completion and meaningful learning outcomes
- Teacher task time saved
- Parent/guardian usability
- Accessibility success
- Low-bandwidth/offline reliability
- Critical defect rate
- Security incidents
- Safeguarding incidents and response time
- Support response time
- Curriculum mapping accuracy
- Payment/reconciliation accuracy
- Service availability
- Cost per active user
- Sustainable operating margin
- User retention and satisfaction

These are operational measures, not guarantees of educational outcomes.

## 15. Final conclusion

The project is now structurally better aligned for a professional path, but **the repository alone cannot make Pacific Education legally compliant, secure, Ministry-approved or production-ready**.

The correct target is a controlled, evidence-driven transition:

**Prototype → Controlled Pilot → Verified Production Implementation → Independent/Professional Review → Owner Approval → Controlled Launch → Continuous Governance**

Production remains fail-closed until that evidence exists.
