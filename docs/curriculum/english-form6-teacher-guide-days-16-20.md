# Form 6 English — Week 4 Teacher Marking Guide (Days 16–20)

**Status:** PENDING_TEACHER_REVIEW and PENDING_OFFICIAL_INDICATOR_VERIFICATION. Original practice sources only; not prescribed texts or examination materials.

## Common marking principles
Assess comparative reasoning, evidence precision, method awareness, source limits and sustained cohesion. Accept accessible oral, signed, AAC, dictated, Braille or written responses. Do not score accent, disability-related communication differences, handwriting speed or device access as errors. Record response mode, independence and prompts. Keep PENDING_TEACHER_REVIEW. Pathways are flexible scaffolds.

## Day 16 — Oral communication
- Committee notice establishes the formal meeting and written-comment route, but only one time/location and a seven-day window.
- Residents' message identifies possible barriers for shift workers; it represents a small group, not all households.
- Credit practical alternatives: additional meeting time, telephone/online/written options, extended deadline, accessible summary or targeted outreach.
- Next step: distinguish a formal opportunity from equitable access to participate.

## Day 17 — Close reading
- Official report uses institutional, compressed language and reports completion and selection.
- Personal account uses first person and a specific example to question whether an individual comment influenced the outcome.
- It does not prove the comment was ignored. A response log, draft comparisons or committee records could establish how it was considered.
- Next step: separate textual evidence, inference and proof of intent.

## Day 18 — Vocabulary and language
- “Comprehensive” implies breadth; “community ownership” implies influence and shared control.
- “Logged” confirms that comments were recorded, not that they were considered or adopted.
- A cautious rewrite: “The project recorded 46 written comments and held three meetings. The available note does not establish the range of groups represented or how feedback influenced the final design.”
- Next step: match strength of wording to strength of evidence.

## Day 19 — Critical literacy
- The 62% figure applies to 150 respondents. Without invitation numbers, sampling method and response rate, it cannot be assumed to represent all residents.
- Access group note contributes examples of barriers but does not quantify their prevalence.
- Further methods: accessible paper/telephone options, broader outreach, representative sampling, published invitation/response rates and follow-up interviews with groups less likely to respond online.
- Next step: distinguish sample, respondents, target population and representativeness.

## Day 20 — Writing and shaping
- Final statement records 150 online responses and three meetings and identifies safety, cost and access as decision criteria; it does not publish a response log.
- Community submission requests an accessible log and another opportunity for non-digital users, while acknowledging cost and engineering constraints.
- A strong essay argues from both sources, recognises genuine constraints and explains why a transparent response log is still necessary.
- Next step: revise thesis alignment, source integration, fair counterargument and evidence-based conclusion.

## Pathway evidence
- Copper: selects one accurate detail per source with labels, model and recorded prompts.
- Bronze: organises a comparison with a frame and reduces prompts.
- Silver: independently integrates evidence and evaluates limitations.
- Gold: synthesises perspectives and develops a nuanced, qualified judgement.
