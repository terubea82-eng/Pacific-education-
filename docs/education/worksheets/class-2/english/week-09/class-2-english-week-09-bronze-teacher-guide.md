# TEACHER-ONLY GUIDE — Class 2 English Week 9 (Bronze)

**Days 41–45 | Retelling and Sequence | Do not distribute as the learner worksheet**

## Intended learning evidence
Learner identifies beginning/middle/ending, sequences events, uses temporal connectives, explains a simple action/result relationship and retells the narrative with relevant details.

## Text used
**A Seed for the Garden:** On Monday, Sera found a bean seed in the garden shed. She filled a pot with soft soil and made a small hole. First, she placed the seed in the hole. Next, she covered it with soil and watered it. She put the pot where it could receive sunlight. A few days later, a green shoot pushed through the soil. Sera watered the plant when the soil felt dry. Finally, two leaves opened and the plant began to grow.

## Indicative answers and teaching notes
- **Day 41:** Beginning — Sera/Mila starts planting the seed; middle — seed is covered/watered and cared for; ending — shoot/leaves appear and learner shares or records the growth. Accept accurate equivalent grouping based on the passage.
- **Day 42:** A defensible order is seed placed in soil → plant cared for/watered → shoot appears → leaves open. If the task's event labels are interpreted differently, accept only an order supported by the actual passage and ask learner to point to the evidence.
- **Day 43:** Accept a coherent retell using temporal words in a sensible order. Do not require the exact wording of the passage.
- **Day 44:** Examples: watering and sunlight help the seed/plant grow; checking the soil helps Sera decide when to water; the seed is covered and later a shoot appears. Credit a plausible relationship grounded in the text; distinguish stated facts from outside knowledge.
- **Day 45:** Look for the main planting, care and growth events in order plus at least two relevant details. Accept oral, signed, AAC, picture-supported or written retells.

## Pathway support — adjustable, not a fixed ability label
Use the word bank and event prompts. Give a short phrase or sentence for each part; a teacher may reread one section on request.

## Accessible assessment and marking
Credit meaning, correct order and valid equivalent wording. Spelling or handwriting should not reduce the English comprehension mark unless that is the specific skill being assessed. Allow speech, sign, pointing, drawing with labels or AAC. Use follow-up prompts consistently and record their level. No single weekly score establishes permanent pathway placement.
