# PacEdu Fiji — All-Level Subject and Concept Activity Blueprint

**Status:** planning blueprint / original practice-content specification  
**Levels:** ECE, Classes 1–8, Forms 3–7  
**Curriculum verification:** pending official source and educator review for each subject/level/outcome  
**Purpose:** drive subject-box content: Daily Activities, Worksheets, Revision, and Examination Practice.

## Non-negotiable content rules

1. Preserve the Fiji level labels **ECE**, **Class 1–Class 8**, and **Form 3–Form 7**.
2. Do not claim full Fiji Ministry curriculum alignment until the current authorized syllabus, exact outcome/achievement indicator, page/section, version, URL, reviewer, and review date are recorded.
3. Subject availability differs by level, school, language pathway, and senior-secondary option. The catalogue below is a coverage checklist, not a claim that every school teaches every subject.
4. Every activity must have: level, subject, concept, learning intention, success check, short explanation, worked example, student tasks, four flexible ability versions, answer guidance/marking criteria, accessible response alternatives, and a printable worksheet.
5. Day 1 is an initial diagnostic/observation, not a lesson grade. Use evidence to select support; do not permanently label learners.
6. Keep student worksheet and teacher answer key separate. Students see assigned/teacher-approved work only. Formal marks and high-stakes decisions require educator review.
7. Use original examples and local contexts (school, market, bus, village/settlement, farm, weather, water, community services) without assuming every learner has the same resources or home circumstances.
8. For languages, use vocabulary and texts checked by a competent teacher/fluent speaker. Do not invent local-language forms.
9. For practical, health, science, PE, agriculture, technology, and food activities, include age-appropriate safety instructions and safe alternatives.
10. Current affairs may be used only when a credible, current source is available and the item is relevant to the selected country/level. Otherwise use curriculum-first everyday-life contexts.

## Subject-box experience

Each subject box opens the same four sections:

- **Daily Activities** — date, term/block, week, day, concept and assigned activity.
- **Worksheets** — printable/downloadable student page and separate teacher guide.
- **Revision** — practice grouped by concepts already taught, with feedback and retry.
- **Examination Practice** — original mock/practice papers, marking criteria and teacher-only answer keys. Do not label generated practice as an official past paper.

Every section is filtered by class/form, subject, term, concept, and flexible ability band. The teacher may preview and approve work. The student view remains focused on the activity, response area, accessibility controls, and submit/save status.

## Four flexible ability versions

- **Supported start:** concrete objects/pictures, short steps, worked example, fewer choices, guided first item.
- **Developing:** one hint, familiar context, partial worked example, independent attempt after support.
- **Class-goal practice:** class-appropriate independent tasks and explanation of thinking.
- **Extension:** transfer to a new context, compare methods, justify an answer, or solve a more complex problem.

The labels describe current task support, not a fixed ability or identity. A learner can use different bands in different concepts.

## Concept-to-activity pattern by subject family

Every row below is an original activity seed, not an official syllabus indicator. Expand each verified concept into sequenced daily tasks, worksheets, revision, and practice papers for every applicable class/form.

### English
**Concept sequence:** listening and speaking; phonological awareness/phonics where developmentally appropriate; vocabulary; reading fluency; comprehension; grammar and sentence construction; paragraph/text structure; writing for purpose and audience; oral presentation; evidence and critical reading at higher levels.

**Worked activity seed — community notice**
- Example: “The school clean-up starts at 9:00 am on Friday. Bring a reusable water bottle.”
- Supported: match the notice to a clean-up picture; identify Friday.
- Developing: answer who/what/when questions using the notice.
- Class-goal: write two complete answers and one sentence about preparation.
- Extension: rewrite the notice for a different audience and explain what information must remain.
- Marking: 1 mark per accurate retrieval answer; writing rubric checks meaning, relevant detail, sentence clarity, and level-appropriate conventions.

### Mathematics
**Concept sequence:** number sense; counting/place value; operations; patterns; fractions/decimals/percentages; measurement; time/money; shape/space; data/statistics; probability; ratio/rate; algebra; graphs; financial and practical problem-solving.

**Worked activity seed — school market**
- Example: 3 bundles cost $2 each. Total = 3 × $2 = $6.
- Supported: use three $2 price cards and count $2, $4, $6.
- Developing: draw three groups of $2 and write the total.
- Class-goal: solve a two-item shopping problem and show the calculation.
- Extension: compare two price offers and justify the cheaper option, including units.
- Marking: award method marks for a correct representation and answer; accept an equivalent valid strategy.

### Science / Basic Science / Biology / Chemistry / Physics (as applicable)
**Concept sequence:** observation and questions; living things; materials; forces and energy; Earth/weather/environment; scientific equipment and safety; fair testing; recording results; interpreting evidence; cells/ecosystems/body systems; matter/reactions; motion/electricity/waves; experimental evaluation at higher levels.

**Worked activity seed — plant growth**
- Example: compare two similar plants with the same water but different light exposure.
- Supported: point to or describe one visible change.
- Developing: record one observation each day using words or pictures.
- Class-goal: identify the changed condition and write a conclusion from results.
- Extension: identify limits and propose a repeatable fair-test improvement.
- Marking: separate observation from inference; credit conclusions supported by recorded evidence.

### Social Studies / History / Geography / Civics (as applicable)
**Concept sequence:** self/family/community; places and maps; shared rules and responsibilities; local services; environment and resources; culture and change; timelines and evidence; population and settlement; migration/trade; governance/citizenship; historical sources; spatial data and competing viewpoints.

**Worked activity seed — safe shared space**
- Example: a school path is blocked by litter after heavy rain.
- Supported: identify the problem in a picture.
- Developing: name who is affected and one safe action.
- Class-goal: compare two solutions and identify a likely benefit.
- Extension: recommend a response using stakeholder views, costs, fairness and evidence.
- Marking: accept reasoned alternatives; assess evidence, relevance, and respectful treatment of viewpoints.

### Healthy Living / Health Education
**Concept sequence:** hygiene; safe water and food; nutrition; rest and activity; feelings and help-seeking; relationships and respect; personal safety; disease prevention; risk and protective factors; health information literacy and community wellbeing, following the applicable syllabus and safeguarding rules.

**Worked activity seed — handwashing**
- Example: wet hands, apply soap, rub all surfaces, rinse, dry safely.
- Supported: sequence picture cards.
- Developing: explain when to wash hands.
- Class-goal: identify missing steps in a routine and correct them.
- Extension: plan an accessible school reminder and evaluate whether it helps.
- Marking: sequence accuracy, safety, and explanation. Sensitive health topics must be age-appropriate and educator-reviewed.

### Physical Education
**Concept sequence:** body awareness and safe movement; locomotor/non-locomotor skills; balance and coordination; warm-up/cool-down; games and rules; teamwork/fair play; fitness and goal-setting; movement analysis; inclusive adaptations and safety planning.

**Worked activity seed — movement stations**
- Example: walk between markers, balance briefly, then pass a soft ball.
- Supported: demonstrate or choose one safe movement.
- Developing: follow a three-step sequence.
- Class-goal: perform the sequence safely and self-check one skill.
- Extension: adapt the station so different learners can participate and explain why.
- Marking: safety, participation, process and reflection; do not grade learners solely on disability-related physical capacity.

### Arts and Crafts / Visual Arts / Design
**Concept sequence:** line, shape, colour, texture, pattern; observation and representation; planning; safe materials and tools; making and revising; visual communication; design criteria; audience and reflection; sustainability and material choice.

**Worked activity seed — rain-safe school sign**
- Example: design a clear “Keep the path clear” sign using a symbol and short text.
- Supported: choose a suitable symbol from examples.
- Developing: draw a symbol and add a short message.
- Class-goal: design, test readability, and revise the sign.
- Extension: compare two designs for visibility, accessibility and durability.
- Marking: communication, relevance, safe process, and reflection, not one “correct” artistic style.

### Music
**Concept sequence:** sound and silence; beat and rhythm; pitch and dynamics; pattern; listening; singing/playing with safe volume; notation where appropriate; composition; performance/reflection; cultural context using verified, respectful resources.

**Worked activity seed — repeating rhythm**
- Example: clap–clap–tap, clap–clap–tap.
- Supported: copy a short pattern by tapping, moving, or indicating.
- Developing: identify which part repeats.
- Class-goal: create a four-part pattern and perform or represent it.
- Extension: change tempo or dynamics and explain the effect.
- Marking: pattern, intentional choices and reflection; provide non-audio alternatives.

### iTaukei / Hindi / other language pathways
**Concept sequence:** listening; speaking/interaction; vocabulary; sound/script recognition where appropriate; reading; comprehension; grammar; writing; text types; culture and language variation using verified local resources.

**Worked activity seed — greeting and response**
- Use only expressions verified by a competent teacher or fluent speaker.
- Supported: match a verified greeting to a context.
- Developing: select an appropriate response from checked examples.
- Class-goal: create a short dialogue using approved vocabulary.
- Extension: compare register or audience using a verified source.
- Marking: meaning and level-appropriate accuracy; record source/reviewer for language examples.

### Digital Literacy / Computer Studies / ICT
**Concept sequence:** devices and input/output; file handling; keyboarding; information search; source checks; privacy; passwords and account safety; online communication; data representation; algorithms; coding concepts; spreadsheet/document/presentation tools; digital ethics and security.

**Worked activity seed — checking a message**
- Example: a message asks a learner to share a password to receive a prize.
- Supported: choose “share” or “do not share” and identify a trusted adult.
- Developing: identify the unsafe request and a safe next step.
- Class-goal: list two clues that make a message suspicious.
- Extension: write a verification checklist and explain why each step helps.
- Marking: safe decisions and reasons. Never request real passwords or sensitive student data in worksheets.

### Agriculture / Agricultural Science
**Concept sequence:** plant needs; soil and water; seed selection; planting and care; tools and safety; animal care where applicable; pests and disease; compost and soil conservation; weather and records; sustainable production; costs/yields and evidence-based decisions at higher levels.

**Worked activity seed — seedling record**
- Example: record height and number of leaves for a seedling over five days.
- Supported: match pictures showing a seedling's stages.
- Developing: record one daily observation.
- Class-goal: create a table and describe the change.
- Extension: compare growth rates and discuss what the evidence cannot prove.
- Marking: accurate observations, labels/units and evidence-based conclusion.

### Commercial Studies / Business / Commerce / Accounting / Economics
**Concept sequence:** needs and wants; goods/services; consumers and producers; saving/spending; simple budgets; records and transactions; costs/revenue/profit; markets and prices; business communication; accounting records; demand/supply and economic choices, according to the verified subject syllabus.

**Worked activity seed — class stall budget**
- Example: spend $8 on supplies and sell goods for $12. Difference before other costs = $4.
- Supported: match price cards and count the total.
- Developing: calculate sales minus supplies.
- Class-goal: make a simple income/expense table and calculate the balance.
- Extension: include another cost and explain why the first $4 is not necessarily final profit.
- Marking: calculations, labels, and assumptions.

### Office Technology / Technical / Basic Technology / Graphics / Vocational options
**Concept sequence:** safe tool use; instructions and diagrams; measurement; materials; planning; prototypes; digital/office documents; data entry; layout and visual communication; testing; quality criteria; troubleshooting; workflow and occupational safety.

**Worked activity seed — organise a class notice**
- Example: make a one-page notice with a title, date, venue and clear instructions.
- Supported: place labelled fields in the correct order.
- Developing: complete a template with given information.
- Class-goal: create a readable notice and check each required field.
- Extension: justify layout choices for print and phone display.
- Marking: required information, accuracy, readability, safe and appropriate tool use.

### Home Economics / Food and Nutrition
**Concept sequence:** personal and food hygiene; safe handling; food groups/nutrition; meal planning; measurement; budgeting; textiles/repair where applicable; household safety; resource use; evaluation of choices under the current syllabus.

**Worked activity seed — safe snack plan**
- Example: plan a snack using available foods, safe drinking water, and clean hands.
- Supported: identify a clean utensil and safe water picture.
- Developing: put safe preparation steps in order.
- Class-goal: plan a balanced snack within a stated budget.
- Extension: compare cost, nutrition information and food-safety constraints.
- Marking: hygiene, reasoning, measurements and budget accuracy. Do not require learners to bring food or money.

### Religious / Values / Family Life Education (only where offered and approved)
**Concept sequence:** follow the school's authorised programme; respectful dialogue, values, relationships, responsibility, personal safety, and reflection appropriate to age and community. Do not assume a single faith or make sensitive personal disclosure compulsory.

**Worked activity seed — respectful disagreement**
- Example: two classmates prefer different group activities.
- Supported: identify a respectful response from options.
- Developing: describe one way to listen.
- Class-goal: role-play a fair decision-making process.
- Extension: compare approaches for fairness, inclusion and consequences.
- Marking: respectful reasoning and application of the approved programme; never score personal belief or disclosure.

## Stage progression

Use the same concept family only when appropriate, increasing the thinking demand rather than merely increasing the amount of writing.

- **ECE:** play, concrete objects, movement, picture choice, talk, drawing and observation.
- **Classes 1–2:** familiar words, counting/manipulatives, short instructions, pictures and one-step explanations.
- **Classes 3–4:** short passages, multi-step tasks, tables, labelled drawings, and simple reasons.
- **Classes 5–6:** explain strategies, compare examples, interpret simple data, and write connected responses.
- **Classes 7–8:** justify choices, transfer concepts to new contexts, interpret evidence and solve multi-step problems.
- **Forms 3–4:** apply subject vocabulary and methods, analyse cases/data, explain cause/effect and show working.
- **Forms 5–7:** use subject-specific terminology, sustained reasoning, data/source evaluation, independent investigation, and examination-style response structures appropriate to the selected subject.

These are instructional design guides, not official achievement standards.

## Daily activity template

For each verified level + subject + concept:

1. **Metadata:** country, level, subject, concept, term/block, week, day, content version, author/reviewer, source and mapping status.
2. **Learning intention:** one observable skill.
3. **Success check:** evidence that shows the learner has practised the skill.
4. **Recall:** one short question linked to previously taught work.
5. **Explain:** short learner-friendly explanation.
6. **Worked example:** solve/show one task step by step.
7. **Guided practice:** one task with a hint.
8. **Independent practice:** 3–5 appropriate questions/tasks.
9. **Four versions:** supported, developing, class-goal, extension.
10. **Feedback:** correct answer or criteria, explanation, and one retry/next step.
11. **Teacher guide:** answer key, marking rationale, common misconception, safety/accessibility note.
12. **Evidence:** completion and learner work; do not make automatic marks final for high-stakes assessment.
13. **Printable worksheet:** student page separated from teacher-only guide.
14. **Linking:** tag each item to the concept so revision and mock exams can reuse the skill without simply repeating the exact same question.

## Revision and examination generation rules

- Build revision only from concepts that have actual daily activities and teacher-approved coverage.
- Use a balanced mix of multiple choice, matching, true/false with correction, short answer, reading/data interpretation, practical/performance evidence, and extended responses where suitable.
- Publish marking criteria and sample answers for teacher review. Accept valid alternative methods where appropriate.
- Avoid claiming an exam paper is official or predictive. Generated papers are original PacEdu practice papers unless a rights-cleared official source is explicitly recorded.
- Include accommodations and equivalent ways to show learning where appropriate.
- Store exam scope, duration and weighting only after the school/teacher confirms them; never assume one national timetable or school-specific option.
- Maintain separate student and teacher views. Never expose answer keys before the configured assessment is completed/released.

## Coverage and release gates

A level/subject is **content-present** only when subject-specific activities exist. It is **curriculum-mapped** only when exact current official outcomes are verified and recorded. It is **educator-reviewed** only when a qualified reviewer and date are recorded. It is **pilot-ready** only after functional tests and representative teacher/device review.

Required per level/subject:
- [ ] Confirm subject is applicable to that level and school programme.
- [ ] Record current official syllabus/source version and exact outcome references.
- [ ] Build concept inventory and sequence.
- [ ] Draft daily activities with examples and four flexible ability versions.
- [ ] Create linked worksheets and separate teacher guides.
- [ ] Create revision and practice exam coverage only for taught concepts.
- [ ] Add answer-key, privacy, accessibility, safety, offline and regression tests.
- [ ] Educator reviews language, level fit, cultural appropriateness and answer validity.
- [ ] Record review date and remaining gaps.

**Current truth:** this blueprint is not evidence that all content is already integrated into the live app. Existing multi-subject generators are a starting point; a complete subject-specific bank for every class/form and every current official concept remains work to implement and verify. Do not mark all coverage complete from this document alone.
