# Class 3 English — four ability worksheets

Status: original pilot worksheets; verify and record the current Fiji Class/Year 3 English syllabus outcome before classroom release. Ability bands are flexible, subject-specific guides: Support 0–49%, Developing 50–64%, Expected 65–79%, Advanced 80–100%.

## A. Support (0–49%) — Find details and write sentences

**Example:** “Tui has a pencil.” Who has a pencil? Tui.

Read: “Pita went to the library with his cousin. He borrowed a book about fish. At home, he read the book to his younger sister.”

1. Where did Pita go?
2. Who went with Pita?
3. What was the book about?
4. Who listened to the story?
5. Choose the correct word: Pita (borrowed / cooked) a book.
6. Write one sentence about a book you like.

**Teacher key (10 marks):** library (1); cousin (1); fish (1); younger sister (1); borrowed (1); relevant sentence with capital and full stop (up to 5).

## B. Developing (50–64%) — Sequence and vocabulary

**Example:** First means it happened before the next event.

Read: “Heavy rain fell overnight. In the morning, the school path was muddy. The class monitor placed a warning sign near the slippery part and told the teacher. The teacher arranged a safer path.”

1. What happened overnight?
2. Why was the path muddy?
3. What did the monitor do first?
4. What did the teacher arrange?
5. Put in order: teacher arranges a safe path / rain falls / monitor reports the path.
6. Write three sentences explaining how to stay safe on a wet path.

**Teacher key (10 marks):** rain fell (1); rain made the path muddy (1); placed a sign/reported it (2); safer path (1); rain → monitor acts/reports → teacher arranges path (2); relevant safety paragraph (3).

## C. Expected (65–79%) — Infer and summarise

Read: “Mila wanted to grow vegetables beside her home. The soil was dry, so she added compost and watered it. She planted bean seeds and marked the rows with sticks. Each afternoon, she checked the soil. When several days passed without rain, she asked her family to help save water for the garden.”

1. What problem did Mila notice?
2. How did compost and water help her prepare?
3. Why did she mark the rows?
4. What can you infer about Mila? Give a clue from the passage.
5. Summarise the passage in two sentences.
6. Suggest one question you would ask Mila about her garden.

**Teacher key (12 marks):** dry soil (1); prepared soil for planting (2); identify rows/seed places (2); reasonable inference supported by clue (2); accurate concise summary (3); relevant question (2).

## D. Advanced (80–100%) — Compare evidence and persuade

Read: “Two classes tried to save water. Class A put up a reminder sign. Class B put up a sign, checked taps at lunchtime, and recorded water-meter readings each Friday. After four weeks, Class B recorded lower water use. Neither class recorded a starting reading before the project.”

1. Name two differences between the approaches.
2. What evidence suggests Class B's approach may have helped?
3. Why is it difficult to prove the project caused the change?
4. Suggest a fairer way to compare the classes.
5. Write a persuasive paragraph (6–8 sentences) proposing one way your school could save water. Include a claim, reasons, evidence you would collect, and a respectful call to action.

**Teacher key (12 marks):** two valid differences (2); lower recorded use (2); no baseline/other causes possible (2); same baseline, period and recording method (2); paragraph: claim (1), reasons/evidence (2), clear respectful action and structure (1).
