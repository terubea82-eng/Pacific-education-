# Form 5 English — Week 1 Teacher Marking Guide (Days 1–5)

**Status:** PENDING_TEACHER_REVIEW and PENDING_OFFICIAL_INDICATOR_VERIFICATION. PacEdu working guide only. The original practice stimuli are not prescribed texts or examination materials.

## Common assessment principles
- Assess thesis quality, close analysis, relevance of evidence, coherence, synthesis, qualification and audience awareness.
- Accept defensible alternative readings and equivalent wording. Do not score accent, disability-related speech differences, handwriting speed or device access as errors.
- Accept accessible oral, signed, AAC, dictated, Braille or written evidence where it demonstrates the same objective.
- Record response mode, independence and prompting. Keep results PENDING_TEACHER_REVIEW. Copper–Gold are flexible scaffolds, not permanent labels.

## Day 1 — Oral communication
**Expected:** defensible thesis, analysis of two details, coherent reasoning, qualification and response to a counterargument.
- Possible thesis: institutions should recognise everyday languages, provided communication is accurate and accessible.
- Evidence: home expressions carry humour, memory and relationships; formal phrasing may be expected in wider institutions; register depends on audience, purpose and power.
- Counterargument may address consistency, shared understanding or institutional resources. A strong response weighs these concerns rather than dismissing them.
- Next step: develop a thesis and support it with evidence/explanation before responding to a challenge.

## Day 2 — Close reading
**Expected:** thesis about the narrator's changing attitude, precise evidence and analysis of the ending.
- The dictionary shifts from a protected record of disappearing words to an active part of intergenerational conversation.
- Evidence: it is kept on a high shelf and rarely opened; the cousin borrows it; the word is spoken, mispronounced, corrected gently and shared.
- The ending suggests preservation requires use and exchange, not storage alone.
- Accept other readings when supported by precise detail and explanation.
- Next step: link a language choice or structural shift to a developed interpretation.

## Day 3 — Vocabulary and language
**Expected:** analysis of metaphor, rhetorical urgency, audience positioning and consent-aware revision.
- “Not a museum piece” contrasts static display with living practice.
- “Every voice must be heard” creates urgency and inclusion but may imply pressure or compulsory participation.
- Missing details include consent, storage duration, access and control of recordings.
- A good revision preserves invitation while making participation voluntary and consent explicit.
- Next step: assess both persuasive effect and ethical implication of wording.

## Day 4 — Critical literacy
**Expected:** source comparison, evaluation of evidence, ethical reasoning and qualified conclusion.
- Source A is more accountable: it is issued by a named committee and describes voluntary recording and contributor approval.
- Source A omits how long recordings will be stored; minutes confirm storage arrangements remain under discussion.
- Source B is anonymous and supplies no evidence for its claims. The minutes do not confirm any sale of recordings.
- A careful conclusion separates confirmed information, unresolved questions and unsupported claims.
- Next step: use source, claim, evidence, omission, limit and ethical impact as an evaluation frame.

## Day 5 — Writing and shaping
**Expected:** analytical essay with thesis, close analysis, synthesis of benefits/concerns, qualification and reasoned conclusion.
- Possible thesis: preservation has value only when it is paired with contributor consent and control.
- Benefits: intergenerational learning and visibility for overlooked experiences.
- Concerns: privacy, ownership, consent, access and context loss.
- Safeguards: clear permission, contributor review, access rules and withdrawal process.
- Accept alternative theses if the essay analyses evidence and reaches a reasoned judgement.
- Next step: plan a thesis-led paragraph sequence; ensure each paragraph advances the argument.

## Pathway support
- Copper: supported thesis/evidence frame and explicit modelling.
- Bronze: paragraph plan and evidence locator with reduced prompts.
- Silver: independent close analysis and coherent reasoning.
- Gold: synthesis, qualification, ambiguity or counterargument.
