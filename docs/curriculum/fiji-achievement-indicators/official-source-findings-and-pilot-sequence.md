### Source B — Year 6 and Year 8 Teachers Guide, Term 3 (2015): disaster-awareness content

The same guide includes a Red Cross / Girl Guides / Boys Scouts section with a 25-lesson progression. It provides achievement statements without the same coded PE indicator format. Keep these as cross-curricular safety-learning statements, not as a PE indicator or a substitute for a subject syllabus:

- Identify and discuss natural and human-made disasters that affect Fiji.
- Determine the type of disaster and state and practise relevant precautions.
- Explain safe procedures during a disaster and actions to take afterwards.
- Formulate and explain what people should do during and after disasters.

The guide sequences the 25 lessons in five blocks: lessons 1–5 discuss disaster types and effects; 6–10 collect and organise pictures; 11–15 practise precautions; 16–20 discuss safe actions during and after a disaster; 21–25 create posters, poems or essays and role-play preparedness. This is the guide's sequence, not a Ministry-prescribed 115-day distribution.

## 8. Ministry disability-inclusive assessment guidance — design requirements, not curriculum indicators (12 November 2026)

### Source J — A Teacher's Guide to Disability-Inclusive Education in Fiji (Ministry of Education, 2023)

- Official Ministry-hosted URL: https://www.education.gov.fj/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/SIE-guide-high-res-web-version-1.pdf
- Citation in the guide: Sprunt et al. (2023), *A Teacher's Guide to Disability-Inclusive Education in Fiji*, Ministry of Education.
- Relevant printed pages: 54–60, especially section 7.1 and 7.4.
- The guide says assessment tools and strategies should be flexible for students with disabilities. It gives examples such as written assignments, posters and class presentations, and says summative assessments should provide reasonable accommodations.
- It distinguishes assessment for learning, as learning and of learning; it recommends using varied evidence such as observation, checklists, anecdotal records, informal quizzes, interviews/conversations, self-assessment, work samples, and oral/written responses.
- For Deaf or hard-of-hearing learners, examples include extra time, interpreter support where appropriate, and alternative response modes such as drawing, oral presentation or signing when the assessed construct is subject knowledge rather than writing/reading comprehension.
- For blind or low-vision learners, examples include Braille/tactile paper, accessible digital copies, screen-reader software, extra time and large print. Graphs/tables should not be assessed in inaccessible formats if learners have not been taught to access them; an alternative question may assess the same understanding.
- The guide says adjustments must be individualised and discussed with the child, caregiver and teacher before being finalised. These are guidance examples, not blanket automatic permissions for voice input or a substitute for teacher authorisation.

### PacEdu implementation rule derived from Source J

1. Keep the official curriculum outcome and assessed learning construct unchanged when providing an accommodation, unless a teacher-authorised modification is documented.
2. Store the student's approved response mode/accommodation separately from the indicator record (for example: keyboard, speech-to-text, signed response with interpreter, Braille/tactile, screen reader, drawing, oral response).
3. Do not treat speech-to-text or a scribe as automatically appropriate for literacy/writing outcomes; the accommodation must not supply the skill being assessed.
4. Make audio, speech, screen-reader, tactile and alternative response options available only in ways consistent with the learner's documented needs and teacher approval.
5. Keep evidence of the response mode, accommodation, teacher approval and any human review with the assessment record.
6. These inclusion requirements may shape worksheet presentation and evidence collection across ECE–Year 13, but they do not create or replace curriculum indicators.

### Copyright and reuse checkpoint

The Ministry's Policy on Free Educational Resources (approved 1 December 2015) states that school reproduction of Ministry free resources requires Permanent Secretary approval for teaching and learning, and that the resources remain Ministry property under school custody. This is a caution against copying whole guides, textbook pages, illustrations or large blocks of source content into PacEdu. Before commercial or public digital distribution, obtain written Ministry permission or use independently authored activities aligned to properly cited outcomes, with legal review of the applicable copyright terms. The policy itself is a historical 2015 policy; confirm whether it has been superseded before relying on it as the current legal position.

## 11. Clarification: use Fiji Educational Prescriptions as the primary concept source (12 November 2026)

The project owner clarified that the concepts to be identified and distributed are written in the Fiji Ministry of Education's **Educational Prescriptions** published on the Ministry website. This clarification governs the next extraction phase and supersedes treating the limited teacher-guide examples in this register as the starting point for the whole curriculum.

### Source and planning interpretation

- The Ministry's School Standards Monitoring and Inspection Policy asks whether current prescriptions are available, accessible and used by teachers, and whether each school's Scheme of Work (SOW) is aligned to the prescription. This confirms that prescriptions are a primary classroom planning reference. Official URL: https://www.education.gov.fj/wp-content/uploads/2022/11/Schools_Standard_Monitoring_and_Inspection_Policy-_Reviewed_2014.pdf
- The Ministry's *Na Noda Mataniciva* guideline explicitly explains that teachers unpack detailed sub-strand outcomes to identify the concepts, skills and attitudes; these become the focus of teaching and planning. Official URL: https://www.education.gov.fj/wp-content/uploads/2022/06/Na-Noda-Mataniciva.pdf
- Therefore, PacEdu's extraction model must capture the concepts stated in each current Educational Prescription and unpack them into teachable/assessable learning indicators, while retaining a traceable link to the exact prescription outcome and source location. The indicators are not to be invented independently or substituted with broad NCF outcomes.

### Required extraction fields for every prescription entry

1. Fiji official level/year and subject exactly as written.
2. Educational Prescription document title, version/edition, issuing authority and effective year/date.
3. Strand/sub-strand/topic or unit exactly as named in the prescription.
4. Exact prescribed concept / outcome / learning expectation (verbatim only where legally permitted; otherwise a concise faithful paraphrase).
5. Source page, section, table or outcome code.
6. Unpacked concept(s), knowledge, skill(s) and attitude(s), clearly distinguished from source wording.
7. PacEdu learning indicator written as observable learner performance and explicitly linked to the source concept/outcome.
8. Prerequisite / progression relationship where supported by the prescription.
9. Suggested evidence and assessment method that genuinely measures the stated concept.
10. Proposed term/week/day placement in the 45 + 45 + 25 day calendar, with teacher-review status.
11. Version/effective-status check and reviewer approval.

### Distribution rule

## 15. Protected global curriculum-first gate — owner instruction (12 November 2026)

**Mandatory rule for every PacEdu jurisdiction and institution:** PacEdu must first identify the prescribed concepts or learning indicators from the educational curriculum/prescription mandated or formally authorised for that learner's country, jurisdiction, level, subject and programme. Only then may it create the daily activities and worksheets that teach those concepts. This applies to all countries and all recognised educational institutions using PacEdu; Fiji is the first implementation case, not the global limit.

### Which authority controls the curriculum?

- Follow the applicable national or subnational Ministry/regulator curriculum where it is legally mandated for the school or learner.
- For an examination or qualification programme, follow the authorised examination/qualification body's current prescription where that programme governs the subject and level.
- For an institution-specific programme, use that institution's formally approved curriculum only where it has authority to set that programme; it must still comply with applicable national law, regulator requirements and accreditation conditions.
- Where requirements conflict, record the conflict and obtain a written ruling from the responsible regulator/authority. Do not let PacEdu choose whichever syllabus is easiest.
- A textbook, commercial course, third-party website, teacher-created scheme or AI-generated topic is not a mandated prescription merely because it is educational. It may support teaching but cannot replace the controlling curriculum.

### Locked creation sequence

1. Identify the learner's jurisdiction/country, institution or programme, official level/year, subject and applicable school/academic year.
2. Locate the controlling educational prescription and verify issuing authority, document title, edition/version, effective date/status and source page/section.
3. Extract the prescribed concepts/outcomes/indicators first, preserving source IDs and progression. Where a source uses concepts rather than coded indicators, retain the concept and create a clearly labelled PacEdu unpacked indicator linked to it.
