# PacEdu Fiji ECE–Form 7: Daily Activities, Revision and Examination Master Plan

**Status:** Proposal-branch curriculum-content pack; not merged, not live in the app, and not a substitute for Ministry-approved schemes of work. Exact formal curriculum alignment remains `outcome-mapping-pending` until a reviewer records the adopted syllabus/framework version, official outcome/indicator wording and ID, page/section, school adoption status, reviewer and review date.

**Purpose:** Put learning, practice, revision, checkpoints and examination practice inside the same class-and-subject box in the teacher workspace. Use the school calendar to supply actual dates; never invent dates or show a subject not confirmed by the school.

## 1. Verification findings and release rules

1. Fiji’s Higher Education Commission describes six Foundation Areas of Learning and Development for ECE and seven Key Learning Areas for primary/secondary schooling. This is framework-level information, not a complete current subject-by-class list: https://nic.hec.org.fj/school-education
2. The Ministry reported a comprehensive review of the 2013 National Curriculum Framework launched in 2026; its May 2026 statement said the reviewed framework would be submitted for Cabinet endorsement after consultation. Check the current Ministry-approved version before formal mapping: https://www.parliament.gov.fj/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/Daily-Hansard-Monday-25th-May-2026.pdf
3. The Ministry’s `Na Noda Mataniciva` ECE guidelines describe outcomes and local contextualisation rather than prescribing one fixed content list. ECE must remain play-based, developmentally appropriate and observation-led: https://www.education.gov.fj/wp-content/uploads/2022/06/Na-Noda-Mataniciva.pdf
4. The Ministry’s National Curriculum Assessment and Reporting policy requires valid and reliable assessment and a range of formal and informal evidence: https://www.education.gov.fj/wp-content/uploads/2022/11/POLICY-IN-NATIONAL-CURRICULUM-ASSESSMENT-AND-REPORTING.pdf
5. Historical textbook lists and third-party worksheets may help locate resources but cannot independently prove the current required subjects or achievement indicators.

**Release gate:** A lesson is not labelled “officially curriculum-aligned” until it has an official source URL, current version/adoption status, class/level, subject, exact outcome ID and wording, source page/section, reviewer and review date. Otherwise label it **PacEdu teacher-reviewed enrichment — outcome mapping pending**. Practice tests must say **PacEdu practice assessment — not an official examination paper** unless official public release and reuse permission are confirmed.

## 2. Workspace structure

Each class has one subject box per school-confirmed subject. Inside that box, show one chronological stream:

1. Today's dated activity and learning goal
2. Worked example or teacher demonstration
3. Student practice at the assigned ability pathway
4. Short exit check and evidence options
5. Next-day retrieval practice / corrections
6. Weekly checkpoint and targeted reteaching
7. End-of-unit revision
8. Term practice assessment, answer guide and marking rubric
9. Post-assessment feedback and next-step activity

Do not create separate global “daily activities”, “revision” and “exam papers” pages. Filters may be offered inside a subject box (day, week, revision, assessment, printable), but the default remains one ordered sequence. A date is generated from the school calendar and timetable. If either is missing, show “School calendar/timetable needs setup”; do not fabricate a date or subject.

## 3. Four ability pathways

Use these as temporary instructional supports, not permanent student labels. The teacher reviews evidence and can change a pathway for each skill.

- **Supported Start:** concrete objects, oral/visual instructions, one worked example, reduced number of items, guided practice, extra time and frequent checks.
- **Developing:** prompts and partial examples, small steps, mixed practice, sentence starters or visual organisers as appropriate.
- **Working at Class Goal:** independent practice on the intended class-level skill, explain the method, apply it in a new familiar context.
- **Extension:** multi-step reasoning, compare methods, justify answers, create a solution, investigate a local real-life problem and reflect on limitations.

Do not infer fixed percentage cut-offs without a school-approved assessment policy. A percentage is one piece of evidence, not the sole basis for grouping. Store the assessed skill, evidence, date and teacher-confirmed next step.

## 4. Level map and subject-box setup

The following is a **setup checklist**, not a claim that every listed subject is compulsory at every school. The school must confirm actual subject boxes against the currently adopted syllabus, timetable, language stream and examination programme.

| PacEdu level | Curriculum setup and expected activity style |
|---|---|
| ECE | Use the six ECE Foundation Areas as the framework check; play, movement, talk, stories, drawing, counting, nature exploration, relationships, routines and observation. Do not force formal written exams. |
| Year 1 | Build foundational oral language, early reading/writing, number sense, patterns, shapes, measurement, observation, family/community and wellbeing. Use the school-confirmed subjects and language streams. |
| Year 2 | Strengthen decoding, comprehension, sentence writing, number operations, measurement, patterns, observation, community and independent routines. |
| Year 3 | Transition toward broader Key Learning Area organisation; use school-approved subject labels and outcomes. Increase reading-to-learn, explanation and recording observations. |
| Year 4 | Build fluency, comprehension, written explanations, multi-step numeracy and subject vocabulary; add short independent tasks and cumulative review. |
| Year 5 | Prepare for age-appropriate LANA-related literacy/numeracy skills where applicable, without pretending PacEdu practice is the official assessment. Increase source reading, data interpretation and problem solving. |
| Year 6 | Consolidate primary concepts, longer responses, mathematical reasoning, investigations, revision plans and school-confirmed examination formats. |
| Year 7 | Practise transfer across topics, evidence-based explanations, structured writing, multi-step mathematics and subject-specific vocabulary. |
| Year 8 | Consolidate primary learning and prepare for school-confirmed Year 8 examination requirements; include timed practice only when appropriate. |
| Form 3 / Year 9 | Each subject box must use the school’s selected subject and current Year 9 syllabus. Establish baseline knowledge, subject terminology, practical skills and FY9FE-aligned practice only after official requirements are confirmed. |
| Form 4 / Year 10 | Use current Year 10 subject syllabus, prerequisite review, multi-step application, practical/report-writing skills where relevant and school-confirmed FY10E practice. |
| Form 5 / Year 11 | Use the exact selected subject syllabus and school programme; prioritise depth, independent study, data/source evaluation and exam-response technique. |
| Form 6 / Year 12 | Use the selected Year 12 certificate syllabus and official assessment structure; maintain subject-specific practical/coursework requirements and FY12CE preparation only when verified. |
| Form 7 / Year 13 | Use the selected Year 13 certificate syllabus, current official outcomes and assessment weighting; support deep revision, timed practice and FY13CE preparation without reproducing restricted papers. |

### Subject box selection rule

- ECE: configure the Foundation Areas from the school’s adopted ECE guidance; use ECE rather than renaming the level.
- Years 1–2: confirm the school’s adopted subject labels and language streams. Do not automatically display every subject found in an old textbook list.
- Years 3–8: load only subjects and outcomes confirmed in the current school programme.
- Forms 3–7: load the school’s actual selected subjects/electives and the correct syllabus version; do not assume every student takes the same subjects.
- Hide unconfirmed optional boxes. If a required source is missing, show a teacher-only “curriculum mapping needed” status rather than inventing an outcome.

## 5. Reusable term calendar sequence

The user-defined planning pattern is nine teaching weeks for Terms 1 and 2, five teaching weeks before the Term 3 examination period, followed by intervention/revision and examinations as set by the actual school calendar. This is a **PacEdu planning configuration**, not a verified national calendar for every school.

### Term 1 and Term 2 — nine teaching weeks per term

| Week | Daily sequence inside every confirmed subject box |
|---|---|
| 1 | Day 1: short diagnostic / prior-knowledge check. Day 2: teach concept A with a local example. Day 3: guided practice A. Day 4: apply concept A. Day 5: retrieval quiz, feedback and reteaching group. |
| 2 | Day 1: retrieve week 1 learning. Day 2: teach concept B. Day 3: guided practice B. Day 4: combine A and B. Day 5: short checkpoint and corrections. |
| 3 | Day 1: retrieval. Day 2: teach concept C. Day 3: worked example and practice. Day 4: apply C to a new familiar context. Day 5: checkpoint; assign pathway for the next skill. |
| 4 | Day 1: spaced review of weeks 1–3. Day 2: teach concept D. Day 3: guided-to-independent practice. Day 4: explain reasoning / produce a response. Day 5: mid-unit assessment and feedback. |
| 5 | Day 1: repair the two most common errors. Day 2: teach concept E. Day 3: practise E. Day 4: combine C–E. Day 5: short cumulative quiz and corrections. |
| 6 | Day 1: retrieval from weeks 1–5. Day 2: teach concept F or an approved practical skill. Day 3: practice with feedback. Day 4: real-life application / investigation / extended text. Day 5: checkpoint and targeted support. |
| 7 | Day 1: review. Day 2: teach concept G. Day 3: apply G independently. Day 4: cumulative mixed questions. Day 5: timed mini-assessment if developmentally and syllabus appropriate. |
| 8 | Day 1: targeted revision by skill. Day 2: mixed-topic practice. Day 3: explain errors and redo missed items. Day 4: subject-specific practice paper/task. Day 5: marking, moderation and feedback. |
| 9 | Day 1: revision plan and retrieval. Day 2: practice assessment part A. Day 3: practice assessment part B or practical task. Day 4: corrections and oral explanation. Day 5: teacher review, portfolio update and next-term goals. |

For each week, replace “concept A/B/C…” with the exact official outcome(s) after verification. The five daily slots are planning slots, not an instruction to invent five new outcomes every week. Combine outcomes where the syllabus expects a longer sequence.

### Term 3 — five teaching weeks before the school’s exam period

| Week | Daily sequence |
|---|---|
| 1 | Day 1 baseline across key outcomes; Day 2 teach/reteach priority outcome 1; Day 3 guided and independent practice; Day 4 local real-life application; Day 5 retrieval check. |
| 2 | Day 1 retrieval; Day 2 priority outcome 2; Day 3 worked examples; Day 4 mixed outcome 1+2 practice; Day 5 checkpoint and correction. |
| 3 | Day 1 retrieval; Day 2 priority outcome 3; Day 3 guided practice; Day 4 unfamiliar-but-accessible application; Day 5 cumulative mini-test. |
| 4 | Day 1 analyse errors; Day 2 targeted small-group teaching; Day 3 mixed-topic practice; Day 4 practice assessment; Day 5 mark, moderate and teach missed skills. |
| 5 | Day 1 retrieval plan; Day 2 practice assessment section A; Day 3 section B / practical / oral task as relevant; Day 4 corrections and confidence-building; Day 5 teacher sign-off and individual revision plan. |

Then schedule school-approved revision, intervention and exams according to the real school calendar. Do not assume a universal exam date or publish a restricted examination paper.

## 6. Daily lesson record template (every class and subject)

**Header:** country=Fiji; school; class; subject; term; week; learning day; auto-date; timetable slot; curriculum version; official outcome ID/text; source/page; mapping status; teacher reviewer.

**Student-facing sequence:**
1. “Today I will learn…” (one clear outcome).
2. “Look at this example…” (worked example, demonstration, picture, object, source or short text).
3. “Try it…” (pathway-specific task).
4. “Show what you know…” (short check).
5. “Review yesterday…” (2–5 retrieval items, adjusted for ECE and class level).
6. “Next step…” (feedback and next activity).

**Evidence options:** written response, oral explanation, teacher observation, practical demonstration, drawing/model, approved audio, or approved photo/video where appropriate. Camera/voice must not be the only evidence route. Avoid capturing bystanders or sensitive information.

**Teacher controls:** approve activity before student release; confirm the student record/ID; review evidence; request a retry or mark “needs teacher review”; protect student ID from filenames and public links. Automated marking may suggest a result but must flag ambiguous handwriting, voice recognition, practical work and low-confidence cases for teacher review.

## 7. Assessment and marking templates

### ECE observation record
Observe play, communication, movement, early number, exploration, social interaction and self-help against the adopted ECE outcomes. Record what the child did, context, support provided and next opportunity. Do not use high-stakes timed written exams as the default ECE assessment.

### Primary practice assessment
Use a balanced mix of oral response, reading/comprehension, writing, numeracy, short-answer, matching, multiple choice and practical tasks only where these match the subject and current syllabus. Include:
- student instructions in plain language;
- number of marks and time guidance suitable for the level;
- answer key with acceptable alternatives;
- item-by-item marking criteria;
- skill/outcome assessed;
- partial-credit rules;
- feedback and linked reteaching activity;
- accessible alternative for disability, language or connectivity barriers.

### Secondary practice assessment
Build only from the verified subject syllabus and assessment specification. Where relevant, include knowledge, application, analysis, source/data interpretation, structured extended responses, practical/coursework and evaluation. Provide a mark scheme with criteria, sample acceptable points, method marks where applicable and moderation notes. Do not invent official grade boundaries, examination weightings or paper structures.

### Marking record
For each item store: maximum mark; awarded mark; rubric criterion; feedback linked to the skill; evidence/source; confidence; reviewer status; and next learning activity. Report skill mastery separately from total percentage. Do not permanently label learners “poor” or “top”.

## 8. Four pathway versions of each activity

Every daily activity record must provide four versions of the same learning goal—not four unrelated curricula.

| Pathway | Required adaptation |
|---|---|
| Supported Start | Concrete example, small number of questions, vocabulary preview, oral/visual response and guided first item. |
| Developing | Prompt or scaffold, moderate practice set, one example, gradual removal of help. |
| Working at Class Goal | Full intended class-level task, independent response and explanation. |
| Extension | Multi-step or open-ended application, reasoning, comparison, creation or justified solution. |

All four versions share the same official outcome when appropriate. If a prerequisite skill is missing, link to a prerequisite activity and record why; do not quietly replace the class outcome.

## 9. Printable and download requirements

Within each subject box, provide:
- print activity sheet;
- accessible large-text version;
- answer guide and marking rubric (teacher-only until the assessment window closes);
- revision pack by verified outcome;
- practice paper clearly labelled non-official unless authorised;
- offline-friendly copy with class/subject/date/version;
- teacher key kept separate from student copy.

Do not put student full names or FEMIS IDs into public filenames or URLs. Use access-controlled internal identifiers. Never expose another student’s work when showing a portfolio.

For **ECE, Years 1–8, and Forms 3–7**, repeat this checklist for every school-confirmed subject box:
- [ ] Confirm subject/elective and curriculum version from official source and school programme.
- [ ] Record each official outcome/indicator and exact source page/section.
- [ ] Allocate outcomes across the school-approved term calendar; retain prerequisite order.
- [ ] Write the daily explanation, local example, four pathway tasks and exit check.
- [ ] Add retrieval/revision items linked to prior outcomes.
- [ ] Add weekly checkpoint and reteaching links.
- [ ] Add end-of-term revision and a practice assessment aligned to the verified outcomes.
- [ ] Add answer key, item-level rubric, partial-credit rules and moderation notes.
- [ ] Check language, disability access, low-bandwidth/print use and cultural relevance.

- [ ] Run automated validation for missing sources, missing answers, broken links, incorrect class/subject and accidental student-data exposure.

## 11. Build and release acceptance checks

- No subject appears unless confirmed for that class/school.
- No lesson claims official alignment without source/version/outcome/page/reviewer metadata.
- No missing date is replaced with a made-up date.
- Daily activity, revision, practice exam and marking guide remain in the same subject stream.
- Four ability pathways exist for every eligible activity; ECE adaptations remain play-based.
- Answer keys and unreleased assessment content are teacher-only.
- Students see an activity only after teacher approval and only in their own account.
- Student identification data is access-controlled and absent from public links/filenames.
- Forms 3–7 use their own selected subjects and current syllabus; no primary worksheet is blindly reused.
- Official exams, grade boundaries and assessment weightings are not invented or copied from restricted materials.
- Automated assessment flags uncertain results for teacher review.
- Existing working application code is preserved; this document alone does not change app behaviour.
