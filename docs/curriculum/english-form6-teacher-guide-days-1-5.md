# Form 6 English — Week 1 Teacher Marking Guide (Days 1–5)

**Status:** PENDING_TEACHER_REVIEW and PENDING_OFFICIAL_INDICATOR_VERIFICATION. PacEdu working guide only. These linked-source stimuli are not prescribed texts or examination materials.

## Common marking principles
- Assess comparative interpretation, synthesis, precise evidence, analysis of authorial choices, evaluation of limitations and nuanced conclusions.
- Accept defensible alternative interpretations and equivalent wording. Do not penalise accent, disability-related communication differences, handwriting speed or device access.
- Accept accessible oral, signed, AAC, dictated, Braille or written responses. Record response mode, independence and prompting.
- Keep all outcomes PENDING_TEACHER_REVIEW. Pathways are flexible scaffolds, not fixed ability labels.

## Day 1 — Oral communication
**Expected:** coherent thesis, synthesis of two sources, analysis of value/risk, qualification and response to a challenge.
- Source 1 supports intergenerational learning and contributor review/correction.
- Source 2 raises the risk of recordings being reused beyond their original purpose and asks about access/withdrawal.
- The draft policy offers voluntary participation, contributor review and withdrawal but leaves access rules unfinished.
- A strong response may recommend proceeding conditionally after clear access rules, cost/resources and governance are settled.
- Next step: practise synthesising rather than summarising sources separately.

## Day 2 — Close reading
**Expected:** comparative interpretation with one similarity, one difference, precise evidence and analysis.
- Both texts value the family gathering.
- Text A emphasises continuity and memory; Text B emphasises access, explanation and invitation.
- The contrast shows that belonging may involve both shared inheritance and active inclusion.
- Credit other interpretations when supported by details from both texts.
- Next step: use a comparative claim and connect evidence from each source within the same paragraph.

## Day 3 — Vocabulary and language
**Expected:** analyse metaphor, formal/technical register and evaluative language across texts.
- “Stories are a bridge between generations” presents language as connection and continuity.
- “Contributors may submit material subject to review, access controls and withdrawal provisions” uses procedural, formal wording to foreground governance but may feel technical.
- “Cold and complicated” communicates dissatisfaction but lacks a specific example.
- A justified revision should improve clarity without removing the policy's precision.
- Next step: explain the cumulative effect of language choices rather than treating each word in isolation.

## Day 4 — Critical literacy
**Expected:** rank sources for a defined claim, identify omission, distinguish evidence from speculation and qualify a conclusion.
- Source A is an identifiable draft and describes voluntary participation/review but omits post-publication audio access rules.
- Source B is anonymous and unsupported; it does not establish automatic publication without permission.
- Source C confirms that consent/access rules remain under discussion; it does not confirm the anonymous post's claim.
- Strong conclusion: publication rules remain unresolved and require confirmation; do not state that unauthorised publication is already planned.
- Next step: state what each source can and cannot prove.

## Day 5 — Writing and shaping
**Expected:** sustained comparative essay with thesis, integrated evidence, analysis, evaluation and nuanced conclusion.
- Possible thesis: preservation is ethical only when contributor control is built into governance.
- Educational value: intergenerational learning and preservation of pronunciation, stories and local knowledge.
- Risks: extraction, loss of control, unclear reuse, storage and access.
- Safeguards must be clear, enforceable and understood by contributors.
- Credit different nuanced judgements if they compare viewpoints and address limitations.
- Next step: organise essay by analytical claims and integrate sources rather than writing one isolated paragraph per source.

## Pathway support
- Copper: source-by-source gloss and comparison frame.
- Bronze: comparative planning frame and reduced prompts.
- Silver: independent synthesis and precise evidence.
- Gold: nuanced synthesis, evaluation of assumptions and qualification.
