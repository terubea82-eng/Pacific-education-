## Day 1 — Listening and speaking: The Mangrove Walk
**Core answer:** Main message: mangroves support wildlife and help protect shorelines. Evidence: roots shelter young fish and crabs; roots help hold the muddy shore; the group reported rubbish safely to the guide.
**Marking (5):** 1 clear summary; 1 first accurate detail; 1 second accurate detail; 1 reasoned explanation of safe reporting; 1 coherent and respectful response. Accept paraphrase and accessible response modes.
**Pathway evidence:** Copper identifies main message and completes a supported reason; Bronze uses the frame; Silver summarises with two details; Gold compares ecological and community reasons.

## Day 2 — Reading and viewing: A Rainwater Project
**Core answer:** Central idea: a school collects rainwater safely to support its garden and monitor water use. Evidence: roof gutters feed a covered tank; learners record rainfall and garden use. Inference: a closed lid and clean collection area help keep stored water protected from contamination. The passage explicitly says collected rainwater is not treated as drinking water.
**Marking (5):** 1 central idea; 1 each for two relevant details; 1 inference; 1 textual link for the inference. Accept other defensible inferences supported by the text.
**Pathway evidence:** Copper selects the central idea and details; Bronze completes the frame; Silver gives evidence-based inference; Gold explains the distinction between water conservation and drinking-water safety.

## Day 3 — Vocabulary and language: Preparing for the Cyclone Season
**Core answers:** preparedness = being ready in advance; reliable = trustworthy; assistance = help/support; informed = having useful facts before deciding. Possible alternatives: ready/plan ahead; dependable; help; aware/knowledgeable (check context).
**Marking (6):** 1 each for four accurate explanations; 1 each for two suitable alternatives and/or one accurate revised sentence (accept equivalent demonstration of understanding). Do not require one memorised definition.
**Pathway evidence:** Copper matches definitions; Bronze completes frames; Silver explains and revises; Gold distinguishes near-synonyms such as reliable and accurate.

## Day 4 — Grammar and sentence craft: A Clear Community Notice
**Core answer examples:** “The school hall will be open on Friday afternoon, and families can attend the reading afternoon.” “If a learner needs an accessible copy, the family can contact the class teacher before Thursday.” Other grammatically correct combinations are acceptable.
**Marking (5):** 1 first combined sentence; 1 second combined sentence; 1 suitable conjunctions; 1 accurate punctuation/capitalisation; 1 clear polite instruction. Award for valid alternative sentence structures.
**Pathway evidence:** Copper joins one supported pair; Bronze combines two pairs; Silver revises two pairs and adds an instruction; Gold produces a polished notice and explains one clarity improvement.

## Day 5 — Writing and shaping: A Helpful Community Idea
**Marking (6):** 1 identifies a real/sensible need; 1 clear proposed action; 1 relevant detail; 1 further practical detail(s); 1 logical cohesion and audience awareness; 1 effective closing invitation. For Gold, credit an inclusive barrier and workable solution within the same objective.
**Pathway evidence:** Copper uses a short frame; Bronze plans and writes 5–6 connected sentences; Silver develops a paragraph with three details; Gold writes 8–10 purposeful sentences, considers an access barrier and revises for clarity.
**Accept:** relevant and feasible ideas, equivalent wording and any accessible communication mode that demonstrates the intended learning.
