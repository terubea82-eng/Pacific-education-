# TEACHER ONLY — Class 1 English Week 18 Marking Guide (Copper)

**Questions and complete responses**  
**Programme days:** 86–90 | **Term:** 2 | **Week:** 9  
**Pathway:** Copper | **Do not place this answer guide in the learner download.**

## Learning intention
Learner asks simple questions and gives relevant answers that match the question. All pathways address the same intention; supports and response complexity vary.

## Passage used in the matching learner worksheet
> Read or listen: “Mere has a red bag. She puts a book in the bag. Mere walks to school.”

## Worked-example rationale
Question: What does Mere have? Answer: A red bag. I look for the thing Mere has and answer with the information from the passage.

## Indicative passage answers — accept equivalent wording
1. **Who has a bag?** Mere.
2. **What colour is the bag?** Red.
3. **What does Mere put in the bag?** A book.
4. **Where does Mere walk?** To school.

## Day-by-day marking guidance

| Day | Evidence to look for | Marking guidance |

## Pathway-specific scaffolding and expected evidence

- **Copper:** Read the passage and questions aloud; use picture/object supports and two-choice answers; allow pointing, matching, gesture, sign, AAC or dictated words. Expected evidence: selects or communicates a relevant answer to familiar who/what questions and participates in a supported question exchange.
- **Bronze:** Provide question-word symbols and a small answer bank. Model a response, then reduce prompts. Expected evidence: answers basic who/what/when questions and asks or completes a simple partner question with support.
- **Silver:** Invite independent use of who/what/where/when and a complete response containing the key information. Expected evidence: locates details, asks a relevant question and records a partner's answer.
- **Gold:** Invite question-word variety, accurate interview recording and complete-sentence reporting. Expected evidence: asks clear questions, gives complete relevant answers and supports answers with passage details.

## Marking principles

- A response is correct when it answers the question's meaning. Accept equivalent words, phrases and complete sentences; do not require the exact sample wording.
- Accept a concise answer when it contains the key information. Encourage full sentences where appropriate to the pathway and task.
- Do not require a learner to speak aloud. Accept sign, AAC, pointing, matching, drawing, dictated or written responses.
- Do not penalise spelling, handwriting, accent, speech clarity, eye contact or speed unless one is explicitly the learning target.
- Interview questions must be respectful and age-appropriate. Do not require disclosure of private or distressing information; a favourite book, game, food or school activity is sufficient.
- No fixed numerical pass threshold is assigned in this draft. Teacher records: secure / developing / revisit, with evidence and next step.
