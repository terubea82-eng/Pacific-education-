# PacEdu worksheet pack — Class 1 Mathematics
**Status:** Original pilot material; official Fiji syllabus/outcome mapping and teacher review are still required.  
**Term/Week/Day:** Editable teacher setting. **Learning goal:** Count, compare and solve simple number problems using familiar Fiji-life examples.  
**Four separate downloads:** Support (0–49%), Developing (50–64%), Expected (65–79%), Advanced (80–100%). These are provisional progress bands, not permanent labels.

## Worksheet A — Support (0–49%)
**Learner ID:** __________ **Date:** __________ **Group:** Support  
**Instruction:** Count the objects. Point, say, circle, or write the answer.

**Example:** ● ● ● = **3**

1. Count: ● ● = ____  
2. Count: ● ● ● ● = ____  
3. Circle the bigger number: **2** or **5**  
4. Finish the count: 1, 2, __, 4  
5. Mere has 2 bananas. Her teacher gives her 1 more. How many bananas now? ____  
**Teacher key:** 1) 2; 2) 4; 3) 5; 4) 3; 5) 3. One mark each (5).

## Worksheet B — Developing (50–64%)
**Learner ID:** __________ **Date:** __________ **Group:** Developing  
**Instruction:** Work out each number problem. Use counters if needed.

**Example:** 3 shells + 2 shells = 5 shells.

1. 4 + 1 = ____  
2. 6 − 2 = ____  
3. Which is smaller: 7 or 4? ____  
4. Fill the missing number: 2, 3, __, 5  
5. A shop has 5 coconuts and sells 2. How many are left? ____  
**Teacher key:** 1) 5; 2) 4; 3) 4; 4) 4; 5) 3. One mark each (5).

## Worksheet C — Expected (65–79%)
**Learner ID:** __________ **Date:** __________ **Group:** Expected  
**Instruction:** Write a number sentence and answer.

**Example:** 5 mangoes + 3 mangoes = 8 mangoes.

1. 7 + 4 = ____  
2. 12 − 5 = ____  
3. Put in order from smallest to largest: 9, 3, 6 → __________  
4. There are 8 pupils in one group and 6 in another. How many pupils altogether? ____  
5. Litia has 10 crayons. She shares 4 with a friend. How many does she keep? Show the number sentence: __________  
**Teacher key:** 1) 11; 2) 7; 3) 3, 6, 9; 4) 14; 5) 10 − 4 = 6. One mark each (5).

## Worksheet D — Advanced (80–100%)
**Learner ID:** __________ **Date:** __________ **Group:** Advanced  
**Instruction:** Solve. Show how you know. More than one method may work.

**Example:** 6 + 4 = 10. Check: 10 − 4 = 6.

1. A class collects 9 cans on Monday and 7 on Tuesday. How many cans altogether? Show a number sentence. __________  
2. The class needs 20 cans. How many more are needed after collecting 16? __________  
3. Complete and explain the pattern: 2, 4, 6, 8, __, __. __________  
4. Make two different number sentences that equal 12. __________ and __________  
5. Sera says 8 + 5 = 12. Is she correct? Show a way to check and explain the mistake. __________  
6. Create a simple market problem using addition or subtraction, then solve it. __________  
**Teacher key:** 1) 9 + 7 = 16; 2) 20 − 16 = 4; 3) 10, 12 (add 2 each time); 4) any two correct examples, e.g. 6+6 and 10+2; 5) no, 8+5=13, check by counting on or using 13−5=8; 6) assess sensible problem and correct solution. Total 7 marks (Q4=1, Q6=2; others 1 each).

## Accessibility
- **Blind/low vision:** Read symbols aloud; provide screen-reader-friendly equations and tactile counters. Never rely on dot pictures or color alone.
- **Deaf/hard of hearing:** Display all instructions in text; use number cards, clear diagrams and captions for any video. Audio is optional, not required.
- **Other access needs:** Accept pointing, counters, typing, AAC, oral answers or a scribe when suitable; record response mode.

## Teacher quick guide
1. Give the worksheet matching the learner's current Mathematics evidence.
2. Show the example first; let the child try before prompting.
3. Mark with the key and record any help used.
4. Review more than one activity before changing groups; a learner can be advanced in one subject and developing in another.
5. Keep the key in the teacher/reviewer view and release the student sheet through existing approval controls.
