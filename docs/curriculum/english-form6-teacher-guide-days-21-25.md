# Form 6 English — Week 5 Teacher Marking Guide (Days 21–25)

**Status:** PENDING_TEACHER_REVIEW and PENDING_OFFICIAL_INDICATOR_VERIFICATION. Original practice sources only; not prescribed texts or examination materials.

## Common marking principles
Assess comparative reasoning, context, source limitations, consent, evidence precision and sustained cohesion. Accept accessible oral, signed, AAC, dictated, Braille or written responses. Do not score accent, disability-related communication differences, handwriting speed or device access as errors. Record response mode, independence and prompts. Keep PENDING_TEACHER_REVIEW.

## Day 21 — Oral communication
- Youth group statement describes retaining language and central message, adapting rhythm and inviting elders to review.
- Practitioner interview supports youth learning but notes that some chants have specific settings and require consultation with relevant knowledge holders.
- Credit recommendations for context review, consent, consultation and explanation of performance choices.
- Counterargument may state that consultation supports informed creativity rather than preventing it; avoid treating one interview as a universal rule.

## Day 22 — Close reading
- Personal reflection uses first-person memory, notebook imagery and a reflective question to present adaptation as layered inheritance.
- Archive note uses formal descriptive language and qualifies its scope by acknowledging local versions and missing views.
- Credit supported alternative interpretations of whether the new version extends, changes or relocates the song's meaning.

## Day 23 — Vocabulary and language
- “Authentic” and “unchanged” may imply one fixed standard or version.
- “Selected” limits the scope; “vary across families” acknowledges diversity.
- A suitable rewrite: “Explore performances developed by participating groups, reflecting selected interpretations and contemporary choices.” Credit other engaging wording that avoids claiming one version represents everyone.

## Day 24 — Critical literacy
- Organiser report establishes that six participating groups approved descriptions and selected representatives; it does not establish that all families were invited.
- Community response raises concerns about invitation and representativeness but does not quantify the scope of exclusion.
- Further checks: publish selection criteria; document who was invited and how; provide accessible contribution routes; obtain consent; accurately label the scope of each performance; invite corrections.
- Distinguish consent to participate from authority to represent a whole community.

## Day 25 — Writing and shaping
- Policy offers proposal, review and correction rights but lacks selection and disagreement procedures.
- Advisory note supports youth participation while recommending consent, context notes, correction and accurate limits on representation.
- Counterargument: extensive safeguards could discourage participation. Credit proportionate, clear procedures that support creativity and protect contributors.
- Next step: revise thesis, integration of both sources, ethical reasoning and practical conclusion.

## Pathway evidence
- Copper: identifies evidence from both sources with labels, model and recorded prompts.
- Bronze: organises comparison using a frame and reduces prompts.
- Silver: independently analyses context, consent and source limitations.
- Gold: synthesises perspectives and evaluates ethical implications with nuance.
