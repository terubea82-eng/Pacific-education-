# Form 7 English — Week 6 Teacher Marking Guide (Days 26–30)

**Status:** PENDING_TEACHER_REVIEW and PENDING_OFFICIAL_INDICATOR_VERIFICATION. Original practice sources only; not prescribed texts, Ministry materials or past examination questions.

## Common marking principles
- Assess critical synthesis, evidence precision, uncertainty, method awareness, source limitations and sustained cohesion.
- Accept well-supported alternative interpretations and accessible oral, signed, AAC, dictated, Braille or written responses. Do not score accent, disability-related communication differences, handwriting speed or device access as errors.
- Record evidence, response mode, independence and prompts. Keep PENDING_TEACHER_REVIEW.
- Pathways are flexible scaffolds, not permanent ability labels.

## Day 26 — Oral communication
- Weather bulletin reports variation at three stations and cautions against treating short-term variation as proof of a long-term trend.
- Preparedness group reports local damage and asks for local observations to be considered; it lacks a systematic rainfall dataset.
- Credit recommendations that combine station monitoring with local impact reports and transparent uncertainty. Local accounts can identify impacts and questions even when they are not measurements of rainfall trends.
- Next step: distinguish measurement, lived impact and policy relevance.

## Day 27 — Close reading
- Editorial uses an extended metaphor (“warning written in sand”) and a future-focused question to create urgency.
- Technical note specifies four sites, two periods and inability to attribute one cause; it recommends further seasonal measurement.
- Credit analysis showing that urgency and uncertainty can coexist when claims are accurately framed.
- Next step: explain how scope and certainty shape reader response.

## Day 28 — Vocabulary and language
- “Front line” frames the island as exposed to threat; “takes everything” is absolute and emotive.
- “May influence” signals possibility, not certainty; “does not quantify” identifies a measurement gap.
- Suitable rewrite may call for action to assess and reduce coastal risk while acknowledging that impacts differ by location and require further evidence.
- Next step: match rhetorical urgency to evidence strength.

## Day 29 — Critical literacy
- Proposal predicts reduced overtopping under selected modelled wave conditions but omits full assessment of beach movement and maintenance.
- Users' submission offers six accounts and requests review but lacks coastal measurements and broad representation.
- Further evidence: model assumptions and validation; beach/sediment movement assessment; boat landing/access mapping; maintenance costs; independent coastal review; wider user consultation.
- Next step: distinguish model outputs, observations and stakeholder experience.

## Day 30 — Writing and shaping
- Planning brief offers possible protection benefits but lacks full beach-effects assessment and long-term maintenance budget.
- Community note identifies access, landing-place and cost concerns but does not represent every household or provide technical measurements.
- A balanced recommendation may support further design and urgent interim safety measures while withholding final approval until critical gaps are addressed.
- Credit a fair counterargument that delay may leave the road exposed, answered with proportionate interim measures and a clear evidence timetable.
- Next step: revise thesis, evidence integration, uncertainty qualification and conditions for decision.

## Pathway evidence
- Copper: identifies accurate details from both sources with labels, model and recorded prompts.
- Bronze: organises comparison using a frame and reduces prompts.
- Silver: independently integrates evidence and evaluates uncertainty.
- Gold: develops nuanced methodological critique and policy qualification.
