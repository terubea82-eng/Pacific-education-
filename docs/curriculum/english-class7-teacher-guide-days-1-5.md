## Day 1 — Listening and speaking: Planning a Youth Forum
**Core viewpoints:** sports equipment can support physical activity; shaded seating and accessible paths can widen use; collecting views and checking the budget can guide a shared decision. Common ground is improving the community space for different users.
**Marking (5):** 1 accurate first viewpoint; 1 accurate second viewpoint; 1 common-ground statement; 1 relevant question; 1 balanced explanation/contribution. Accept paraphrase and accessible response modes.
**Pathways:** Copper identifies two ideas; Bronze uses the frame; Silver summarises viewpoints and asks a question; Gold evaluates a fair decision criterion.

## Day 2 — Reading and viewing: Reading a Public Information Report
**Core claim:** extended hours may improve access for some people. Evidence: visitor numbers were higher on trial afternoons; some visitors said later hours helped. Limitation: community events may have influenced attendance; transport and caring responsibilities remained barriers.
**Marking (5):** 1 purpose/claim; 1 each for two accurate evidence points; 1 limitation; 1 evaluation that does not overstate the data.
**Pathways:** Copper finds a fact; Bronze completes a report frame; Silver analyses claim/evidence/limitation; Gold evaluates continuation and proposes further data.

## Day 3 — Vocabulary and language: Language in a Campaign Message
**Core answers:** “careless” may blame; “shared” signals collective responsibility; “protect” presents a positive purpose; “help” invites participation. Other defensible connotation explanations are acceptable.
**Marking (6):** up to 4 for contextual word explanations; 1 for tone/connotation analysis; 1 for audience-appropriate revision and justification.
**Pathways:** Copper matches meanings; Bronze explains two words; Silver analyses both versions; Gold creates and justifies a third version.

## Day 4 — Grammar and sentence craft: Building a Reasoned Argument
**Example:** “Although some learners prefer audio or shared reading, a flexible reading period can offer different ways to enjoy texts.” Accept grammatically correct alternatives that connect claim, reason or qualification.
**Marking (5):** 1 clear claim; 1 relevant reason; 1 qualification/counterpoint; 1 cohesive linking; 1 accurate punctuation and sentence structure.
**Pathways:** Copper combines a supported pair; Bronze combines two pairs; Silver writes a reasoned paragraph; Gold adds and explains a concession/counterpoint.

## Day 5 — Writing and shaping: A Proposal to Improve Access
**Marking (6):** 1 defined need; 1 evidence/feedback; 1 feasible action; 1 inclusion measure; 1 consideration of resources; 1 measurable review step. For Gold, credit ethical feedback collection and a clear success indicator.
**Pathways:** Copper identifies a problem and change; Bronze uses the planning frame; Silver drafts a structured proposal; Gold prioritises and justifies actions for the committee.

## Assessment and access
- Accept Braille, large print, screen-reader text, audio directions, sign, AAC, pointing, eye gaze, object choice, dictation, typing, scribing or handwriting.
- Allow additional processing time, sensory breaks and adapted tools. Do not infer ability from disability, speech, accent, handwriting or device access.
- Record actual evidence, response mode, prompt level and next teaching step.
- Copper/Bronze/Silver/Gold are flexible evidence-based pathways, not permanent labels.
- Accept equivalent wording and valid alternative answers.
