# TEACHER-ONLY GUIDE — Class 2 English Week 10 (Bronze)

**Days 46–50 | Nouns and Verbs in Meaningful Sentences | Do not distribute as the learner worksheet**

## Intended learning evidence
Learner identifies nouns as naming words and verbs as action words in context, sorts examples accurately, and constructs meaningful sentences with a clear subject and verb.

## Text used
After school, Jone visited the garden with his sister. They carried a small watering can. Jone filled it with water. His sister planted two flowers. A bee landed on one flower. The children watched it and smiled.

## Indicative answers and teaching notes
- **Day 46:** Accept people, places, animals and things named in the story. Possible examples: Jone, sister, garden, watering can, water, flowers, bee, visited. Some examples may fit different noun categories; accept sensible classifications.
- **Day 47:** Accept action verbs actually used in the story, such as carried, filled, planted, landed, watched, smiled. Learner should identify who/what performs the action.
- **Day 48:** Check word function in context, not only memorised labels. A noun names; a verb expresses an action in these story examples.
- **Day 49:** Accept grammatically understandable sentences that pair a suitable subject/noun with a verb, e.g. “The children watched.” Accept equivalent wording and communication modes.
- **Day 50:** Look for two understandable sentences with clear subjects and verbs; the improved sentence should add a relevant detail without changing the meaning inaccurately.

## Pathway support — adjustable, not a fixed ability label
Offer a noun/action-word sorting prompt if needed. Encourage the learner to make a short sentence with a naming word and an action word.

## Evidence record
For each day record: independent / verbal prompt / visual prompt / modelled / not yet observed. Note the learner's own words or communication response. Distinguish identifying a word from copying it. Record whether the learner can explain or demonstrate its function in context.

## Accessible assessment and marking
Credit valid equivalent wording and accurate identification in context. Accept speech, sign, AAC, pointing, matching, drawing with labels or writing. Do not penalise spelling or handwriting when assessing grammar concepts. Use prompts consistently and record their level. No single weekly score establishes permanent pathway placement.
