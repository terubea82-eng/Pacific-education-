**Learning intention:** I notice sounds around me, hear words that rhyme, and create or identify a simple rhyming pair.

**Ways to answer:** Speak, sign, point, use AAC, select picture/word cards, clap, draw, dictate or write. The teacher reads and describes every task. Learners may use familiar local-language examples orally as a bridge, while the assessed English examples remain clear and familiar. Do not penalise accent or speech differences.

**Access and safety:** Offer large-print/high-contrast or tactile picture cards, quiet listening space, captions/visual sound cues and extra wait time. Do not use sudden or painfully loud sounds. A learner who cannot hear an environmental sound can work with a visual/tactile representation or teacher-described sound; record the response mode.

**Word/picture set for this practice:** cat–hat; sun–fun; bell–shell; clap–tap. These are practice examples, not a claim about official textbook sequence. Teacher checks vocabulary is familiar before use.

**Ability pathway:** Gold — compare sound patterns, justify choices and compose a short rhyme
