- Schedule: 10 sample learning days, not assigned to a specific calendar date. PacEdu should assign dates from the school's confirmed calendar and teacher plan.

## Teacher use
Teach and model the target skill first. Choose the version matching current evidence for that skill. Learners may move between versions. Accept oral, drawn, signed, tactile or written responses where appropriate and where the mode does not change the skill being assessed. Keep the answer guide away from the student view. Do not require internet, paid materials, photos or audio recording.

## Day 1 — Introduce myself

**Topic basis:** Year 1 English resource, Unit 1 “Who am I?”

**Learning objective:** Learners share simple information about themselves and listen respectfully to a partner.

**Teacher model:** “My name is Ana. I am six years old. I like blue.” Use a fictional example first; children need not share personal information they are uncomfortable sharing.

- **Supported Start:** Match a picture or name card to a spoken name; complete orally or with a word bank: “My name is ___.” Draw a favourite safe object.
- **Developing:** Complete: “My name is ___. I like ___.” Say the sentence to a partner or teacher.
- **Working at Class Goal:** Write or dictate three simple sentences about a chosen topic: name (optional), favourite colour and favourite activity. Begin each sentence with a capital letter and end with a full stop.
- **Extension:** Write four connected sentences introducing a fictional character; ask a partner one polite question and record the answer with permission.

**Check for learning:** Can the learner communicate one relevant idea and listen/respond appropriately?

**Worksheet:** 1. Draw yourself or a fictional character. 2. Complete “My name is ___.” 3. Complete “I like ___.” 4. Tell or write one more sentence.

**Mapping:** Topic supported by the Fiji Year 1 English resource; exact current official indicator code and wording pending verification.

## Day 2 — Letter sounds: s, a, t, i

**Topic basis:** Year 1 English resource, early letter-recognition/phonics pages.

**Learning objective:** Recognise selected taught letters and connect each to its taught sound in familiar words.

**Teacher model:** Show one letter at a time, say the sound clearly, model mouth position if helpful, then use a picture/word (e.g. sun for s, ant for a, tap for t, ink for i). Pronunciation and word choices should follow the teacher's approved phonics sequence.

- **Supported Start:** Choose the matching letter from two cards after hearing the sound; trace the letter with a finger or pencil.
- **Developing:** Match four letters to four picture/word cards and say or select the initial sound.
- **Working at Class Goal:** Write each taught letter, match it to a word/picture and identify the first sound in four familiar words.
- **Extension:** Sort a mixed set of familiar words by initial sound and explain how two words differ.

**Check for learning:** Record correct letter-sound matches individually; do not infer reading ability from handwriting alone.

**Worksheet:** Match s/a/t/i to sun/ant/tap/ink; trace and write each letter; circle the word beginning with the named sound from teacher-provided options.

**Mapping:** Exact current indicator code and wording pending verification.

## Day 3 — My friends: listen and describe

**Topic basis:** Year 1 English resource, Unit 2 “My Friends”.

**Learning objective:** Listen to a short description and identify stated details; use simple descriptive words.

**Teacher text:** “Sera has a red bag. Tazim has a blue book. They share their books and read together.” Read twice, slowly.

- **Supported Start:** Point to the red bag or blue book picture when named; answer by pointing, speaking or selecting a card.
- **Developing:** Answer “Who has the red bag?” and “What colour is the book?” using a word bank.
- **Working at Class Goal:** Write two answers in short sentences and describe one item using a colour word.
- **Extension:** Retell the passage in three sentences and add one respectful question a listener could ask.

**Questions:** 1. Who has the red bag? 2. What colour is the book? 3. What do the friends do together?

**Answer guide:** 1. Sera. 2. Blue. 3. They share books/read together.

**Check for learning:** Correctly identify details from listening and use relevant vocabulary.

**Mapping:** Topic supported by the Fiji Year 1 English resource; exact current indicator code and wording pending verification.

## Day 4 — My school: naming and describing

**Topic basis:** Year 1 English resource, Unit 3 “My School”.

**Learning objective:** Use familiar school vocabulary and create simple oral or written descriptions.

**Teacher model:** Use a drawing of a classroom, not a photo of identifiable children. Label: door, desk, book, teacher, class.

- **Supported Start:** Match three picture cards to labels; point to an item in a drawing.
- **Developing:** Complete “I see a ___.” with a word bank; name two classroom objects.
- **Working at Class Goal:** Write three simple sentences describing a classroom or library.
- **Extension:** Write a short welcome note for a new student with two helpful classroom directions.

**Worksheet:** Label a simple classroom drawing; complete “I see a ___”; write/dictate one way to keep the classroom safe and welcoming.

**Check for learning:** Vocabulary is understandable and descriptions match the picture.

**Mapping:** Exact current official indicator code and wording pending verification.

## Day 5 — Put daily routines in order

**Topic basis:** Year 1 English resource, Unit 4 “Getting Ready for School”.

**Learning objective:** Sequence familiar actions and use time/order words such as first, next and then.

**Teacher model:** “First, I get ready. Next, I wash my hands and brush my teeth. Then, I prepare for school.” Use picture cards and avoid implying every family follows the same routine.

- **Supported Start:** Arrange three picture cards in a sensible order with adult prompts.
- **Developing:** Fill in “First ___; next ___; then ___” using a word bank.
- **Working at Class Goal:** Write four steps for a safe, healthy morning routine using order words.
- **Extension:** Write instructions for preparing a school bag and explain why one item is important.

**Check for learning:** Steps are in a reasonable order and the learner can explain at least one choice.

**Worksheet:** Number four routine pictures 1–4; complete three sentences; draw or write one useful item to prepare for school.

**Mapping:** Exact current official indicator code and wording pending verification.

## Day 6 — Rainy-day words and safety

**Topic basis:** Year 1 English resource, Unit 5 “A Rainy Day” and Unit 6 “What I Do on a Rainy Day”.

**Learning objective:** Understand and use familiar weather words; communicate a safe action in wet weather.

**Teacher model:** “It is raining. The path is slippery. I walk carefully and follow my teacher or caregiver.” Explain that children should stay away from floodwater and drains and follow local adult guidance.

- **Supported Start:** Match rain/cloud/umbrella pictures to spoken words; choose the safe picture from two options.
- **Developing:** Complete “On a rainy day, I take ___.” and “I walk ___.” from a word bank.
- **Working at Class Goal:** Write three sentences describing rainy weather and two safe actions.
- **Extension:** Write a short safety message for younger learners and explain why one action matters.

**Questions:** 1. What weather is described? 2. Name one item that can help in rain. 3. Give one safe action near a wet path.

**Answer guide:** Rainy weather; e.g. umbrella/raincoat; walk carefully and follow adult guidance. Accept other safe answers.

**Check for learning:** Correct use of weather vocabulary and one reasonable safety action.

**Mapping:** Exact current official indicator code and wording pending verification.

## Day 7 — Read and understand a short passage

**Topic basis:** Familiar people, school and daily-life themes from the Year 1 English resource.

**Learning objective:** Read or listen to a short passage and answer literal comprehension questions.

**Passage:** “Litia walks to the school gate with her aunt. She sees a small garden. The class waters the plants with the teacher. Litia reads a book after the activity.”

- **Supported Start:** Listen to the passage and point to the school, garden or book when named; answer one who/what question orally or by picture.
- **Developing:** Read with support and answer two who/what questions using sentence starters.
- **Working at Class Goal:** Read the passage and answer all four questions in short sentences.
- **Extension:** Retell the events in order and write one sentence explaining how the class helps the garden.

**Questions:** 1. Who is with Litia at the gate? 2. What does she see? 3. Who helps the class water plants? 4. What does Litia do afterwards?

**Answer guide:** 1. Her aunt. 2. A small garden. 3. The teacher. 4. She reads a book.

**Check for learning:** Correctly identify stated information; do not mark spelling as comprehension failure unless spelling is the target.

**Mapping:** Exact current official indicator code and wording pending verification.

## Day 8 — Write a helpful message

**Topic basis:** Familiar school and family communication; creates a real-life literacy application.

**Learning objective:** Compose a short message for a clear audience and purpose.

**Teacher model:** “Dear class, please put the books back on the shelf. Thank you.” Discuss audience, clear words and respectful tone.

- **Supported Start:** Choose a picture and dictate or complete “Please ___.”
- **Developing:** Complete a two-sentence message using a word bank.
- **Working at Class Goal:** Write a short note asking classmates to care for a shared classroom resource.
- **Extension:** Write a polite message and revise it so the request is clear, respectful and easy to follow.

**Check for learning:** Message has a clear purpose and appropriate wording; accept dictation or assistive communication when appropriate.

**Worksheet:** Who is your message for? What do you want the reader to do? Write/dictate your message. Check: Is it clear? Is it respectful?

**Mapping:** Exact current official indicator code and wording pending verification.

## Day 9 — Words that help us cooperate

**Topic basis:** Family/community activity theme from the Year 1 resource, with a future-readiness focus on communication and teamwork.

**Learning objective:** Use action words and respectful language to describe helping safely.

**Teacher model:** “I help by sorting books.” “We take turns.” “I ask an adult before using something unsafe.” Do not assume any gender-specific household role.

- **Supported Start:** Match help/share/take turns to pictures or actions.
- **Developing:** Complete “I can help by ___.” and practise a polite request.
- **Working at Class Goal:** Write three sentences about helping at school or in the community.
- **Extension:** Create a three-step plan for a safe class task; name one way team members can share responsibility fairly.

**Check for learning:** Learner communicates an action and identifies a respectful way to cooperate.

**Worksheet:** Match action to picture; finish one sentence; draw/write one safe way to help.

**Mapping:** Exact current official indicator code and wording pending verification.

## Day 10 — Review and teacher check

**Purpose:** Review Days 1–9 and collect evidence to choose next activities; this is a formative check, not a national examination.

**Tasks:**
1. Match four taught letters to their sounds.
2. Listen to or read the Day 7 passage and answer two questions.
3. Put three daily-routine pictures in order.
4. Write, dictate or orally deliver two sentences about a familiar topic.
5. Choose a safe action for a rainy day.

**Ability adaptations:**
- **Supported Start:** 1:1 or small-group delivery, picture choices, extra wait time and fewer items while sampling each target skill.
- **Developing:** word banks and sentence starters.
- **Working at Class Goal:** complete all tasks independently where appropriate.
- **Extension:** add a short retell and one justified suggestion for improving a shared classroom space.

**Teacher record:** Record each skill separately (letter-sound knowledge, listening comprehension, sequencing, sentence communication, safety vocabulary). Use results to select the next teaching step. Do not assign a permanent ability label based on this single check.

**Marking guide:** One point per correct response for objective items; for spoken/written communication, use a simple rubric: (0) no relevant response yet, (1) idea conveyed with substantial support, (2) clear relevant idea with some support, (3) clear relevant idea independently. Adapt for accessibility and record the mode used. These scores are local formative evidence, not official national grading thresholds.
