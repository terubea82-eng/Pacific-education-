# PacEdu English Subject Box — Class 1 Daily Activities (Days 1–115)

**Location:** English Subject Box → Class 1 → Term → Week → Day. This is an original PacEdu practice and planning pack for teacher review; it is not an official Ministry-issued daily timetable.

**Programme structure:** 23 planning weeks; Term 1 = Weeks 1–9, Term 2 = Weeks 10–18, Term 3 = Weeks 19–23; five learning days each week = 115 days. School dates and teaching order must be reconciled with the school's approved calendar and scheme of work.

**Curriculum alignment status:** The repository's source register identifies a candidate Fiji Year 1 English resource (*Year 1 English — Language, Literacy and Communication*, states 2013, revised 2019, reprinted 2020), with Unit 1 “Who am I?” and S/s, A/a activities at resource pages 5–7. The copy currently located is hosted by a third party, not the Ministry website. It must be checked against the current authorized school/FEMIS/Ministry copy. The programme below uses original skill targets and planning topics; it does not claim exact official indicator IDs, wording, or week/day placement. For each week, the teacher must record the matching current official strand, outcome/achievement indicator, source edition and page before marking it curriculum-verified.

**Learning areas:** Listening and speaking; phonological awareness and word recognition; reading and viewing; vocabulary and language; writing and shaping. These are planning headings, not a claim that they reproduce the exact current Fiji syllabus strand labels.

**Ability pathways:** Copper, Bronze, Silver and Gold are flexible scaffolding/extension options. They are not fixed ability labels or permanent placements. All groups pursue the same weekly learning intention. PacEdu/teacher uses evidence to adjust support, never to restrict access to the class objective.

**Non-negotiable literacy safety:** Teach only grapheme–phoneme correspondences already introduced in the approved class sequence. Do not require learners to decode words containing untaught letter patterns. Accept oral, pointing, sign, AAC, dictated, drawn and written responses according to the skill being assessed. Do not score accent or disability-related speech differences as errors.

## Differentiation guide

- **Copper:** Provide adult modelling, tactile/large-print or picture supports, a small choice set, sound boxes/letter tiles, repeated oral directions and ample wait time. Accept pointing, matching, oral response, sign, AAC or dictated words.
- **Bronze:** Provide a visual cue, word bank, one worked example and guided practice; learner completes a similar item with prompts reduced where possible.
- **Silver:** Learner completes the same core objective with minimal prompting, reads or writes the expected task using only correspondences and vocabulary already taught.
- **Gold:** Extend the same objective with an unfamiliar but accessible example, a reason, comparison, extra sentence or independent editing; do not introduce untaught code as if already mastered.

## Daily activities

### Term 1 — Week 1 (programme Week 1): Myself, name and identity

**Weekly learning intention:** Share personal information and listen/respond to others.

**Planning focus:** Introduce yourself; match a spoken name to a name card; write or construct your name.

**Suggested resources:** Name cards, mirrors, familiar photographs or drawings, letter tiles and writing materials.

#### Day 1 — Listen and notice

**Teacher model:** Teacher models the target language/skill; learners listen, point to or identify examples.

**Student worksheet/activity:** Circle, point to, match or sort the target examples. Show the response through speech, sign, gesture, AAC, drawing or writing.
**PacEdu working achievement indicator:** The learner can identify or communicate a taught sound, word or idea connected with Myself, name and identity, using only the sounds, words and spelling patterns taught in the approved sequence.

**Worked example:** Teacher completes one example using this week's target, says or shows each step, then completes a second example together with learners. Select the actual word, sound, text or sentence from the current approved teaching sequence; do not introduce untaught correspondences.

**Copper:** Provide adult modelling, tactile/large-print or picture supports, a small choice set, sound boxes/letter tiles, repeated oral directions and ample wait time. Accept pointing, matching, oral response, sign, AAC or dictated words. For this day, respond to the target by finding or matching one example.

**Bronze:** Provide a visual cue, word bank, one worked example and guided practice; learner completes a similar item with prompts reduced where possible. For this day, complete the modelled task with the visual cue and word/picture bank, then attempt one similar item.

**Silver:** Learner completes the same core objective with minimal prompting, reads or writes the expected task using only correspondences and vocabulary already taught. For this day, complete the core activity and show the target skill in a matched/identified example and an oral or accessible response..

**Gold:** Extend the same objective with an unfamiliar but accessible example, a reason, comparison, extra sentence or independent editing; do not introduce untaught code as if already mastered. For this day, extend the same target by giving a reason, comparing two examples, creating an additional example or independently checking the work.

**Teacher prompt:** “What do you notice? Show me the part that helped you.”

**Success evidence:** Teacher records what was recognised and which prompts were needed. Expected evidence sample: A matched/identified example and an oral or accessible response. Record the learner's actual response mode and the prompt level used.

**Quick check (formative):** 1. Show or identify one target example. 2. Complete one new item. 3. Explain, say, point to, sign, dictate or draw how you know. Record: ☐ independently ☐ with prompt ☐ with model ☐ not yet observed.

**Student reflection:** “Today I can…” (learner may say, point, select a symbol, sign, dictate or write).

## Teacher assessment and marking guidance

- **Listening/speaking:** assess relevance and understanding of the message, not accent or willingness to speak publicly. Offer one-to-one, sign, AAC, pointing or other suitable modes.
- **Phonological awareness/phonics:** assess only the sound/letter patterns actually taught. Distinguish hearing a sound, identifying a letter and decoding a word; do not treat them as interchangeable.
- **Reading comprehension:** accept an accurate answer given orally, by pointing, matching, signing, AAC or writing when the target is comprehension. For text-evidence tasks, record the part of the text/picture used.
- **Writing:** assess the stated writing target (meaning, sentence structure, spelling pattern, spacing or punctuation). Do not penalise unrelated handwriting or spelling when not being assessed.
- **Short constructed responses and creative writing:** teacher-mark against a transparent rubric; do not auto-mark using one exact phrase. Credit equivalent meaningful wording.
- **MCQ:** use A/B/C/D only where a selected-answer check is appropriate. Keep answer keys and worked marking guidance in the authorized teacher view, not public student code.
- **Placement:** Copper/Bronze/Silver/Gold are adjustable teaching supports, not labels of a child's potential. Review from evidence and allow movement at any time.
