# Form 4 English — Week 2 Teacher Marking Guide (Days 6–10)

## Common marking principles
Assess interpretation, relevant evidence and explanation. Accept accessible oral, signed, AAC, dictated, Braille or written responses. Do not penalise accent, disability-related communication differences, handwriting speed or device access. Record response mode and prompting; keep PENDING_TEACHER_REVIEW.

## Day 6 — Oral communication
- Position: the bridge was both a crossing and a social meeting place.
- Details: people exchanged news and carried goods; neighbours worked together after a flood.
- Uncertain dates do not remove the value of the account; a record may check chronology while testimony explains experience.

## Day 7 — Close reading
- The narrator recognises the new crossing is safer but feels something about the old bridge has been lost.
- Details: sounds of wheels and river; grandfather pointing out water marks; final contrast between faster crossing and reduced attention.
- The final sentence suggests efficiency can change people's relationship with place.

## Day 8 — Vocabulary and language
- “A witness to many journeys” personifies the bridge as a keeper of community history.
- “Practical improvement” foregrounds safety and function but does not capture memory.
- Reflective register balances the value of change with attachment to familiar places.

## Day 9 — Critical literacy
- Council record confirms a repair after a flood, but not informal gatherings.
- Oral account describes gathering but date is uncertain.
- Photograph shows people on the bridge but has no date/caption and cannot establish purpose.
- Strong response identifies what can be corroborated and why all sources should be read together.

## Day 10 — Writing and shaping
- A developed response explains the different contributions of oral accounts, written records and photographs.
- Oral accounts add feelings/relationships; records check dates/decisions; photographs show visible details.
- Conclusion should explain why uncertainty and limitations must be stated.
