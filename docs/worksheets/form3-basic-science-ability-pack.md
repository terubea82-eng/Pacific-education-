# Form 3 Basic Science — four ability worksheets

Status: original pilot material. The project uses the requested label **Basic Science** for Forms 3–4, but the school must confirm the exact current official subject title and syllabus prescription before publishing this as an aligned worksheet. Flexible bands: Support 0–49%, Developing 50–64%, Expected 65–79%, Advanced 80–100%.

## A. Support (0–49%) — Observation and variables

**Example:** A variable is something that can change in an investigation.

1. Name one tool used to measure temperature.
2. In a plant-growth investigation, name one thing you could measure.
3. State one safety rule for practical work.
4. What is an observation?
5. A learner records plant height each Friday. Is height a measurement or a guess?

**Teacher guide (8 marks):** thermometer (2); height/leaf number (2); suitable safety rule (1); information noticed or measured using senses/tools (1); measurement (2).

## B. Developing (50–64%) — Simple forces and motion

1. What is a force in simple words?
2. Give one example of a push and one of a pull.
3. A trolley travels 12 m in 4 seconds. Calculate its average speed.
4. State the unit for speed when distance is measured in metres and time in seconds.
5. Explain one way friction can help us.
6. Explain one way friction can make movement harder.

**Teacher guide (10 marks):** push or pull (2); valid push and pull examples (2); speed = 12 ÷ 4 = 3 m/s (2); m/s (1); grip/braking/walking (1); slows movement/wears surfaces (2).

## C. Expected (65–79%) — Matter and fair testing

A group tests how quickly equal amounts of salt dissolve in cold and warm water.

1. Identify the independent variable.
2. Identify the dependent variable.
3. Name two factors to keep the same.
4. Explain why equal amounts of salt should be used.
5. State one safety precaution.
6. Write a simple conclusion format using the evidence collected.

**Teacher guide (12 marks):** water temperature (2); time taken to dissolve (2); same water volume, salt mass, stirring method or container (2); fair comparison (2); safe handling/no tasting/hot water handled carefully (2); conclusion links result to measured evidence (2).

## D. Advanced (80–100%) — Evidence and evaluation

A group says, “Warm water always dissolves salt faster.” They tested one cup of cold water and one cup of warm water, stirred them differently, and did not measure the salt.

1. Identify two weaknesses in the method.
2. Explain why the conclusion is too broad.
3. Design a fairer test, identifying the variable changed, what is measured, and at least three controls.
4. State how repeated trials improve confidence.
5. Write a conclusion that distinguishes evidence from a claim.

**Teacher guide (12 marks):** stirring not controlled and salt amount not measured (2); one trial/conditions do not prove “always” (2); clear independent and dependent variables plus controls (4); repeats reduce effect of unusual results (2); evidence-based cautious conclusion (2).

## Access and release
Provide written practical steps, diagrams with text labels, accessible tables, and safe alternatives for learners unable to handle equipment. Do not rely on color or sound alone. Adult supervision is required for practicals. Check the current Form 3 syllabus, correct subject title, safety requirements and taught content before release; keep the key teacher-only.
