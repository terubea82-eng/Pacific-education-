## Teaching boundary
This week reviews familiar word families and spelling patterns already taught in Class 2. Confirm the pattern set from the current class teaching record. Do not assume that every example in the word bank belongs to one family, and do not assess untaught patterns as mastered or deficient.

## Complete passage used by the learner
Tavi has a pet cat. The cat sat on a mat. Tavi fed the cat. The cat ran to the bed.

**Word bank:** cat · mat · sat · fed · ran · bed

## Worked example used
Look at “cat” and “mat”. Say each word, listen for the parts that sound the same, and match the words. Use only a word family or spelling pattern already taught.

## Day-by-day teaching and indicative marking
### Day 16 — Notice word families

**Teaching focus:** Notice familiar words that share an ending or another spelling pattern already taught.

**Matching learner task — Find a family match:** Find two words in the passage that share a word-family or spelling feature you have already learned. Match, point to, say, sign or write them.

**Indicative evidence:** Two words that genuinely share a taught feature. Accept any other correct example that matches a pattern confirmed as taught. If none of the passage words demonstrates the pattern selected for instruction, substitute a familiar, teacher-confirmed example and record the adaptation.

**Observe:** Accurate recognition; ability to compare words; sound/letter awareness appropriate to prior teaching; use of a model to check; and accessible communication of the learner's thinking.

## Pathway-specific teaching support
- **Copper:** Use two-word comparisons, matching, concrete letter tiles and adult modelling. Reduce the number of choices and repeat the task with generous wait time.
- **Bronze:** Provide a word bank, visual cue and one model; then invite the learner to sort or build a similar example.
- **Silver:** Invite independent grouping, reading in context and a brief explanation of the shared feature.
- **Gold:** Invite comparison, justification or careful transfer to a second teacher-confirmed taught pattern. Do not require untaught spelling terminology.

## Marking principles
1. Credit correct equivalent responses and accessible response modes.
2. Do not require a formal label unless the class has learned it.
3. Check that the selected pattern has actually been taught.
4. Record independent, prompted and modelled evidence separately.
5. Do not use a weekly score to permanently assign or rank a learner's pathway.
6. If a word or pattern is unfamiliar or untaught, adapt the task rather than penalising the learner.
