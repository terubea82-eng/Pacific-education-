# PacEdu Bloom's Taxonomy Worksheet and Question Design Standard

**Status:** staging teaching-and-quality standard. This does not create or approve curriculum indicators. Each worksheet must first be mapped to a verified prescribed concept or indicator.

## Non-negotiable learning chain

Official or authorised prescription -> concept/indicator -> unpacked knowledge, skill and attitude -> measurable learning objective -> suitable text, demonstration or data -> questions/tasks -> learner evidence -> feedback and next step.

A question may assess more than one thinking process. Bloom labels guide task design; they are not a rigid ladder, a substitute for curriculum alignment, or a reason to make every question difficult.

## Six thinking dimensions

1. **Remember:** retrieve, name, label, list, identify, match, recall.
2. **Understand:** explain, summarise, classify, compare, give examples, interpret.
3. **Apply:** use a method, calculate, follow a procedure, demonstrate, solve a familiar practical task.
4. **Analyse:** distinguish evidence, identify patterns, infer causes, compare sources, organise information, detect errors.
5. **Evaluate:** justify a choice, assess evidence, critique a method, rank options against stated criteria, defend a conclusion.
6. **Create:** design, plan, compose, construct, propose, investigate, produce an original response.

## Question and task families

- Text comprehension: literal retrieval, vocabulary in context, sequencing, inference, main idea, evidence citation, author's purpose and perspective.
- Mathematical reasoning: representation, estimation, strategy selection, multi-step problem, error analysis, explanation and creation of a new problem.
- Scientific inquiry: observation, variables, fair test, prediction, data table/graph, interpretation, limitations and evidence-based conclusion.
- Social inquiry: source interpretation, timeline, map/diagram, cause and consequence, perspective comparison, evidence-supported judgement and proposal.
- Language production: oral explanation, dialogue, summary, paragraph, letter, report, story, composition, editing and audience/purpose choices.
- Practical/creative performance: demonstration, model, labelled diagram, design, role-play, project, portfolio or performance rubric.

## Worksheet structure

Every complete learner worksheet should include:
- Country/jurisdiction, exact source curriculum level, class label mapping if verified, subject, term/week/day, indicator ID, source version/page and review status.
- One clear objective written in learner-friendly language and one success criterion.
- An accessible stimulus: original passage, teacher-approved excerpt with rights, image/diagram, local data, scenario or practical demonstration. Record source and licence; do not reproduce protected text without permission.
- A worked example or modelled thinking step where appropriate.
- A balanced sequence of questions/tasks across the thinking dimensions that are relevant to the indicator.
- Clear response spaces and accessible alternatives: text, drawing, manipulatives, signed/teacher-authorised voice response or practical demonstration.
- Student copy without answer key or private identifiers; separate teacher guide with answer rationale, worked solution, marking criteria, common misconceptions and acceptable equivalent wording.
- Audio/listen control for each reading or activity, visible text/transcript, stop/replay and accessible navigation. Do not make audio the only presentation.
- Feedback and next-step logic. Auto-mark only objective items with verified keys; subjective, oral, practical and creative work requires teacher review.

## Cognitive progression and differentiation

Copper, Bronze, Silver and Gold use the same approved indicator and core learning objective. Differentiate scaffolding, language load, number of steps, representation, prompts, independence and extension—not the prescribed learning objective.

- Copper: concrete objects, pictures, worked models, short instructions, vocabulary support, guided practice and accessible response choices.
- Bronze: partially guided examples, familiar contexts, structured explanations and supported transfer.
- Silver: independent application, multi-step reasoning, comparison of strategies and explanation with evidence.
- Gold: unfamiliar but age-appropriate contexts, evaluate competing methods, justify decisions, design an investigation or create a solution.

Do not equate a learner's group with fixed ability or identity. Placement must use repeated evidence, accessibility context and teacher review, and learners must be able to move between pathways.

## Question-bank quality rules

- Use A/B/C/D only for multiple-choice items; exactly four plausible, non-duplicative options, one defensible correct answer unless explicitly labelled multi-select (which should not be used in this pilot).
- Avoid trick wording, culturally narrow assumptions, unnecessary reading complexity and distractors that disclose stereotypes.
- Include an answer rationale and evidence source for every keyed objective item.
- Accept correct equivalent words and valid alternative methods in teacher marking guides.
- Check every factual text, current-affairs example, statistic, map and image for date, source, local relevance and rights. If not verified, use a clearly labelled fictional or timeless scenario.
- Use original passages where possible. Record licence and attribution for all third-party materials.
- Never let AI invent an indicator, mark an unverified fact as current, or auto-release unreviewed learner content.

## Assessment safeguards

Assessment should be formative and developmental where required by the applicable Fiji policy and curriculum level. Do not rank learners or create selection decisions where the applicable policy prohibits it. The Fiji Ministry's Policy in National Curriculum Assessment and Reporting states that assessment from pre-school to Class 2, and in primary and Forms 3–4, supports learning/development rather than selection/ranking; confirm current policy and amendments before applying to a live cohort.

## Release acceptance tests

A worksheet is not complete until:
1. Its indicator and source are verified for the exact jurisdiction, level and subject.
2. Its objective and all questions align with that indicator.
3. Bloom thinking tags and question types are reviewed by an educator.
4. All four ability variants retain the same objective and indicator.
5. Worked examples, answer guide, marking criteria and misconception notes are present.
6. Text, audio, transcripts, response alternatives and navigation pass accessibility checks.
7. Copyright, factual accuracy, age suitability and child-safety checks pass.
8. Student and teacher files are separated correctly.
9. Save/resume, offline retry and duplicate-submit tests pass.
10. Teacher approval and trusted server release checks pass.

## Pilot delivery order

Complete the source-backed indicator inventory and exact 115-day distribution by class/level and subject before filling the worksheets. A blank day remains blocked; do not invent a filler indicator. Complete and validate the worksheets before integrating the separate Global Library.
