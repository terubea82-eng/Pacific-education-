# Class 4 English — four ability worksheets

Status: original pilot material; verify the current Fiji Class/Year 4 English syllabus outcome before release. Flexible bands: Support 0–49%, Developing 50–64%, Expected 65–79%, Advanced 80–100%.

## A. Support (0–49%) — Literal comprehension

Read: “Ravi and his grandmother visited the market. They bought taro, tomatoes, and fish. Ravi carried the bag while his grandmother paid the seller.”

**Example:** Who visited the market? Ravi and his grandmother.

1. Where did they go?
2. Name two things they bought.
3. Who carried the bag?
4. Who paid the seller?
5. Write one sentence about a market.

**Teacher key (8 marks):** market (1); any two listed items (2); Ravi (1); grandmother (1); relevant sentence with capital/full stop (up to 3).

## B. Developing (50–64%) — Sequence and main idea

Read: “The school garden looked dry after a week without rain. The gardening group loosened the soil, added compost, and watered the seedlings carefully. They covered the soil with dry leaves to slow water loss. The teacher asked them to record plant growth every Friday.”

1. What made the garden look dry?
2. List two actions the group took.
3. Why might dry leaves help the soil?
4. What did the teacher ask the group to record?
5. Write a suitable main idea in one sentence.
6. Write a four-step instruction for caring for a seedling.

**Teacher key (10 marks):** week without rain (1); any two correct actions (2); helps reduce water loss (2); plant growth (1); accurate main idea (2); logical steps (2).

## C. Expected (65–79%) — Fact, inference and summary

Read: “A community team planned a clean-up beside a stream. Before beginning, they checked the area for sharp objects and asked an adult to handle unsafe waste. They separated reusable items from rubbish and recorded the number of bags collected. The team planned to return a month later to see whether litter had returned.”

1. State two actions that made the clean-up safer.
2. Why did the team count the bags?
3. What can be inferred about the team's future plan?
4. Summarise the passage in two or three sentences.
5. Identify one detail that is a fact and one reasonable inference.

**Teacher key (12 marks):** checked for sharp objects and adult handled unsafe waste (3); measure amount/compare later (2); wants to monitor whether litter returns (2); accurate summary (3); valid fact/inference distinction (2).

## D. Advanced (80–100%) — Evaluate and write

A school newsletter says: “Our clean-up day solved the litter problem.” The report lists 12 bags collected on the first day but gives no later observations.

1. What evidence supports the claim that the team removed litter?
2. Why is “solved the litter problem” too strong a claim?
3. Give two pieces of evidence needed to test the claim.
4. Rewrite the statement so it is accurate and cautious.
5. Write a balanced newsletter paragraph (6–8 sentences) explaining the clean-up's achievement and what should happen next.

**Teacher key (12 marks):** 12 bags collected (2); no follow-up evidence (2); later counts and comparable locations/times (3); cautious rewrite (2); balanced paragraph with evidence and next step (3).

## Access and release
Supply screen-reader-friendly text, large print or tactile/Braille-ready materials as appropriate. Keep core information written; use captions and clear visuals as optional supports. Accept equivalent communication modes where they assess the same English outcome. Retain answers in teacher view. Verify syllabus outcome before release.
