## Common marking principles
Assess comparative reasoning, evidence precision, uncertainty, method awareness, source limitations and cohesion. Accept accessible oral, signed, AAC, dictated, Braille or written responses. Do not score accent, disability-related communication differences, handwriting speed or device access as errors. Record response mode, independence and prompts. Keep PENDING_TEACHER_REVIEW.

## Day 26 — Oral communication
- Weather summary records variation at three stations and cautions against treating short-term variation as proof of a long-term trend.
- Preparedness note records local impacts but lacks a systematic rainfall dataset.
- Credit recommendations combining monitoring with local impact reports; local accounts can identify impacts and questions even when they are not rainfall measurements.
- Next step: distinguish measurement, lived impact and policy relevance.

## Day 27 — Close reading
- Editorial uses an extended metaphor and future-focused question to create urgency.
- Technical note specifies four sites and two periods and states that the data cannot identify one cause.
- Credit analysis showing urgency and uncertainty can coexist when claims are accurately framed.

## Day 28 — Vocabulary and language
- “Front line” frames exposure as urgent; “takes everything” is absolute and emotive.
- “May influence” signals possibility; “does not quantify” identifies a measurement gap.
- Credit a revision calling for action to assess and reduce coastal risk while acknowledging that impacts differ by location and require further evidence.

## Day 29 — Critical literacy
- Proposal predicts reduced overtopping under selected model conditions but omits full beach-movement assessment.
- Users' submission offers six accounts and requests review but lacks coastal measurements and broad representation.
- Further evidence: model assumptions and validation; beach/sediment movement assessment; landing/access mapping; independent coastal review; maintenance and monitoring costs; wider consultation.

## Day 30 — Writing and shaping
- Planning brief offers possible protection benefits but lacks a full beach-effects assessment and long-term maintenance budget.
- Community note identifies access, landing-place and cost concerns but does not represent every household or provide technical measurements.
- A balanced recommendation may support further design and interim safety measures while withholding final approval until critical gaps are addressed.
- Credit a fair counterargument that delay may leave the road exposed, answered with proportionate interim measures and a clear evidence timetable.

## Pathway evidence
- Copper: identifies accurate details from both sources with labels, model and recorded prompts.
- Bronze: organises comparison using a frame and reduces prompts.
- Silver: independently integrates evidence and evaluates uncertainty.
- Gold: develops nuanced methodological critique and policy qualification.
