# TEACHER ONLY — Class 1 English Week 14 Marking Guide (Copper)
**Days:** 66–70 | **Focus:** Shared reading — sequence and retell
**Pathway:** Copper | **Do not place this guide in the learner download area.**

## Intended learning
Learner sequences the main events of a familiar story and communicates a retell using first, next and last. The four pathways support access to the same objective; they are not permanent ability labels.

## Passage used in the matching learner worksheet
Mere sees a ball. She puts it by a tree. She goes to class.

## Expected sequence
1. Mere has / arrives with the ball.
2. Mere places the ball beside the tree.
3. Mere goes to class; in the return detail, she comes back and collects the ball (where included).

Accept an equivalent accurate retell. If a learner identifies the key order accurately by pointing, picture selection, sign, AAC, drawing or dictation, credit the comprehension objective. Do not penalise accent, speech difference, handwriting or spelling unless that feature is the stated assessment target.

## Pathway-specific evidence
- With adult modelling and a small choice set, learner identifies/matches at least one event and participates in ordering the story with support. Record the support and response mode.

## Day-by-day marking notes
- **Day 66:** Note whether learner identifies the beginning event.
- **Day 67:** Record use of the worked example and level of prompting.
- **Day 68:** Check ordering of all three events; note any reversal or confusion.
- **Day 69:** Check whether the learner transfers the sequence into a retell.
- **Day 70:** Select one sample for the portfolio and record the next teaching step.

## Marking rubric
| Criterion | Secure | Developing with support | Not yet observed |
|---|---|---|---|
| Identifies story events | Identifies the key events | Identifies with choices/model | No clear evidence yet |
| Orders events | Correct beginning, middle and end | Partial order with prompts | Order not yet demonstrated |
| Retells meaning | Communicates events in sequence | Communicates some events | Retell not yet observed |
| Uses sequence language | Uses/indicates first, next, last | Uses with cue/model | Not yet observed |

**Equivalent responses:** Credit “at the start / after that / at the end”, meaningful synonyms, picture sequence, signed or AAC sequence, or a clear dictated retell. Do not require exact wording from the sample.
