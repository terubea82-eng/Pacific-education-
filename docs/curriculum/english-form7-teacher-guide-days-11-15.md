# Form 7 English — Week 3 Teacher Marking Guide (Days 11–15)

**Status:** PENDING_TEACHER_REVIEW and PENDING_OFFICIAL_INDICATOR_VERIFICATION. Original practice sources only; not prescribed texts, Ministry materials or past examination questions.

## Common marking principles
- Assess critical synthesis, evidence precision, qualification, source evaluation, sustained cohesion and audience awareness.
- Accept well-supported alternative interpretations and accessible oral, signed, AAC, dictated, Braille or written responses. Do not score accent, disability-related communication differences, handwriting speed or device access as errors.
- Record actual evidence, response mode, independence and prompts. Keep PENDING_TEACHER_REVIEW until reviewed by an authorised educator.
- Copper, Bronze, Silver and Gold are flexible scaffolds, not permanent ability labels.

## Day 11 — Oral communication
**Expected:** sustained recommendation, evidence from both sources, distinction between observation and inference, qualification and response to livelihood concerns.
- Source A gives community testimony about mangrove use, family learning and perceived wave effects; it also reports local pressures.
- Source B provides a six-month volunteer comparison of young shoots at selected sites but does not measure wave height or cover all seasons.
- A sound recommendation may propose targeted access controls only with consultation, alternative access arrangements and monitoring. Credit other justified positions.
- The survey does not prove the cause of the difference. A strong response acknowledges that restrictions could affect families who rely on the bay and proposes fair alternatives.
- Next step: distinguish evidence, inference, limitation and policy choice.

## Day 12 — Close reading
**Expected:** comparative analysis of voice, imagery, register, structure and omission.
- Source A uses first-person memory, sensory details and the contrast between open reef and fence to connect access with belonging.
- Source B uses administrative, future-oriented language and measurable planning items. It omits current local use and customary access arrangements.
- Credit analysis that recognises selectivity without asserting that the notice is false.
- Next step: explain how form and omission shape what a reader notices.

## Day 13 — Vocabulary and language
**Expected:** analyse connotation, register, audience positioning and produce an evidence-calibrated rewrite.
- “Living shoreline” gives the coast an animate, valuable quality.
- “Before it is too late” creates urgency but does not specify a measurable deadline.
- “Selected sites” limits the scope of the technical observation.
- “Do not establish” explicitly signals that evidence is insufficient for a causal conclusion.
- A technical rewrite should specify the observed vegetation loss at three areas, identify the need for repeat measurements and avoid claiming an unproven cause or deadline.
- Next step: calibrate certainty and urgency to the strength of evidence.

## Day 14 — Critical literacy
**Expected:** evaluate interests, source authority, evidence, omissions, prediction versus established fact and further evidence.
- Developer statement identifies proposed benefits and design features but provides no full drainage design or employment estimate.
- Residents' note records concerns and requests but has no hydrological measurements and does not represent every resident.
- Established: the developer says channels and planting are included in the proposal; residents have recorded concerns. Not established: actual runoff effects or the number of jobs.
- Further evidence may include the full drainage/hydrology assessment, employment estimates and conditions, maps of affected gardens/access, consultation records and broader resident views.
- Next step: build a claim/source/evidence/interest/omission/verification matrix.

## Day 15 — Writing and shaping
**Expected:** sustained recommendation, thesis, synthesis of both sources, counterargument, limitations and evidence-based conditions.
- Source A records uses and concerns raised at a meeting but notes that not every household attended and no agreement was reached.
- Source B identifies proposed access route, planting buffer and monitoring but leaves costings, drainage assessment and construction access schedule incomplete.
- A balanced recommendation may support conditional progress only after drainage, cost, access and consultation conditions are addressed. Other positions are creditable if well supported.
- Strong conditions may include publishing technical assessments, maintaining safe community access, monitoring vegetation, setting review triggers and providing a transparent consultation response.
- Next step: review thesis alignment, source integration, counterargument and conclusion.

## Pathway evidence
- Copper: completes the same analytical move with source labels, model, gloss and one-step prompts; record support.
- Bronze: organises synthesis with a comparison grid and planning frame; reduce prompts progressively.
- Silver: independently integrates precise evidence and explains limitations.
- Gold: qualifies conclusions, evaluates competing interests and develops nuanced ethical implications without changing the shared objective.
