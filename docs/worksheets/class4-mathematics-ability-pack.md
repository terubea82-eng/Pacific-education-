# Class 4 Mathematics — four ability worksheets

Status: original pilot material; verify the current Fiji Class/Year 4 Mathematics syllabus and taught content before release. Flexible bands: Support 0–49%, Developing 50–64%, Expected 65–79%, Advanced 80–100%.

## A. Support (0–49%) — Place value and operations

1. Write 527 in hundreds, tens and ones.
2. 245 + 132 = ___
3. 600 − 250 = ___
4. 7 × 8 = ___
5. 36 ÷ 4 = ___
6. A basket holds 24 oranges. Another holds 15. How many altogether?

**Teacher key (10 marks):** 5 hundreds, 2 tens, 7 ones (2); 377 (2); 350 (2); 56 (1); 9 (1); 39 (2).

## B. Developing (50–64%) — Fractions and measurement

**Example:** One half of 12 is 6 because 12 ÷ 2 = 6.

1. Find one quarter of 20.
2. Which is larger: 3/4 or 1/4?
3. Convert 2 m to centimetres.
4. A water container holds 5 L. How many litres are in 3 full containers?
5. A rectangle is 9 cm long and 4 cm wide. Find its perimeter.
6. A market seller has $50 and spends $18.75. How much remains?

**Teacher key (10 marks):** 5 (1); 3/4 (1); 200 cm (2); 15 L (2); 26 cm (2); $31.25 (2).

## C. Expected (65–79%) — Fractions, data and problems

1. 3/8 + 2/8 = ___
2. Find 3/4 of 28.
3. A class recorded these books read: Monday 12, Tuesday 9, Wednesday 15. How many books were read altogether?
4. How many more books were read Wednesday than Tuesday?
5. A rectangular field is 12 m long and 7 m wide. Find its area.
6. Explain how you know the answer to Question 5 uses area rather than perimeter.

**Teacher key (12 marks):** 5/8 (2); 21 (2); 36 (2); 6 (2); 84 m² (2); area counts square units covering the inside (2).

## D. Advanced (80–100%) — Multi-step problem solving

A school plans a rectangular vegetable garden measuring 15 m by 8 m.

1. Find the perimeter.
2. Find the area.
3. Fencing costs $6 per metre. What is the total fencing cost if the whole perimeter is fenced?
4. The school uses 1/4 of the garden for herbs. What area is used for herbs?
5. A learner says the area is 46 m² because 15 + 8 + 15 + 8 = 46. Explain the mistake.
6. Write a different real-life problem that uses area and money, then solve it.

**Teacher key (12 marks):** perimeter 46 m (2); area 120 m² (2); $276 (2); 30 m² (2); added boundary lengths rather than multiplying length by width (2); valid problem with correct working (2).

Offer tactile shapes, number lines, large print and screen-reader-friendly equations as needed. Read word problems when reading is not assessed. Keep the answer key teacher-only and verify the current syllabus and taught content before release.
