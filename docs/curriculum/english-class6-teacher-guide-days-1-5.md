## Day 1 — Listening and speaking: A Community Emergency Plan
**Core message:** know the agreed emergency plan and follow safe official instructions. Evidence includes learning the safe route, keeping contact details accessible, following official warnings and never crossing floodwater or approaching fallen power lines.
**Marking (5):** 1 accurate summary; 1 each for two safe actions; 1 relevant clarifying question; 1 coherent explanation. Accept paraphrase and all accessible communication modes.
**Pathways:** Copper selects the message and matches an action; Bronze uses the frame; Silver summarises and asks a question; Gold prioritises and justifies actions.

## Day 2 — Reading and viewing: Protecting a Coastal Wetland
**Core idea:** wetlands provide habitat and can slow water movement; monitoring informs careful protection. Evidence: bird sightings recorded over four visits; rubbish photographed in two areas; cleared vegetation noted. The survey does not prove one activity caused every change.
**Marking (5):** 1 central claim; 1 each for two accurate evidence points; 1 supported conclusion; 1 correct distinction between recorded evidence and opinion.
**Pathways:** Copper finds one fact; Bronze uses the evidence frame; Silver gives two details and a conclusion; Gold evaluates evidence limits and identifies further information needed.

## Day 3 — Vocabulary and language: Explaining a New Invention
**Core answers:** prototype = early model for testing; unstable = not steady; reliable = dependable; limitations = things not yet possible or problems remaining.
**Marking (6):** 1 each for four contextual explanations; 1 precise revision/synonym; 1 justified word choice. Accept equivalent accurate wording.
**Pathways:** Copper matches simple meanings; Bronze completes frames; Silver explains and revises; Gold compares near-synonyms and connotations.

## Day 4 — Grammar and sentence craft: Writing a Balanced View
**Example:** “Although plastic items can be useful because they are light and durable, single-use items can add to waste.” Accept other grammatically accurate complex/combined sentences that show contrast or cause.
**Marking (5):** 1 accurate combination; 1 suitable linking expression; 1 clear contrast/cause relationship; 1 sentence boundaries/punctuation; 1 coherent paragraph/revision.
**Pathways:** Copper combines one pair with support; Bronze combines two pairs; Silver writes a balanced paragraph; Gold revises for nuance and explains how links guide the reader.

## Day 5 — Writing and shaping: An Evidence-Based Recommendation
**Marking (6):** 1 clear position/recommendation; 1 each for two relevant reasons; 1 realistic limitation; 1 practical next step; 1 audience awareness/evidence or review plan. Gold should acknowledge a counterpoint and propose impact review.
**Pathways:** Copper states an improvement and reason; Bronze uses a planning frame; Silver drafts a structured recommendation; Gold develops an evidence-aware recommendation for decision-makers.

## Assessment and access rules
- Accept Braille, large print, screen-reader text, audio directions, sign, AAC, pointing, eye gaze, object choice, dictation, typing, scribing or handwriting.
- Allow extra processing time, sensory breaks and adapted tools. Do not infer ability from disability, speech, accent, handwriting or device access.
- Record observed evidence, response mode, prompt level and next teaching step.
- Copper/Bronze/Silver/Gold are flexible evidence-based support pathways, not permanent labels.
- Accept equivalent wording and valid alternative responses.
