# Form 3 English — Week 1 Teacher Marking Guide (Days 1–5)

**Status:** PENDING_TEACHER_REVIEW and PENDING_OFFICIAL_INDICATOR_VERIFICATION. PacEdu working guide only. The original practice stimuli below are not prescribed Ministry texts or examination questions.

## Common marking principles
- Assess interpretation, evidence, explanation, audience and purpose; do not score accent, speech differences, disability, handwriting speed or device access as errors.
- Accept equivalent wording, well-supported alternative interpretations and accessible oral, signed, AAC, dictated, Braille or written responses.
- Record evidence, response mode, independence, prompting and next teaching step. Keep outcomes PENDING_TEACHER_REVIEW.
- Copper/Bronze/Silver/Gold are flexible scaffolds, not permanent learner labels.

## Day 1 — Oral communication
**Expected:** clear position, two relevant details, explanation and response to the counter-question.
- Possible position: schools should provide important information in more than one language when translations are checked for accuracy.
- Evidence: families may recognise their experiences; fluent speakers can check translations; families can review important notices; an English version can remain alongside translations.
- Counter-question response should explain a practical accuracy check, such as review by fluent speakers, confirmation by the school or family review.
- Credit a different defensible position if supported by the stimulus and reasoning.
- Next step: rehearse claim → evidence → explanation → response to a question.

## Day 2 — Close reading
**Expected:** defensible analytical claim, two precise details and explanation of imagery or implied meaning.
- Possible claim: the meeting house functions as a shared community space.
- Details: neighbours gather for fishing, school and clinic news; messages appear in different languages; some visitors read messages aloud.
- The image of the building remaining the same while voices become more varied suggests that a community can change through participation without losing its shared place.
- Accept other interpretations when supported by two details.
- Next step: practise quotation/detail → inference → explanation.

## Day 3 — Vocabulary and language
**Expected:** explain connotation, figurative meaning, register and audience effect.
- vibrant: energetic, lively, colourful or full of activity; positive connotation.
- make room for every story: figurative invitation to include different experiences; encourages without forcing anyone to speak.
- Register: encouraging and inclusive. Audience: students, families and staff.
- Formal alternative may be: “The event will include contributions from participants with varied language backgrounds.” Credit other suitable formal versions and a reasoned explanation of the changed register.
- Next step: compare informal, inclusive and formal wording for the same audience/purpose.

## Day 4 — Critical literacy
**Expected:** compare perspective, purpose, evidence and reliability.
- Message A is more reliable on the available evidence because it is signed by the principal, identifies a specific time and venue, and directs readers to the office for confirmation.
- Message B is unattributed and makes unsupported claims about free transport and prizes. The school office has not confirmed those claims.
- Careful questions include: Who wrote this? Has the school confirmed the transport/prizes? Where is the official programme? Is the time current?
- Do not reward merely choosing A; require evidence-based justification.
- Next step: use a source/evidence/claim/reliability comparison grid.

## Day 5 — Writing and shaping
**Expected:** structured recommendation with clear position, at least two details, explanation and purposeful conclusion.
- Possible recommendation: make contributions voluntary and offer multiple formats.
- Evidence: story cards, book recommendations, recorded greetings with permission, drawings and chosen word explanations.
- Explanation: options allow participation without pressure to disclose private information; contributor checks help accuracy.
- Conclusion should reinforce inclusion, consent and checking.
- Accept different structures if coherent and audience-appropriate.
- Next step: plan audience and purpose, select evidence, draft, then revise for clarity and cohesion.

## Pathway evidence
- Copper: supported analytical move using a model, gloss or structured choice; record prompts.
- Bronze: organised response with planning frame and reduced prompts.
- Silver: independent evidence selection and explanation.
- Gold: nuanced evaluation, qualification or counterargument while retaining the shared objective.
