## Learning intention
Learners notice vocabulary, use context clues, connect words with meanings, select precise vocabulary and use a word meaningfully. Accept familiar language and accessible communication modes. Do not require a dictionary skill or formal terminology unless already taught.

## Complete passage used in the learner worksheet
Lani walks to the garden. The soil is soft. She plants a seed. She gives it water. The seed is tiny.

**Word bank:** garden · soil · soft · plants · seed · water · tiny

## Worked example used
Read the sentence: “The seed is tiny.” The word “tiny” tells us the seed is very small. Match “tiny” to a small seed picture or show its meaning.

## Pathway-specific teaching support
- **Copper:** Use pictures, real objects, gestures, repeated oral reading and a small choice set. Model one word at a time; allow matching, pointing or tactile exploration.
- **Bronze:** Provide a word bank and one visual cue. Read the surrounding sentence together, then invite the learner to select a clue.
- **Silver:** Invite independent use of sentence clues and a brief explanation of the word's meaning.
- **Gold:** Invite inference from more than one clue, comparison of near-synonyms or an original context sentence. Keep the vocabulary and text appropriate to taught learning.

## Marking principles
1. Credit equivalent meanings and sensible examples, not only the exact wording in this guide.
2. Credit speech, sign, AAC, pointing, matching, drawing, dictation or writing where it demonstrates understanding.
3. Do not penalise a learner for using a home-language explanation to show understanding; record the response and support English vocabulary development.
4. Distinguish independent evidence from prompted or modelled responses.
5. Do not use a weekly score to permanently assign or rank a learner's pathway.
6. If a target word is unfamiliar for reasons unrelated to the learning intention, explain it and choose a more suitable example.
