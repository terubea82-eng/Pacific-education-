# PacEdu teacher workspace: subject-box learning sequence and Year 1 cross-subject starter

**Status:** Content and workspace integration specification on the proposal branch. Not merged and not live in the app. Teacher review required. Formal outcome mapping remains `outcome-mapping-pending` wherever an exact current official syllabus outcome/indicator and page cannot be verified.

## Non-negotiable workspace design

- Each class/level has its own workspace and only the subjects confirmed for that class, school and curriculum appear.
- Each subject is a single subject box (for example, English, Mathematics, Science). Opening it shows one continuous date-ordered sequence: daily learning, practice, revision, checkpoint assessment, term review and exam preparation. Do not create separate global pages for activities, revision or exams.
- Every item is a record in that subject sequence with school-local date, learning-day number, term/week, objective, official outcome reference and source version, teacher instructions, student task, four ability adaptations, answer/marking guide, evidence alternatives, review status and printable/downloadable output.
- A school calendar controls dates and teaching days. Do not assume all schools have identical term lengths, holidays or exam dates. If the calendar is not set, show “School calendar needed”; never fabricate dates.
- Student activity is released only after teacher approval. Keep student identity IDs protected; do not place FEMIS IDs in public worksheet filenames or URLs.
- A diagnostic result informs support but does not permanently label a child. Review group placement using multiple samples and teacher judgement. No invented percentage cut-offs.
- Offer writing, oral response, accessible text, offline/paper and other reasonable evidence options; camera or voice is not the only route.
- Use locally meaningful examples without requiring internet, personal data, family income disclosure, photos of children or expensive materials.
- The same engine can support other countries, but each country must have its own official curriculum registry, syllabus versions, language/context, calendar, assessment rules and review workflow. Never reuse Fiji outcome codes for another country.

## Curriculum verification record

### Verified context, not a complete subject-by-subject indicator map
- Fiji's National Information Centre for Higher Education describes the curriculum framework as using six Foundation Areas of Learning and Development for ECE and seven Key Learning Areas for primary/secondary. It describes Years 1–8 as primary and Years 9–13 as secondary: https://nic.hec.org.fj/school-education
- The Ministry's Na Noda Mataniciva ECE guidance says ECE outcomes are to be contextualised to local conditions and resources, and connects foundation areas to later learning areas: https://www.education.gov.fj/wp-content/uploads/2022/06/Na-Noda-Mataniciva.pdf
- A publicly available Year 1 Language, Literacy and Communication resource identifies the Ministry's Curriculum Development Unit, 2013, revised 2019 and reprinted 2020; it includes units such as “Who am I?”, “My Friends” and “My School”: https://fliphtml5.com/gjgbb/miwv/Year_1_English_-_Language%2C_Literacy_and_Communication/
- Fiji's 7 October 2025 Cabinet decisions endorsed review of the 2013 Fiji National Curriculum Framework. PacEdu must therefore store the syllabus/framework version and school adoption status instead of assuming one version is universal: https://www.fiji.gov.fj/14th-cabinet-meeting-decisions-7-october-2025/
- Financial education resources describe its integration into related primary subjects including Maths, English and Social Studies. Verify the current applicable teacher guide before asserting a specific indicator: https://www.nfitfiji.com/fin-edu-for-schools/fiji-financial-education-project/

## Four ability pathways (used within every subject box)

1. **Supported Start** — concrete example, modelled steps, fewer items, oral/visual/manipulative choices and scaffolding.
2. **Developing** — guided practice, prompts and gradually reduced support.
3. **Working at Class Goal** — independent task at the selected class objective.
4. **Extension** — deeper reasoning, explanation, application to a new situation, comparison or design challenge.

These are teaching pathways, not permanent student labels. Do not set percentage thresholds until a validated, approved assessment rule exists for the relevant class and subject.

## Year 1 cross-subject 10-day starter sequence

**Use:** content draft for teacher review, not an official syllabus replacement. The ten-day order is a flexible sequence; assign dates from the school's calendar. Exact achievement-indicator IDs remain `outcome-mapping-pending` until checked against the school's current official syllabus. Every task must be placed inside its own subject box. Revision/checkpoint work is part of the same dated sequence.

### English / Language, Literacy and Communication box
| Learning day | Daily learning and student task | Embedded check / revision |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Introduce myself: say or write “I am …”; draw a self-portrait without required identifying details; name a favourite colour or safe classroom object | Match a spoken sentence to a picture; teacher notes oral/written starting point |
| 2 | Hear and identify the beginning sounds for letters selected by the teacher from the adopted sequence (starter examples: s, a, t, i) | Point, say, trace or select matching sound/picture |
| 3 | Listen to a short teacher-read description of a friend or fictional character; identify one detail | Answer who/what question by speech, pointing or writing |
| 4 | Name and describe familiar school objects; use “This is a …” | Picture-word match and one independent sentence |
| 5 | Put three familiar daily routine pictures in order; use first/next/last | Sequence cards and explain one choice |
| 6 | Read/listen to a short rainy-day passage; identify weather words and safe actions | Circle/point to two words; state one safe action |
| 7 | Read/listen to an original short passage about helping at school; answer who/where/what | Three comprehension questions; teacher records evidence |
| 8 | Write, dictate or arrange words for a helpful classroom message | Check meaning, spacing/word order at the learner's stage |
| 9 | Practise respectful words for cooperation and asking for help | Role-play or choose an appropriate response |
| 10 | Review familiar sounds, words, listening and sentence-making | Short teacher checkpoint with feedback; not a high-stakes exam |

### Mathematics box
| Learning day | Daily learning and student task | Embedded check / revision |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Count real or drawn classroom objects; connect quantity to a numeral within the teacher-confirmed range | Count again using objects, pictures or verbal number sequence |
| 2 | Match numerals to groups of objects; compare more, fewer and same | Explain or show which group has more |
| 3 | Sort safe classroom objects/pictures by one feature such as shape or colour | State or show the sorting rule |
| 4 | Continue a simple repeating pattern using shapes, sounds or movements | Choose what comes next and explain |
| 5 | Compare lengths using informal units such as paper clips or hand spans, without unsafe objects | Identify longer/shorter and explain method |
| 6 | Explore joining two small groups of objects as an introduction to addition, using class-taught number range | Show the total with objects, drawings or number sentence |
| 7 | Explore taking away objects from a group as an introduction to subtraction, using class-taught number range | Model the change and state what remains |
| 8 | Use a simple classroom or market picture to identify quantities and compare prices only if numbers are age-appropriate; do not ask children to reveal family finances | Explain a choice using the displayed information |
| 9 | Solve a practical class problem: share a set of materials fairly and check the result | Demonstrate a fair share; discuss any remainder if appropriate |
| 10 | Review counting, matching, comparing and patterns | Short mixed checkpoint; teacher notes which concept needs reteaching |

### Science / science exploration box
| Learning day | Daily learning and student task | Embedded check / revision |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Use senses to observe a safe classroom object; describe visible features without tasting unknown items | Sort observation from guess |
| 2 | Compare living and non-living examples using teacher-provided pictures and safe observations | Explain one feature used to group them |
| 3 | Observe a plant in the school/community; draw or describe its visible parts without damaging it | Label parts the class has learned |
| 4 | Investigate what plants need using pictures or teacher-supervised observation; do not claim a result without evidence | Predict, observe and record |
| 5 | Observe weather using the sky or a simple teacher-provided record; do not go outdoors in dangerous weather | Match weather to suitable safe clothing/activity |
| 6 | Compare materials by a safe property such as hard/soft or rough/smooth; do not taste or smell unknown substances | Sort and describe evidence |
| 7 | Explore movement by rolling or sliding safe objects on a flat surface | Predict and test one change; explain what happened |
| 8 | Discuss clean water and handwashing with a picture sequence; no unsafe water tasting | Order steps and identify a hygiene reason |
| 9 | Notice a local environmental issue such as litter in a picture or supervised safe location; never handle hazardous waste | Suggest one safe action and who can help |
| 10 | Review observation, grouping, prediction and evidence | Practical/oral/picture checkpoint with feedback |

### Social Studies / community learning box
| Learning day | Daily learning and student task | Embedded check / revision |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Identify roles in a classroom community; use fictional names in examples | Match a role to a helpful action |
| 2 | Make a simple picture map of a familiar, safe place such as the classroom | Locate a named object using the map |
| 3 | Discuss ways people help in a community, including different jobs and contributions | Explain one role respectfully |
| 4 | Compare a past and present object or routine using a family-safe fictional example; no child must disclose private family history | State one similarity and one difference |
| 5 | Practise safe ways to ask for help from a trusted adult at school | Select an appropriate response to a scenario |
| 6 | Explore how people care for shared places such as a classroom, village or settlement | Choose one action and explain why it helps |
| 7 | Notice that Fiji includes diverse communities, languages and traditions; avoid treating any group as all the same | Describe one respectful way to learn about differences |
| 8 | Use a pretend classroom shop to practise fair exchange and simple choice without asking about household money | Explain how to take turns and make a choice |
| 9 | Work together to solve a simple shared-resource problem | Teacher observes listening, fairness and cooperation |
| 10 | Review roles, maps, safety, care and respect | Scenario-based checkpoint; feedback and next steps |

| Learning day | Daily learning and student task | Embedded check / revision |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Identify feelings using faces, words or colour cards; learners may pass | Choose a safe way to ask for help |
| 2 | Sequence handwashing steps using pictures | Demonstrate or order the steps |
| 3 | Discuss everyday movement and rest through a movement game | Name or show one way to care for the body |
| 4 | Sort everyday food pictures into a balanced variety discussion without shaming food access or family choices | Explain that different foods provide different nourishment |
| 5 | Practise road and vehicle passenger safety using drawings or role-play away from traffic | Identify one safe and one unsafe action |
| 6 | Identify safe adults and how to seek help | Choose what to do in a fictional scenario |
| 7 | Discuss respectful boundaries: children can say no to unwanted touch and tell a trusted adult | Rehearse a help-seeking phrase; no disclosure required |
| 8 | Practise calm breathing or a quiet reset as an optional strategy | Choose a strategy that feels comfortable |
| 9 | Identify a safe way to keep a shared play area clean | Explain one reason for the action |
| 10 | Review hygiene, movement, safety and help-seeking | Private, supportive teacher check; do not solicit sensitive disclosures |

### Arts / creative expression box (if offered/mapped in the school's curriculum)
| Learning day | Daily learning and student task | Embedded check / revision |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Make a self-expression drawing with paper and available materials | Learner describes a choice if willing |
| 2 | Explore line and shape by drawing objects seen at school | Identify two lines or shapes |
| 3 | Create a rhythm with claps or safe classroom sounds | Repeat or create a short pattern |
| 4 | Respond to a story through drawing, movement or role-play | Connect one creative choice to the story |
| 5 | Observe colours and patterns in the local environment without picking or damaging plants | Recreate a pattern using available materials |
| 6 | Make a simple reusable-material design with clean, safe materials | Explain what was reused |
| 7 | Work in pairs to create a picture or rhythm, with an option to work alone | Practise giving respectful feedback |
| 8 | Make a visual sequence for a safe routine | Put the sequence in order |
| 9 | Improve a previous work after feedback | Name one change made |
| 10 | Review selected work and explain the process, not just appearance | Portfolio checkpoint; assess effort and choices fairly |

### Physical education / movement box (if offered/mapped in the school's curriculum)
| Learning day | Daily learning and student task | Embedded check / revision |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Practise safe personal space and stop/start signals in an open safe area | Respond to signal reliably |
| 2 | Explore walking, hopping or balancing within individual ability | Choose a safe movement level |
| 3 | Roll or pass a soft ball to a partner, or use a seated alternative | Observe control and turn-taking |
| 4 | Follow a short movement sequence | Recall sequence using movement or pictures |
| 5 | Play a cooperative game with no elimination | Describe or show fair play |
| 6 | Practise safe warm-up and cool-down movements | Identify why we start gently and rest |
| 7 | Move to a rhythm, with seated/low-mobility alternatives | Match a movement to a beat or cue |
| 8 | Navigate simple cones/markers at walking pace; never race into obstacles | Follow a safe route |
| 9 | Adapt a game so everyone can participate | Explain one inclusive change |
| 10 | Review safety, coordination and cooperation | Formative observation; no comparison based on disability or resources |

### Languages, technology and other subjects
Add a subject box only after confirming the school offers it and the applicable official syllabus for that class. For Vosa Vakaviti, Hindi, iTaukei language, other language programmes, technology/TVET, agriculture, commerce, accounting, economics, computer studies and senior electives, author separate class-specific sequences. Do not automatically assign every subject to every class. Translate and localise examples with qualified language/curriculum review.

## Revision, checkpoint and examination logic inside each subject
- Daily learning days include a small retrieval/revision item from previously taught content.
- At teacher-configured intervals, add a subject-specific checkpoint in the same subject box, linked to the outcomes already taught.
- Before a school or national examination, the subject box sequences revision by outcome/concept, practice questions, worked examples/marking criteria, and a mock paper only where appropriate to the actual level and official format.
- Do not label a generated paper as a national examination paper or reproduce protected exam content. Clearly label original PacEdu practice papers.
- Every assessment needs answer guidance, marks/criteria, accessibility alternatives, and an outcome map. Do not use one test as the sole basis for a learner's permanent ability group.
- Teacher reviews and approves the item before it appears to students. Store version, reviewer, approval timestamp and change reason.

## Acceptance checks before merge
1. Subject boxes show only subjects valid for the selected class, country, school and current adopted syllabus.
2. Daily activities, revision, checkpoints and exam preparation remain inside the same subject box and sort by school-local date.
3. No scheduled date is invented when the school calendar is missing.
4. Formal-alignment status is blocked unless exact official source/version/outcome/page are present and reviewed.
5. Every subject activity has all four ability pathways, marking guidance and accessible evidence options.
6. Student release is blocked until teacher approval.
7. Cross-country tests prove that curriculum IDs, terms, dates, language and exam formats do not leak between countries.
8. No changes overwrite existing working workspace behaviour; integrate incrementally behind tests.
