# TEACHER ONLY — Class 1 English Week 17 Marking Guide (Gold)

**Describing words and expanded noun groups**  
**Programme days:** 81–85 | **Term:** 2 | **Week:** 8  
**Pathway:** Gold | **Do not place this answer guide in the learner download.**

## Learning intention
Learner identifies or uses describing words with nouns to add relevant detail. All pathways address the same intention; supports and complexity vary.

## Passage used in the matching learner worksheet
> At the school garden, Jone notices a narrow wooden bench beside a tall hibiscus plant. A small brown bird hops onto the rough path and pauses near a fallen red flower. Jone describes what he sees in his notebook so that a classmate who was absent can imagine the scene. Carefully chosen describing words make the information clearer.

## Worked-example rationale
Noun group: “a small brown bird”. Naming word: bird. Describing words: small and brown. The expanded group gives the reader two details about the bird.

## Day-by-day marking guidance

| 81 | Notices or identifies a naming word and a describing word | Accept pointing, matching, selecting, signing, AAC, drawing, dictation or writing. Assess the language concept, not handwriting. |

| 83 | Builds an expanded noun group with a relevant describing word | Accept any sensible adjective that matches the object. More than one answer may be correct. |
| 84 | Applies describing words to an object, phrase or sentence | Credit relevant details and clear meaning; do not require one exact phrase. |

## Pathway-specific scaffolding and expected evidence

- **Copper:** Use real objects, tactile/large-print or picture cards, two-choice matching and adult modelling. Expected evidence may be matching one noun with a describing word, such as “green leaf”. Do not require independent writing.
- **Bronze:** Provide a visual cue and small word bank. Model one noun + adjective combination, then reduce prompts. Expected evidence: a relevant expanded noun group and a simple oral, signed, AAC, drawn or written response.
- **Silver:** Invite independent identification and use of describing words. Expected evidence: identifies noun and adjective in examples and creates a relevant expanded noun group or sentence.
- **Gold:** Invite multiple details, comparison and precise word choice. Expected evidence: uses several relevant expanded noun groups in connected description and explains how a word changes the reader's picture. Accept varied accurate wording.

## Indicative responses — not an exclusive answer key

- Examples: narrow wooden bench; tall hibiscus plant; small brown bird; rough path; fallen red flower. Credit other contextually sensible alternatives and reasoned word substitutions.

## Marking principles

- A noun names; a describing word adds information. Credit accurate understanding even if the learner does not use technical grammar terms.
- More than one describing word may fit a noun. Do not restrict marking to the examples above.
- A describing word must make sense in context; encourage precise, respectful descriptions.
- Assess the stated language skill, not spelling, handwriting, speech clarity, accent, eye contact or speed unless explicitly being assessed.
- Accept oral, sign, AAC, pointing, matching, drawing, dictated or written responses. Record response mode and access support.
- No fixed numerical pass threshold is assigned in this draft. Teacher records: secure / developing / revisit, with evidence and next step.
