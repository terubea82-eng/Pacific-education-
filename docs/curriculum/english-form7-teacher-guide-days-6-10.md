## Common marking principles
Assess interpretation, synthesis, source evaluation and limitations. Accept accessible oral, signed, AAC, dictated, Braille or written responses. Do not penalise accent, disability-related communication differences, handwriting speed or device access. Record evidence, response mode, independence and prompts. Keep PENDING_TEACHER_REVIEW.

## Day 6 — Oral communication
- Main claim may be that the market was a social space as well as a trading place.
- Evidence: news travelled between stalls; families exchanged more than goods; the speaker acknowledges memory may change.
- A strong response values oral testimony while recognising that dates/events should be checked where possible.
- Answer to challenge: documentary checking can add context without erasing the elder's lived memory; each source answers different questions.

## Day 7 — Close reading
- Central idea: photographs preserve appearance at a moment, while stories can carry names, knowledge and relationships over time.
- Evidence: the aunt names river bends and plants; photographs do not record names or pauses in explanation.
- The contrast develops a view that place is understood through both physical features and shared knowledge.
- Accept other readings when supported by precise detail.

## Day 8 — Vocabulary and language
- fragmented: incomplete, in parts, with gaps; acknowledges limits in memory.
- vivid: clear, lively or strongly sensory; suggests detail and emotional force.
- “Meeting place disguised as a row of stalls” is a metaphor that reframes the market as social rather than merely commercial.
- Cautious language recognises the value of testimony without claiming that every date/detail is independently proven.

## Day 9 — Critical literacy
- Oral interview provides memory, relationships and informal exchange; it may have uncertain dates.
- Photograph supports visible layout/location on its dated occasion; it cannot prove conversations or feelings.
- Ledger supports recorded stall fees and activity; it omits informal gathering and relationships.
- Corroboration may include the market's location and physical arrangement; social exchange remains testimony unless supported by other records.
- Strong conclusion uses sources together and avoids claiming that one source disproves another outside its scope.

## Day 10 — Writing and shaping
- Thesis: combined sources provide a fuller account because each contributes different evidence.
- Interview: memory and relationships; photograph: physical layout on a date; ledger: dated activity/fees.
- Limitations: none is complete; undocumented experiences may remain absent.
- Credit a coherent synthesis that integrates at least two source types, evaluates limits and concludes carefully.

## Pathway evidence
- Copper: supported source identification and modelled claim/detail.
- Bronze: organised source comparison using frame.
- Silver: independent synthesis and source limitation.
- Gold: nuanced synthesis and methodological evaluation.
