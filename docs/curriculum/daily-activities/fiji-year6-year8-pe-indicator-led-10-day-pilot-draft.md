## 2. Daily activity sequence

Each session is a proposed 45-minute slot. The guide's 10-period allocation is respected as a planning ceiling for this draft; the sequence is not a new Ministry-prescribed day-by-day timetable. A school must confirm the actual Term 3 calendar and whether this post-examination guide is appropriate for its cohort.

### Year 6 — Physical Education

#### Day 1 — Fitness and training methods
- **Prescription anchor:** PE 6.1.1.1a — examine and demonstrate how fitness relates to training methods.
- **Learning intention:** Explain that a training method should match the fitness goal and demonstrate one safe example.
- **Teach/model:** Model a simple link: brisk walking or steady jogging develops aerobic endurance; short controlled strength exercises develop muscular strength. Explain that a warm-up and suitable rest are part of safe training.
- **Worked example:** Goal = improve ability to keep moving during a game. Choose a steady, moderate-intensity walk/jog interval; explain why it matches the goal.
- **Guided activity:** Learners match teacher-approved fitness goals to safe training examples and explain one match.
- **Independent evidence:** Complete a three-column record: fitness goal / suitable method / reason. Demonstrate one low-risk movement.
- **Success check:** Learner names a fitness goal, selects a relevant method and gives a sensible reason.
- **Copper:** Match picture cards and demonstrate with support.
- **Bronze:** Choose between two methods and complete a sentence frame.
- **Silver:** Select a method and explain the fitness connection.
- **Gold:** Compare two methods and justify the best match for a stated goal.
- **Safety/access:** Adapt movements to ability; no forced exertion or public comparison. Offer seated or low-impact alternatives.

#### Day 2 — Aerobic and anaerobic training
- **Prescription anchor:** PE 6.1.2.2a — demonstrate aerobic and anaerobic training approaches.
- **Learning intention:** Distinguish sustained moderate activity from short, higher-effort bursts and demonstrate safe examples.
- **Teach/model:** Explain the difference using age-appropriate language; do not ask learners to exercise maximally.
- **Worked example:** A steady 4-minute brisk walk is a sustained aerobic example; a short 5-second quick-feet burst followed by full recovery is a brief high-effort example.
- **Guided activity:** Teacher leads a low-impact circuit with choice of walking, arm actions, step-taps or seated alternatives. Learners identify which stations are sustained and which are brief bursts.
- **Independent evidence:** Sort four activity cards into aerobic / short-burst examples and explain one choice.
- **Success check:** Learner demonstrates or identifies one safe example of each approach.
- **Copper:** Sort two illustrated examples with a partner.
- **Bronze:** Sort four examples using word/picture prompts.
- **Silver:** Demonstrate two examples and explain the difference.
- **Gold:** Design a short, safe sequence containing both types and explain the recovery breaks.
- **Safety/access:** Teacher checks health/safety arrangements before activity; learners may opt for an equivalent low-impact or seated movement.

#### Day 3 — How training affects fitness
- **Prescription anchor:** PE 6.1.2.2b — describe and demonstrate how training affects fitness.
- **Learning intention:** Describe one possible fitness effect of regular, appropriate practice.
- **Teach/model:** Use a fictional learner's practice log; explain that improvement varies and requires suitable practice, rest and safe progression.
- **Worked example:** “After several weeks of regular brisk walking, the learner may be able to walk for longer before needing a rest.” Avoid promising a fixed result.
- **Guided activity:** Compare a fictional before/after activity log; identify what changed and what evidence supports the claim.
- **Independent evidence:** Write, say, sign or draw one cause-and-effect statement about training and fitness.
- **Success check:** Learner describes a plausible effect and links it to an appropriate training habit.
- **Copper:** Choose an effect from picture cards.
- **Bronze:** Complete “Regular practice can help me…”.
- **Silver:** Explain one effect using evidence from the sample log.
- **Gold:** Explain why rest, consistency and gradual progression matter when interpreting the log.
- **Safety/access:** Do not weigh, rank or publicly compare learners' bodies or fitness. Use fictional records for analysis.

#### Day 4 — Compare training methods
- **Prescription anchor:** PE 6.1.2.2c — analyse how different training methods develop fitness.
- **Learning intention:** Compare two training methods and identify the fitness area each may develop.
- **Teach/model:** Model a simple comparison of steady walking and controlled body-weight movements, linking each to an appropriate fitness area without claiming one method is universally best.
- **Worked example:** “Steady walking mainly practises endurance; controlled chair-squats practise lower-body muscular strength. The choice depends on the goal.”
- **Guided activity:** Small groups compare teacher-approved method cards against a goal card.
- **Independent evidence:** Complete a comparison table with method, fitness focus and reason.
- **Success check:** Learner compares at least two methods and selects one that fits a stated goal.
- **Copper:** Match one goal to one method.
- **Bronze:** Compare two methods using a prompt table.
- **Silver:** Explain similarities and differences.
- **Gold:** Justify a method choice and identify one limitation or safety consideration.
- **Safety/access:** Demonstrations remain controlled and supervised; no maximal testing or unsafe competition.

#### Day 5 — Basic first-aid awareness for sports injuries
- **Prescription anchor:** PE 6.1.2.2d — explore and demonstrate basic first-aid responses to sports injuries.
- **Learning intention:** Recognise when to stop activity, protect the area from further harm and seek a responsible adult/qualified help.
- **Teach/model:** Use teacher-approved, age-appropriate scenarios. Teach: stop play; do not move a seriously injured person unless there is immediate danger; alert the teacher/first aider; follow the school's emergency plan. Serious injury, suspected fracture/dislocation, head injury or concussion symptoms require urgent adult/medical assessment.
- **Worked example:** A learner falls and reports severe pain. The correct student response is to stop play, alert the teacher/first aider, keep others clear and follow adult instructions—not attempt to diagnose or manipulate the injury.
- **Guided activity:** Learners sequence response cards for a minor scrape scenario and a serious-injury scenario.
- **Independent evidence:** Role-play how to summon help and explain why play must stop.
- **Success check:** Learner identifies the need to stop, tell an adult and avoid unsafe handling.
- **Copper:** Point to the “stop” and “tell an adult” cards.
- **Bronze:** Put three safe response cards in order.
- **Silver:** Explain the safe response for two scenarios.
- **Gold:** Compare minor and serious scenarios and justify when urgent help is required.
- **Safety/access:** This is awareness education, not first-aid certification. Only a qualified/authorised adult demonstrates any hands-on procedure. No practice of treating fractures, dislocations, concussion or serious wounds on classmates.

### Year 8 — Physical Education

#### Day 6 — Demonstrate skills used in different sports
- **Prescription anchor:** PE 8.1.1.1a — study and demonstrate skills in different sports.
- **Learning intention:** Demonstrate selected fundamental skills safely and identify the sport context in which they are used.
- **Teach/model:** Teacher models a safe, controlled pass, catch, kick or run-start using suitable equipment and space.
- **Worked example:** Demonstrate a two-hand chest pass to a partner at a safe distance; show ready hands, controlled push and follow-through.
- **Guided activity:** Skill stations with teacher-approved options: passing/receiving, catching, controlled kicking, running or jumping technique.
- **Independent evidence:** Learner chooses two skills, demonstrates or describes them and identifies a sport where each is used.
- **Success check:** Safe participation and an identifiable skill demonstration or equivalent accessible explanation.
- **Copper:** Demonstrate one movement with a model and support.
- **Bronze:** Practise one skill with prompts.
- **Silver:** Demonstrate two skills with control.
- **Gold:** Demonstrate two skills and explain how technique changes with the sport/task.
- **Safety/access:** Use soft/appropriate equipment, clear zones and non-contact alternatives; adapt for mobility and sensory needs.

#### Day 7 — Similarities and differences between sports skills
- **Prescription anchor:** PE 8.1.2.2a — investigate skills that are similar and different across sports.
- **Learning intention:** Identify shared skills and explain one way the skill changes between sports.
- **Teach/model:** Compare running in two sports or passing in two sports; identify the shared movement and different purpose/rules.
- **Worked example:** Running occurs in many sports, but the direction, speed, timing and decision may differ according to the game.
- **Guided activity:** Groups use a Venn diagram or tactile/large-print comparison cards to compare two teacher-selected sports.
- **Independent evidence:** Complete “Both sports use… / In sport A… / In sport B…” with one accurate example.
- **Success check:** Learner identifies one shared skill and one meaningful difference.
- **Copper:** Match identical skill pictures across two sports.
- **Bronze:** Identify one shared skill using prompts.
- **Silver:** Complete a two-sport comparison.
- **Gold:** Explain how context, rules or position changes the use of a shared skill.
- **Safety/access:** Use inclusive examples; no learner is excluded for not being able to perform a particular movement.

#### Day 8 — Learn and execute sport skills
- **Prescription anchor:** PE 8.1.2.2b — describe and participate in learning and performing sports skills.
- **Learning intention:** Participate in a skill-learning process and describe what helped improve performance.
- **Teach/model:** Demonstrate a short cycle: watch/model → practise → receive respectful feedback → adjust → try again.
- **Worked example:** For a gentle underarm pass, observe the model, practise at a suitable distance, receive one specific cue, then repeat.
- **Guided activity:** Learners rotate through one or two skill stations and use a simple observation cue card.
- **Independent evidence:** Demonstrate or explain one adjustment made after feedback.
- **Success check:** Learner participates in the learning cycle and identifies a useful cue or adjustment.
- **Copper:** Follow one visual/tactile cue with assistance.
- **Bronze:** Practise with one verbal or visual reminder.
- **Silver:** Apply feedback and describe the change.
- **Gold:** Give respectful, specific feedback and explain why the adjustment supports the skill.
- **Safety/access:** Feedback addresses the task, not the learner's body or personal worth. Offer non-performing roles such as observer, caller or equipment organiser.

### Days 9–10 — Consolidation and evidence (Year 6 / Year 8 separately)
- **Prescription anchors:** Revisit only the relevant indicators listed above for the learner's year level; do not mix Year 6 and Year 8 indicator codes.
- **Day 9:** Learners revisit two selected skills/concepts through teacher-led stations and explain the indicator connection.
- **Day 10:** Learners complete a short practical demonstration plus an accessible oral, signed, drawn or written reflection. Teacher records evidence against each indicator actually taught.
- **Success check:** Evidence is recorded indicator-by-indicator; absence of evidence is marked “not yet observed”, not an assumed failure.
- **Differentiation:** Copper/Bronze/Silver/Gold alter prompts, complexity, practice support and response mode only. The prescribed learning objective remains unchanged.
