# Form 4 English — Week 1 Teacher Marking Guide (Days 1–5)

**Status:** PENDING_TEACHER_REVIEW and PENDING_OFFICIAL_INDICATOR_VERIFICATION. PacEdu working guide only. The original practice stimuli are not prescribed Ministry texts or examination questions.

## Common marking principles
- Assess developed reasoning, relevant evidence, explanation, qualification and audience awareness. Do not score accent, disability-related speech differences, handwriting speed or device access as errors.
- Accept well-supported alternative interpretations, equivalent wording and accessible oral, signed, AAC, dictated, Braille or written responses.
- Record evidence, response mode, independence and prompting. Keep all outcomes PENDING_TEACHER_REVIEW.
- Pathways are flexible scaffolds, not permanent ability labels.

## Day 1 — Oral communication
**Expected:** coherent argument, two relevant details, developed explanation and reasoned response to the limited-staff counterargument.
- Defensible position: prioritise translation of high-impact public notices, with accuracy safeguards.
- Evidence: families can understand school/health information; inaccurate safety instructions can mislead; fluent-speaker review and community feedback are proposed.
- Qualification: limited staff may require prioritisation by urgency and impact.
- Credit a different position if it is coherent and supported by the passage.
- Next step: rehearse claim → evidence → explanation → qualification → conclusion.

## Day 2 — Close reading
**Expected:** developed interpretation, precise details, analysis of contrast/structure and consideration of an alternative reading.
- The phrase “A better coast for all” sounds inclusive, but the text questions whether all affected groups have been heard.
- Evidence: the notice lists visitor benefits but says little about nearby families; the resident's fishing-access question remains unanswered; the design is still under review.
- The final contrast between promise and unanswered question creates doubt and invites scrutiny.
- Alternative readings are acceptable if supported by precise details.
- Next step: explain how a selected structural or language choice guides a reader's response.

## Day 3 — Vocabulary and language
**Expected:** analyse connotation, register, vagueness and audience effect; justify a revision.
- “Transformative” implies major positive change but is not supported by a cost comparison in the brochure.
- “Stakeholders” is formal and may make individual residents sound like categories in a project list.
- “The community will benefit” is vague because it does not specify which people benefit or how.
- Balanced revision may name the expected benefit, identify who experiences it and acknowledge unresolved concerns.
- Next step: replace vague claims with specific, evidenced wording.

## Day 4 — Critical literacy
**Expected:** compare claims and source quality, identify omission, distinguish confirmed facts from unverified claims and qualify a conclusion.
- Source A is more accountable: it identifies the project office and says the design is under review.
- Source A omits the completed environmental assessment.
- Source B is anonymous and offers no supporting document, date or named source; its absolute claim is not verified.
- The meeting record confirms that fishing access was raised and design review continues. It does not prove that access will be removed.
- Strong conclusion: the effect on fishing access remains unresolved and should be checked against the final design and assessment.
- Next step: use claim/source/evidence/omission/limit as a repeatable evaluation frame.

## Day 5 — Writing and shaping
**Expected:** developed multi-paragraph response with thesis, organised evidence, reasoning, qualification/counterargument and reasoned conclusion.
- A balanced position may support the project conditionally, oppose it pending evidence or recommend redesign/consultation.
- Relevant benefits: pedestrian safety, easier market access, public space.
- Relevant concerns: fishing access, privacy, shoreline effects and absent environmental assessment.
- Counterargument should fairly represent the other view; conclusion should identify what evidence and consultation are needed before a decision.
- Credit coherent alternative positions supported by the stimulus.
- Next step: plan paragraph sequence and check each paragraph contributes to the thesis.

## Pathway evidence
- Copper: supported claim and evidence with model/frame; record prompts.
- Bronze: organised argument using an evidence locator and paragraph frame.
- Silver: independent developed response with precise evidence.
- Gold: nuanced qualification, synthesis or counterargument while retaining the same objective.
