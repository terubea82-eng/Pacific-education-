# TEACHER ONLY — Class 1 English Week 21 Marking Guide (Silver)

**Writing: Instructions and procedural language**  
**Programme days:** 101–105 | **Term:** 3 | **Week:** 3  
**Pathway:** Silver | **Keep this guide separate from the learner worksheet.**

## Learning intention
Learner understands and creates simple ordered instructions, using action verbs and sequence language such as first/next/last. The same objective applies to all four pathways; scaffolding and independence vary.

## Complete procedure in the matching learner worksheet

**How to wash your hands**

1. Turn on the clean running water.
2. Wet both hands.
3. Use soap and rub palms, backs of hands and between fingers.
4. Rinse all the soap away.
5. Turn off the tap and dry your hands with a clean towel.

**Action-word examples:** turn on, wet, use, rub, rinse, turn off, dry.

**Worked example:** First, turn on the water and wet your hands. Next, use soap and rub all parts of your hands. Then, rinse away the soap. Finally, turn off the tap and dry your hands.

## Day-by-day evidence and marking guidance

| Day | Evidence | Teacher guidance |
|---|---|---|
| 101 | Listens to/reads the procedure and notices an action or sequence | Accept pointing, matching, gesture, sign, AAC, speech, drawing or dictated response. Record whether learner identifies an action and/or first step. |
| 102 | Follows a model and identifies order words or completes an ordered example | Use real objects, picture cards or a teacher demonstration. Repeat directions and allow ample wait time. |
| 103 | Practises sequencing and communicating steps with a partner/adult | Credit a sensible sequence shown with cards, objects, speech, sign, AAC, dictation or writing. |
| 104 | Applies the same procedural skill to a safe familiar routine | Ensure routine is age-appropriate and safe. Do not use electrical equipment, sharp tools or cleaning chemicals. |
| 105 | Shares a short set of ordered instructions and reflects | Record action verbs, sequence words, logical order and level of support. This is formative evidence, not a permanent ability label. |

## Pathway-specific scaffolding and expected evidence

- **Copper:** Present three large-print/tactile picture or object cues and model each step. Accept selecting, ordering, gesture, sign, AAC or dictated words. Expected evidence: communicates or selects a three-step order with adult support.
- **Bronze:** Provide four picture/word cards and a small action-word bank; model first/next/last and reduce prompts. Expected evidence: orders a familiar procedure and uses at least one action or sequence word.
- **Silver:** Invite independent ordering of five steps and a short written/dictated procedure. Expected evidence: uses action verbs and sequence words to communicate a logical sequence.
- **Gold:** Invite a complete numbered procedure, materials list where useful, and explanation of why the order matters. Expected evidence: independently creates clear, complete steps with precise action verbs and logical sequencing.

## Indicative responses and marking principles

- The correct handwashing sequence is the numbered sequence printed in the learner worksheet. Accept equivalent wording that preserves the safe and logical order.
- Action verbs include the words in the worksheet's action-word list. Also accept suitable alternatives such as “wash”, “soap”, “clean” or “pat dry” when used accurately in a step.
- Sequence words include “first”, “next”, “then”, “after that”, “finally” and “last”. Accept other clear time/order phrases.
- Credit meaningful instructions even when spelling or punctuation is developing. Do not require the exact model sentence.
- Accept sign, AAC, pointing, picture/word-card ordering, drawing with explanation, dictation or writing. Do not make handwriting or speech the only evidence.
- Do not penalise accent, speech clarity, eye contact, handwriting or speed unless explicitly the teaching target.
- No fixed numerical pass threshold or permanent ability placement is assigned. Record: secure / developing / revisit, with evidence and next step.
