Assess comparison, synthesis, evidence quality and source limitations. Accept accessible oral, signed, AAC, dictated, Braille or written responses. ## Day 6 — Oral communication
- Comparative claim: the sources show both organised market activity and its social role.
- Ledger provides dated evidence of stall names, fees and market days; oral account provides relationships and exchange of news.
- Neither source captures the whole market experience. A reasoned judgement distinguishes the question being asked: the ledger is stronger for dated transactions; oral testimony is stronger for lived relationships.

## Day 7 — Close reading
- Both narrators treat the meeting house as a place of memory.
- Text A uses sound and personification (“a witness that never speaks”) to foreground lived experience.
- Text B uses notices/minutes/names to foreground documentary memory, while admitting that records omit feelings.
- Strong comparison explains how each perspective includes and excludes different evidence.

## Day 8 — Vocabulary and language
- “Living records” suggests active memory and retelling.
- “Fixed traces” suggests documents preserve selected details and dates.
- The contrast supports comparison rather than an automatic ranking of source types.
- Measured register suits the purpose because it discourages overclaiming.

## Day 9 — Critical literacy
- Minutes provide dated evidence of formal meetings and decisions.
- Interview provides personal experience and informal support but uncertain dates.
- Undated photograph shows chairs in a circle but cannot establish event/date/purpose.
- Further evidence: photographer/source, date, caption, archive catalogue, people identified or a matching meeting record.
- A strong response distinguishes what is corroborated from what remains interpretation.

## Day 10 — Writing and shaping
- Thesis: community memory is best understood by comparing oral, written and visual evidence.
- Oral accounts contribute relationships/emotions; records contribute dates/decisions; photographs contribute visible details.
- Limits: memory may change; records omit informal experience; photographs without context can be ambiguous.
- Credit an integrated essay that evaluates at least two source types and reaches a reasoned conclusion.

## Pathway evidence
Copper: supported source identification; Bronze: guided comparison; Silver: independent evidence integration; Gold: evaluation of missing perspectives and qualified judgement.
