### Read the full passage

**Designing a useful book-box label**

Class 3 wants a label for a shared book box. The label must fit the front of the box, show the words “Class 3 Books” clearly and stay attached during normal classroom use. The learners have clean thin card, a ruler, a pencil, blunt-ended scissors and a glue stick.

Before making the label, the group identifies what success will look like: the label fits the available space, the writing is readable, the edges are neat and the label is attached securely. The group measures the box, chooses a suitable label size and marks the card with a ruler and pencil. They check the measurements before cutting. They use blunt-ended scissors with teacher supervision and cut slowly away from their bodies. They keep fingers away from the blades and pass closed scissors handle first. They do not use damaged tools or card with sharp edges. They stop and tell the teacher if they find a hazard.

After cutting, the group places the label against the box without gluing it. They check the fit and readability, make a safe adjustment if needed, then use a glue stick to attach it. Finally, they check whether the label is straight and secure.

### Worked example

**Question:** Why should the group test the label against the box before gluing it?  
**Model answer:** Testing first shows whether the size and position are correct. If it does not fit, the group can safely adjust it before attaching it permanently.  
**Success criterion:** The label fits the space and the words are readable.
