## Common marking principles
Assess thesis, evidence, explanation and source limitations. Accept accessible oral, signed, AAC, dictated, Braille or written responses. Do not penalise accent, disability-related communication differences, handwriting speed or device access. Record response mode, independence and prompts. Keep PENDING_TEACHER_REVIEW.

## Day 6 — Oral communication
- Thesis: oral history explains the social meaning of the landing place; records may help verify chronology.
- Evidence: warnings and shared net repair; uncertainty about the jetty's construction year.
- Uncertain dates do not make the whole account worthless; different sources answer different questions.

## Day 7 — Close reading
- The path symbolises practical connection and shared memory.
- Details include links to the landing place, school and meeting tree; stories attached to particular turns; safer surface but less visible landmarks after widening.
- Credit balanced analysis of development's benefits and cultural concerns.

## Day 8 — Vocabulary and language
- “A thread through the community's memory” suggests one account connects events and people.
- “Partial but valuable” recognises limits without dismissing testimony.
- Measured, respectful register suits a discussion of personal memory and evidence.

## Day 9 — Critical literacy
- Works record can support construction year and contractor, not social uses.
- Oral interview supports remembered uses and shared work, with uncertain date.
- Photograph shows boats but has no date/photographer, limiting chronology and context.
- Strong conclusion distinguishes corroborated chronology from social experience.

## Day 10 — Writing and shaping
- Thesis: a fuller account combines oral, written and visual sources.
- Oral testimony adds relationships and practical knowledge; records add chronology/official decisions; photographs add visible detail.
- Limits: uncertain memory, omissions in records and missing image context.
- Credit a coherent analytical essay with at least two source types and a reasoned conclusion.
