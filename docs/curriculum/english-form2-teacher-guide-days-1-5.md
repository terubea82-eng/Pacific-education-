## Shared passage — “The Notice on the Board”
The passage describes a community water-supply meeting notice with a date, venue, organiser and contact details. A second online copy omits the date and adds an unverified claim. Learners compare the versions and consider why readers should check complete information before acting.

## Day 1 — Central idea and supporting details
**Learning goal:** identify the central idea and select accurate supporting details.
- Central idea: readers should check community information carefully and use the complete, reliable notice before acting.
- Supporting details: the original notice includes date, venue, organiser and contact details; the online copy omits the date; the online copy adds an unverified claim.
- Credit a one-sentence summary that captures the need to check complete and reliable information.
- Accept central message, main point, key message or equivalent wording for “central idea”.
- Next step: distinguish the main message from a small detail using a shorter notice.

## Day 2 — Vocabulary and word choice
**Learning goal:** infer a word's meaning from context and explain the writer's choice.
- reliable: trustworthy, dependable, able to be believed or checked.
- omission: something left out or missing.
- verify: check, confirm or establish whether information is true.
- Credit a new sentence that uses the selected word accurately and explains why the word fits.
- Accept synonyms that preserve the context; do not insist on dictionary wording.
- Next step: use the surrounding sentence and whole passage to test an inferred meaning.

## Day 3 — Text structure, fact and inference
**Learning goal:** analyse organisation and distinguish stated fact from inference.
- Stated facts include that the notice gives a date, venue, organiser and contact details, and that the online copy leaves out the date.
- An inference may be that the second version is less useful or less trustworthy because key information is missing and a claim is unverified. It must be linked to clues in the passage.
- Credit recognition of a comparison structure, contrast between versions or problem-and-check sequence where explained with evidence.
- Further information could include the source/author of the online copy, confirmation from the organiser, the correct date, or evidence for the added claim.
- Next step: label fact, clue and inference separately.

## Day 4 — Audience, purpose and coherent response
**Learning goal:** create a coherent response using details and language suited to an audience and purpose.
- Possible audience: community members, families, students or people deciding whether to attend.
- Possible purpose: warn readers to check a notice, explain which version is complete, or provide a clear correction.
- Relevant details: missing date, unverified claim, and the complete notice's organiser/contact details.
- Credit a clear structure with an appropriate opening, supporting details and a useful closing or action.
- Accept different genres if the audience and purpose are clear and evidence is accurate.
- Next step: plan audience and purpose before drafting.

## Day 5 — Review, revision and transfer
**Learning goal:** review accuracy, cohesion and purpose, then apply learning to a new situation.
- Strengths may include the complete date, venue, organiser and contact information in the first notice.
- A weakness in the online copy is the missing date and unverified claim.
- A strong revision adds or confirms the missing date, identifies the source and removes or verifies unsupported claims.
- Transfer examples may include a school event notice, sports meeting, transport update, health announcement or community clean-up notice.
- Credit an explanation of how the revision makes the message clearer, more accurate or more useful.
- Next step: reread once for meaning, once for organisation and once for accuracy.

## Pathway-specific marking support
- **Copper:** accept identification from guided choices, pointing or a sentence frame; record what support was required.
- **Bronze:** credit a response completed with the worked model or planning frame and fewer prompts after the first step.
- **Silver:** expect independent selection of relevant evidence and a clear explanation.
- **Gold:** expect precise evidence, evaluation of reliability or a justified alternative interpretation while retaining the same learning goal.
