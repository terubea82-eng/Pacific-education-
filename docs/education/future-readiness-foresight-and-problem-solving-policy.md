# Future Readiness, Foresight and Practical Problem-Solving Policy

Status: proposed product policy for review in PR #306. This document does not itself activate features in the live app.

## 1. Purpose

PacEdu should progressively teach learners to recognise present-day signals, investigate causes, forecast plausible future challenges, prepare for uncertainty, and develop, test, evaluate and improve safe solutions. The pathway applies across health, environmental protection, climate and disasters, food and water security, education, accessibility, transport, energy, technology, employment, community wellbeing and other relevant local or global issues.

Learners should become evidence-informed problem-solvers and responsible innovators, not be taught that uncertain forecasts are guaranteed outcomes or that a single solution works everywhere.

## 2. Mandatory curriculum alignment

Every formal activity, worksheet, project, practical task, revision question, mock examination or examination item in this pathway must be linked to the verified curriculum applicable to the learner's country or jurisdiction, class/year/form, subject and effective curriculum version.

For each learning item, store:
- country/jurisdiction, class/year/form, subject and curriculum version/effective date;
- authoritative syllabus/framework source URL, document title/version/date and relevant page/section;
- exact achievement indicator, learning outcome, concept or skill ID and wording when available;
- a measurable student learning objective;
- the evidence, forecasting, design or problem-solving steps that teach the outcome;
- assessment task, answer guidance/rubric and success criteria that measure the same outcome;
- age/ability adaptations, materials, accessibility requirements, safety controls and supervision;
- learner evidence, review status, reviewer/date and version history.

Verify exact indicators against authoritative sources. Do not invent indicator IDs, quote unofficial text as official, or transfer an outcome from one country or curriculum version to another. Where mapping cannot be verified, label the item `outcome-mapping-pending`; it may be offered only as a clearly labelled supplemental exploration subject to teacher review, and must not be represented as formally curriculum-aligned or as a replacement for required curriculum content. Integrated projects must map each subject's contribution separately where applicable.

## 3. Learner foresight cycle

1. **Observe:** Identify a present condition or change using safe observation, approved class surveys, reliable local reporting, official statistics, scientific information or historical records.
2. **Ask and define:** State the problem, who or what may be affected, where it occurs, the time period and why it matters. Distinguish a measured problem from a hypothesis.
3. **Check evidence:** Record sources and dates; compare credible sources; identify data gaps, bias, uncertainty, units and differences in definitions.
4. **Find patterns and causes:** Use age-appropriate tables, graphs, maps, scientific explanations and reasoning. Distinguish correlation from causation and symptoms from possible contributing causes.
5. **Forecast multiple scenarios:** Describe a plausible baseline, a better outcome and a worse outcome, where useful. State assumptions, timescale, confidence/uncertainty and what new evidence would change the forecast. Use simple trend extrapolation only when justified; never present it as certainty.
6. **Consider consequences:** Identify possible impacts on people, ecosystems, services, costs, fairness and different groups, including unintended consequences.
7. **Design options:** Generate multiple locally suitable options, including prevention, preparedness, adaptation, education, community action and technology. Consider cost, resources, maintenance, accessibility, culture, environment, permissions and who benefits or bears risks.
8. **Select and plan:** Justify a preferred option using evidence and clear criteria. Define responsibilities, materials, timeline, partners, permissions, safeguards, a baseline and measurable success indicators.
9. **Prototype or act safely:** Use simulations, models, desk research, supervised demonstrations or authorised community projects appropriate to age and risk. Do not conduct unauthorised human/animal experiments or hazardous fieldwork.
10. **Evaluate and improve:** Compare results with baseline and success criteria, record failures and limitations, seek feedback, revise the solution and retest where safe.
11. **Communicate responsibly:** Present evidence, sources, uncertainty, costs, risks, outcomes and limitations without exaggerating impact or exposing private information.
12. **Monitor and revisit:** Update the evidence and forecast when conditions change; preserve a versioned record of what changed and why.

## 4. Issue coverage and examples

The issue bank may include, where relevant and evidence-supported:
- health and disease prevention, health-information access and safe health technology;
- climate change, flooding, drought, cyclones, coastal change and disaster readiness;
- water quality, sanitation, waste, pollution, biodiversity, soil and ocean health;
- food security, farming, fisheries, nutrition, food waste and changing prices;
- literacy, numeracy, attendance, inclusive education and access to learning;
- digital safety, scams, misinformation, privacy, cybersecurity and AI literacy;
- energy, transport, infrastructure, connectivity and accessibility;
- employment, vocational skills, household costs, entrepreneurship and changing work;
- public services, community safety, culture, language, inclusion and resilience;
- new or emerging issues supported by credible, dated evidence.

This is a discovery taxonomy, not a claim that each issue affects every country or is already a prescribed curriculum topic. Current-affairs activities must distinguish verified facts, projections, proposals, opinions and uncertainties.

## 5. Health, medicines and medical technology safeguards

Health projects must use current, authoritative public-health sources appropriate to the country, such as the national health ministry and relevant recognised public-health or research organisations. They may teach prevention, evidence appraisal, public-health communication, accessibility, service navigation and safe technology design.

Traditional and Indigenous knowledge should be approached respectfully and ethically, with consent where required. Traditional use alone does not prove that a remedy is effective or safe. Students may review published research and investigate evidence quality, but must not prescribe remedies, ingest or administer experimental preparations, test treatments on people or animals, disclose anyone's health status, or advise people to stop or delay professional care. Claims about cures, efficacy, dosage, safety or interactions require qualified review and strong appropriate evidence. Clearly distinguish laboratory or early-stage findings from established clinical evidence.

Student-designed diagnostic, treatment or monitoring devices must remain educational prototypes unless appropriately validated and authorised by qualified professionals and relevant regulators. Never represent an unvalidated prototype as a medical device ready for clinical use.

## 6. Age and ability progression

Use four adaptable bands, without treating a band as a fixed label of a learner's worth:
- **Supported Start:** Notice a familiar issue, sort observations, ask guided questions and choose from safe solution ideas.
- **Developing:** Describe evidence, identify a possible cause, make a simple conditional prediction and explain one solution.
- **Working at Class Goal:** Compare sources, explain patterns and assumptions, compare solution options and measure outcomes against criteria.
- **Extension:** Evaluate uncertainty and competing scenarios, critique methods and sources, design a justified prototype or pilot, analyse limitations and propose an improved next iteration.

The task's complexity, reading load, data, independence and technical depth must be adapted to the verified curriculum and learner's age, accessibility needs and demonstrated skills. For ECE and younger primary learners, use concrete observations, stories, pictures, simple patterns and teacher-guided safe activities. Progressively introduce quantitative forecasting, source critique, systems thinking and complex design in later levels.

## 7. Equity, safeguarding and privacy

Require teacher/adult supervision, school/community permission and specialist oversight proportionate to risk. Do not ask learners to confront suspected offenders, trespass, handle hazardous waste or chemicals, enter unsafe environments, collect sensitive personal/medical data, publish identifying details or conduct unsafe experiments. Use anonymised or aggregated public data wherever possible. Participation in personal or sensitive surveys must be voluntary and safeguarded; provide a non-disclosing alternative. Do not use student projects as a substitute for emergency services, professional medical care, legal advice or official disaster response.

## 8. Evidence record and evaluation

For each project, retain:
- project/issue ID, location or jurisdiction, date and question;
- sources, publication dates, data definitions/units, evidence types and limitations;
- baseline observations and identified signals;
- forecast scenarios, assumptions, timescale, confidence and update triggers;
- possible causes, affected groups and alternative explanations;
- solution options, costs, benefits, risks, maintenance and fairness/environmental considerations;
- selected plan, permissions, responsibilities, safety review and resources;
- curriculum version, class, subject, verified outcome/concept or `outcome-mapping-pending`;
- ability-band adaptation, assessment rubric, prototype version and test method;
- observed results versus success indicators, feedback, limitations and next iteration;
- reviewer, review date and change history.

A student should be assessed on the quality of reasoning, use of evidence, curriculum learning, safe design, collaboration, reflection and honest reporting—not on whether an uncertain forecast comes true or whether a complex community problem is solved by the student.

## 9. Publication and change-control gates

Do not publish a project as formally curriculum-aligned until its authoritative curriculum source, exact outcome/concept, teaching alignment, assessment criteria, class/subject match, age/accessibility fit and safety review have passed validation. Missing or stale mappings must block the formal curriculum-aligned label.

Research discoveries and changes in public-health or curriculum sources are review leads, not automatic permission to publish a new lesson or treatment claim. Recheck content when official curriculum versions, health guidance or source evidence changes. Preserve historical mappings and require qualified educator or health-professional review where appropriate. Any automated forecast must show its source, date, assumptions, uncertainty and limitations.

## 10. Validation requirements

Automated tests and review checks should flag:
- missing or unverifiable curriculum outcome mappings;
- invalid or mismatched country, curriculum version, class or subject;
- absent assessment criteria or evidence records;
- forecasts presented as facts or without dates, assumptions or uncertainty;
- medical claims lacking approved sources/review;
- unsafe testing, treatment substitution, sensitive-data collection or unapproved device claims;
- missing permissions, supervision or risk controls where required;
- unsupported claims that a project solved a population-level problem or caused an observed change.

A passing policy document is not proof that runtime enforcement, curriculum retrieval, forecasting, medical review or student project workflows are already implemented. Those capabilities require separate code, tests, educator review and verified deployment.
