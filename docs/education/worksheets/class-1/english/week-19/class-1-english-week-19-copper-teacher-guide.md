# TEACHER ONLY — Class 1 English Week 19 Marking Guide (Copper)

**Writing: Plan and compose a sentence**  
**Programme days:** 91–95 | **Term:** 3 | **Week:** 1  
**Pathway:** Copper | **Keep this guide separate from the learner worksheet.**

## Learning intention
Learner plans an idea orally, visually or with objects and composes a meaningful simple sentence. The four pathways share this objective; scaffolding and independence differ.

## Prompt and model in the learner worksheet
Picture or object prompt: a child places a red ball in a basket. If a picture is not available, the teacher may arrange a real ball and basket or describe the scene.

**Planning notes:** Who? a child. What? puts. What object? a red ball. Where? in a basket.

**Worked example:** The child puts the red ball in the basket.

## Indicative task responses
1. Choose the person: CHILD / BALL.

2. Choose the action: PUTS / SLEEPS.

3. Choose where the ball goes: IN THE BASKET / ON THE ROOF.

4. Use the word/picture cards or dictate a sentence about the scene. The teacher may write your exact words.

## Day-by-day evidence and marking

| Day | Evidence | Teacher guidance |
|---|---|---|
| 91 | Notices the prompt and identifies a person/object and action | Accept selection, matching, gesture, sign, AAC, speech, drawing or dictated response. Record whether the learner understood the scene. |
| 92 | Uses a model or planning prompt to organise an idea | Credit a meaningful plan even if it is not written as a sentence yet. Model who/what/where/when as appropriate. |
| 93 | Composes a sentence with guided support | Look for a clear idea and sensible word order. Accept word cards, dictated language or AAC composition. |

## Pathway-specific scaffolding and expected evidence

- **Copper:** Offer real objects or a clear tactile/large-print picture, two or three choices, adult modelling and ample wait time. Scribe exact learner language if requested. Expected evidence: communicates a person/action or a simple meaningful idea, with adult support to assemble or dictate a sentence.
- **Bronze:** Offer a small word/picture bank and a sentence frame; gradually reduce prompts. Expected evidence: arranges or selects words and composes a relevant simple sentence with a cue.
- **Silver:** Invite independent planning using who/action/object/where prompts. Expected evidence: composes a meaningful sentence and checks word order and spaces with minimal prompting.
- **Gold:** Invite more precise detail and a second related sentence, while keeping the main idea clear. Expected evidence: composes and reviews one or two connected sentences and can explain an editing choice.

## Marking principles

- Meaning is primary. Accept valid sentence structures and vocabulary different from the sample if they communicate an idea that matches the prompt.
- Accept oral language, sign, AAC, pointing with a scribed response, word-card arrangement, drawing with explanation or written work. Do not treat handwriting as the only evidence of composition.
- Do not penalise spelling, handwriting, accent, speech clarity, eye contact or speed unless explicitly taught as the target.
- Capital letters, word spacing and full stops should be noted at the level taught, not used to erase credit for a meaningful composed idea.
- For learners using dictated or AAC responses, the teacher may transcribe exactly and separately model conventional written punctuation.
- No fixed numerical pass threshold or permanent ability placement is assigned. Record: secure / developing / revisit, with evidence and next step.
