# Fiji Future Prosperity Learning Activities Blueprint

**Status:** Design and authoring blueprint; not a claim that all activities are implemented or curriculum-verified in the live app.

## Purpose
PacEdu should help every learner build the knowledge, practical skills, values and adaptability to improve personal wellbeing, strengthen Fiji, and contribute responsibly to other nations. These skills must be integrated through the applicable Fiji curriculum, not replace required curriculum content. Preserve the product label **ECE**. Scope: ECE, Years 1–13 (including Forms 3 and 4 where schools use those labels), and each subject actually offered at that level.

## Mandatory curriculum alignment
Every activity marked formally aligned must record country/jurisdiction, class/year/form, subject, curriculum/syllabus version and effective dates, official source title and URL/document, publication/version date, page/section, exact outcome/achievement-indicator/concept ID and wording where available, measurable objective, teaching steps, worksheet, answer guide/rubric, accessibility adaptations, ability band, safety/material requirements, review status and change history.

Do not invent outcome IDs, official quotations, syllabus versions, subject offerings or examination requirements. If the source or exact mapping is missing, set status to **outcome-mapping-pending**; allow only teacher-reviewed supplemental exploration and do not call it formally curriculum-aligned. Re-check sources when the Ministry publishes an approved revision. Monitoring or a policy document alone does not generate or publish lessons.

## Four ability bands
1. **Supported Start:** concrete examples, pictures/audio, small steps, worked examples, reduced task load and appropriate support.
2. **Developing:** guided practice, sentence starters, partly completed examples and two-option comparisons.
3. **Working at Class Goal:** independent practice at the verified class objective, explain reasoning and show working/evidence.
4. **Extension:** deeper reasoning, transfer to a new context, evaluate sources, compare designs, justify trade-offs and identify uncertainty.

Use diagnostic evidence, not labels such as “weak” or “poor.” Groups are flexible and subject-specific. Percentages may inform a teacher's judgement only where a valid rubric supports them; do not infer overall ability from one test or one camera/voice submission.

## Future-prosperity capabilities to integrate when curriculum-relevant
- Literacy, multilingual communication, numeracy, scientific reasoning and evidence-based decisions.
- Critical thinking, creativity, problem-solving, design, engineering and practical making.
- Digital/AI literacy, cybersecurity, privacy, media literacy and checking misinformation.
- Financial literacy, saving, budgeting, fair trade, entrepreneurship and responsible record-keeping.
- Agriculture, food security, nutrition, water, sanitation, renewable energy, waste reduction and climate/disaster resilience.
- Health promotion, emotional wellbeing, first-aid awareness, safeguarding, respectful relationships and knowing when to seek qualified help.
- Civic responsibility, ethics, human rights, inclusion, disability accessibility, peacebuilding, leadership and collaboration.
- Cultural heritage, local languages, traditional knowledge considered respectfully alongside evidence, and care for land and sea.
- Career exploration, TVET, employability, teamwork, project planning, repair, maintenance and lifelong learning.
- Global cooperation: compare how Fiji and other countries address similar challenges while respecting cultural context; never assume a foreign curriculum or solution transfers unchanged.
- Future foresight: distinguish evidence from opinion, notice signals, consider several possible futures, estimate uncertainty, prepare safe options and revise plans as evidence changes.

Assess learning and reasoning, not wealth, access to expensive materials, or whether a forecast turns out exactly right.

## Calendar-aware phases
Use the school's confirmed calendar and examination notices as the source of truth. Fiji Government's published 2026 dates are Term 1: 26 January–1 May; Term 2: 18 May–21 August; Term 3: 7 September–4 December. The current date is late October 2026, within Term 3; do not present Term 1/2 as upcoming. These dates do not imply every class has the same weekly plan or exam date.

Recommended configurable phases:
- **Opening/diagnostic:** baseline literacy, numeracy and subject evidence; identify support needs and goals.
- **Teaching/practice:** sequence daily tasks from verified outcomes; model, practise, check, reteach and extend.
- **Application:** apply taught concepts to local Fiji situations and carefully sourced international comparisons.
- **Revision:** retrieval practice, worked examples, misconceptions and spaced review; avoid introducing large amounts of untaught examinable content.
- **Assessment:** follow the school's approved assessment schedule and use transparent criteria and feedback.
- **Intervention:** short targeted practice based on evidence, followed by reassessment; never as punishment.
- **Holiday/real-world learning:** optional, safe, low-cost, offline-capable tasks; no requirement to purchase materials or have internet.

Teachers can configure a school-specific weekly map (including a 9-week, 5-week or revision/exam pattern when that is the confirmed plan). PacEdu must not impose one sequence on all schools.

## Illustrative activity patterns by learning stage
These are examples of task design, **not official achievement indicators**. Map each to the actual official syllabus before publication as aligned.

### ECE
- **Supported Start:** sort safe picture cards of local foods, plants, animals, transport and weather; point to/name items; practise sharing and handwashing routines.
- **Developing:** group objects by an observable feature and describe a difference using words, signs, pictures or home language where appropriate.
- **Working at Class Goal:** sequence a short story about caring for a classroom, garden or beach; count and compare small groups.
- **Extension:** draw a sequence showing how a family can reduce waste or save water and explain the choice orally or with symbols.

### Years 1–2
- **Supported Start:** match picture to word, count drawn objects and sequence three safe steps for handwashing or planting a seed.
- **Developing:** read a short Fiji-context passage, answer who/what/where questions and record a small count with pictures.
- **Working at Class Goal:** write short instructions, solve a class-level number problem and explain one step.
- **Extension:** compare two ways to reduce classroom waste and give a reason based on an observation.

### Years 3–4
- **Supported Start:** use a labelled picture, word bank and worked example to read a simple table or describe a local environment.
- **Developing:** collect and tally a small class dataset (not identifiable personal data) and write a short evidence-based paragraph.
- **Working at Class Goal:** calculate totals/differences, interpret a simple chart and propose an improvement with a reason.
- **Extension:** compare possible improvements by cost, fairness and likely effect; identify missing evidence.

### Years 5–6
- **Supported Start:** complete a scaffolded budget or labelled diagram and explain one key term.
- **Developing:** use a supplied table to calculate percentages or compare resource use; identify fact versus opinion.
- **Working at Class Goal:** prepare a short report combining a chart, written explanation and realistic recommendation about water, food waste, safety or energy.
- **Extension:** check source reliability, compare local evidence with a credible international source and explain why results may not transfer directly to Fiji.

### Years 7–8
- **Supported Start:** interpret a worked example and use a template to explain a verified syllabus concept.
- **Developing:** gather non-sensitive local observations, represent data and explain a pattern with guidance.
- **Working at Class Goal:** investigate a curriculum-linked community issue, compare solutions using evidence, estimate cost and justify a recommendation.
- **Extension:** analyse limitations, uncertainty and unintended consequences; present a safe model or simulation and an improvement plan.

### Years 9–10 (including Forms 3–4 where applicable)
- **Supported Start:** break a verified syllabus problem into steps, using a formula/diagram template and key vocabulary.
- **Developing:** solve a structured case study, compare sources and show working for a budget, measurement, experiment or written argument.
- **Working at Class Goal:** plan a small curriculum-linked investigation or design brief, record repeatable results, evaluate against criteria and communicate conclusions.
- **Extension:** compare designs, model costs/trade-offs, test assumptions, discuss ethics/sustainability and identify evidence needed before real-world deployment.

### Years 11–13
- **Supported Start:** revise prerequisites using worked examples, annotated sources and a checklist tied to the chosen syllabus.
- **Developing:** complete a guided subject case study, interpret evidence, prepare a budget/project plan and cite sources.
- **Working at Class Goal:** conduct a subject-appropriate investigation, technical design, business case, field study or analytical report aligned to the official syllabus and assessment guide.
- **Extension:** evaluate a prototype, software model, enterprise proposal, research question or policy option, including risk, lifecycle cost, maintenance, accessibility, uncertainty, source quality and realistic implementation.

Show subject-specific examples only when the subject is actually offered at that level and mapping is verified. Do not assume every subject is available in every class.

## Common daily worksheet record
Every printable/downloadable worksheet should include: (1) auto-generated local date, term/phase, country, class/form, subject and daily activity number; (2) official curriculum reference and mapping status; (3) learner-friendly objective; (4) short teaching/example section; (5) separate tasks for all four ability bands; (6) questions and answer space, using suitable matching, MCQ, true/false, short response, reading/audio or practical evidence; (7) separate answer key/rubric when needed; (8) success criteria, feedback and next-step/reteach recommendation; (9) safe low-cost materials and offline alternative; and (10) teacher approval, student-record linkage and portfolio evidence with privacy safeguards.

Camera/audio evidence can support assessment but is not infallible and should not be the sole basis for high-stakes marks. Provide a non-camera alternative, protect student data and require teacher review where appropriate.

## Safety and integrity gates
- Health activities teach prevention, reliable information and how to seek qualified care; never diagnose learners, test remedies on people, recommend stopping treatment or claim unvalidated cures.
- Children must not operate dangerous machinery or test occupied boats, vehicles, aircraft, mains electrical equipment, pressure systems, structures or clinical devices without qualified supervision and required approvals. Prefer models/simulations.
- Do not collect/publish sensitive health, household, identity or location data for class projects.
- No guarantees of business, financial or employment success. Teach ethical business, honest advertising and age-appropriate legal awareness.
- Do not penalise disability, poverty, lack of devices/connectivity, English fluency where it is not the assessed objective, or inability to buy supplies.
- Use credible sources, label uncertainty, cite evidence and review potentially harmful or misleading content.

## Implementation sequence
1. Verify an official Fiji curriculum/source registry by subject, class and version.
2. Map outcomes only from source documents; require teacher/curriculum specialist review before marking verified.
3. Author daily activities/worksheets per verified outcome, subject, class and ability band.
4. Add answer keys/rubrics and tests for missing sources/outcomes, wrong class/subject, unsafe instructions and unsupported claims.
5. Generate calendar-aware schedules from each school's confirmed calendar and assessment plan.
6. Pilot with teachers/learners, offline and low-cost options; record corrections.
7. Release incrementally only after tests, review and approval. This document alone does not activate a live-app feature.
