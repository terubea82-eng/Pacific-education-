# Form 7 English — Week 1 Teacher Marking Guide (Days 1–5)

**Status:** PENDING_TEACHER_REVIEW and PENDING_OFFICIAL_INDICATOR_VERIFICATION. PacEdu working guide only. These linked-source sets are original practice stimuli, not prescribed texts or official examination materials.

## Common marking principles
- Assess independent critical judgement, synthesis, source evaluation, methodological awareness, nuance, academic register and argument coherence.
- Accept defensible alternative interpretations and equivalent wording. Do not penalise accent, disability-related communication differences, handwriting speed or device access.
- Accept accessible oral, signed, AAC, dictated, Braille or written evidence. Record response mode, independence and prompting.
- Keep outcomes PENDING_TEACHER_REVIEW. Copper–Gold are flexible scaffolds, not permanent labels.

## Day 1 — Oral communication
**Expected:** nuanced thesis, integration of at least two sources, analysis of public benefit/individual rights, qualification and response to challenge.
- Source A supports intergenerational learning where language is used meaningfully; participation depends on access, time and priorities.
- Source B raises consent, review, access and withdrawal concerns.
- Source C shows that funding and data-retention rules remain unresolved.
- Strong recommendation may be conditional on clear governance, sustainable funding and contributor control.
- Next step: synthesise claims across sources instead of reporting them one at a time.

## Day 2 — Close reading
**Expected:** comparative thesis, close analysis of map/journey metaphors, ambiguity and nuanced explanation.
- Both writers value language and continuity.
- Text A presents language as a map connecting memory/place; Text B presents it as a journey shaped by change and borrowing.
- The metaphors imply different models of preservation: retaining routes versus adapting through use.
- Accept alternative readings when supported by both texts.
- Next step: compare assumptions and implications, not only similarities/differences.

## Day 3 — Vocabulary and language
**Expected:** cumulative analysis of rhetorical choices, audience positioning and conceptual gap.
- “Negotiated practice, not a display case” rejects static preservation and foregrounds participation.
- “Custodians of context” assigns responsibility but may shift institutional duties onto contributors.
- “Access without accountability is not inclusion” uses balanced syntax for force, but leaves accountability undefined.
- A strong revision specifies who is accountable, for what and how it is measured.
- Next step: connect rhetorical effect with conceptual precision.

## Day 4 — Critical literacy
**Expected:** compare authority/method, identify limits and unsupported claims, propose triangulation and qualify conclusion.
- Source A supports participant views in a small pilot; it does not prove long-term or district-wide fluency outcomes.
- Source B's claim that fluency doubled is unsupported without author, date or dataset.
- Source C records family concerns but is not an outcome study.
- Triangulation could combine transparent participant data, independent assessment over time, consultation records and clear methodology.
- Strong conclusion distinguishes educational promise from proven impact.
- Next step: state exactly what each method can and cannot establish.

## Day 5 — Writing and shaping
**Expected:** sustained critical synthesis with integrated evidence, methodological evaluation, governance analysis, counterargument and nuanced recommendation.
- Possible thesis: expansion should be conditional on better outcome evidence and enforceable consent/governance safeguards.
- Evidence: small pilot suggests participant value but does not prove long-term fluency; families raised consent/access/time/control concerns; funding and retention rules are unresolved.
- Reject the anonymous numerical claim as unsupported, not as conclusively false.
- Evaluate what further evidence is required and who should oversee it.
- Accept different recommendations if they distinguish claims, evidence, limitations and ethical conditions.
- Next step: build a thesis-led argument with integrated sources and explicit methodological evaluation.

## Pathway support
- Copper: source labels, glossary, annotated model and thesis/evidence frame.
- Bronze: source comparison planner and reduced prompts.
- Silver: independent synthesis and transparent evaluation.
- Gold: methodological critique, competing interpretations and nuanced qualification.
