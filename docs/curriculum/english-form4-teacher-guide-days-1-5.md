# Form 4 English — Week 1 Teacher Marking Guide (Days 1–5)

**Status:** PENDING_TEACHER_REVIEW and PENDING_OFFICIAL_INDICATOR_VERIFICATION. PacEdu working guide only. The five practice stimuli are original, not prescribed Ministry texts.

## Common marking principles
- Assess reasoning, evidence, interpretation, language choices, audience and purpose. Do not score accent, speech difference, disability, handwriting speed or device access as errors.
- Accept equivalent wording, well-supported alternative interpretations and accessible oral, signed, AAC, dictated, Braille or written responses.
- Record evidence, response mode, independence, prompts and next teaching step. Keep PENDING_TEACHER_REVIEW until educator review.
- Copper, Bronze, Silver and Gold are flexible scaffolds, not permanent learner labels.

## Day 1 — Oral communication
**Expected:** reasoned position, two accurate details, explanation of significance, limitation and response to counter-question.
- Possible position: multilingual public signs can support recognition and understanding if translations are reviewed.
- Evidence: visitors may feel recognised; fluent-speaker review and reader testing were suggested; shared-language version remains available.
- Limitation: inconsistent or poorly checked translations may confuse readers, especially on safety notices.
- If fluent speakers disagree, credit a practical verification process: consult additional qualified speakers, compare context and usage, test comprehension with intended readers, and refer safety-critical wording to the responsible authority.
- Accept a different defensible position if evidence and reasoning are sound.
- Next step: rehearse claim → evidence → explanation → limitation → response.

## Day 2 — Close reading
**Expected:** developed interpretation, precise evidence and explanation of viewpoint, imagery, contrast and final question.
- Possible interpretation: community identity can remain connected to older memories while incorporating new voices and experiences.
- Evidence: familiar mango tree and altered noticeboard; grandmother explains a word; new shop plays music from a neighbouring island.
- Viewpoint: close third-person perspective follows Litia's reflections and realisation.
- Final question invites reflection about who has authority to represent a place; it does not supply a single fixed answer.
- Avoid unsupported assumptions about the village or its residents.
- Next step: move from detail to inference to explanation of authorial effect.

## Day 3 — Vocabulary and language
**Expected:** contextual connotation, figurative meaning, audience/purpose and formal adaptation.
- “Exchange” suggests reciprocal sharing; “contest” implies competition or ranking.
- “Leave room for voices” is figurative language encouraging inclusion and listening.
- Audience: newsletter readers, especially students; purpose: invite participation and respectful listening.
- Formal adaptation should use neutral, precise language, e.g. invite staff to contribute a word, story or question and listen respectfully to others. Credit equivalent formal notices.
- Next step: compare the same message across informal, inclusive and formal registers.

## Day 4 — Critical literacy
**Expected:** compare purpose, source accountability, confirmed details, omissions and unsupported claims.
- Message A is supported by an identified district information office and specific venue/time; it refers readers to official updates.
- Message B is anonymous and adds unconfirmed claims about free buses, meals and prizes. The district office confirms only the time, venue and free entry.
- Useful questions: Who authored the forwarded message? Has the district office confirmed each added claim? Where is the official access/transport update? When was the message last updated?
- Credit a nuanced judgement: Message A is more reliable for confirmed logistics, while still directing readers to check updates.
- Next step: compare source, claim, evidence, omission and verification route.

## Day 5 — Writing and shaping
**Expected:** multi-paragraph recommendation, clear position, at least three relevant details, developed explanation, counterargument and conclusion.
- Strong recommendations include voluntary contributions, multiple formats, permission for recordings, contributor checking, privacy protection and labels identifying individual perspectives rather than claiming to represent an entire community.
- Counterargument may note that a small number of contributions could make the display appear incomplete or representative of everyone. A response can label contributions accurately, invite more voluntary voices and explain the limits of the display.
- Credit a coherent structure and explicit audience awareness; do not require one exact paragraph formula.
- Next step: plan argument and evidence, draft, then revise for cohesion and precision.

## Pathway evidence
- Copper: demonstrates the analytical move with model, accessible excerpt, gloss or step-by-step prompts; record support.
- Bronze: uses a planning frame and evidence locator with prompts reduced over the task.
- Silver: independently selects evidence and develops explanation.
- Gold: qualifies an interpretation, evaluates limitations or develops a counterargument while retaining the same objective.
