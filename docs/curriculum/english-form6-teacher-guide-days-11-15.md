# Form 6 English — Week 3 Teacher Marking Guide (Days 11–15)

**Status:** PENDING_TEACHER_REVIEW and PENDING_OFFICIAL_INDICATOR_VERIFICATION. Original practice sources only; not prescribed texts or examination materials.

## Common marking principles
- Assess critical comparison, synthesis, evidence precision, source evaluation, qualification and sustained cohesion.
- Accept well-supported alternative interpretations and accessible oral, signed, AAC, dictated, Braille or written responses. Do not score accent, disability-related communication differences, handwriting speed or device access as errors.
- Record evidence, response mode, independence and prompts. Keep PENDING_TEACHER_REVIEW.
- Pathways are flexible scaffolds, not permanent ability labels.

## Day 11 — Oral communication
**Expected:** comparative thesis, evidence from both sources, qualified judgement and practical response to broader consultation.
- Minutes establish that a committee reviewed proposals and agreed to a one-month timetable trial; they do not capture all discussion or reasons.
- Interview gives one household's experience, including both improvement and difficulty; it is not representative of all households.
- A fair judgement requires wider evidence: households across distances, work schedules and access needs should be heard. Credit recommendations for surveys, further interviews, trial monitoring or an accessible feedback process.
- Next step: distinguish formal decision records from individual lived experience.

## Day 12 — Close reading
**Expected:** compare voice, structure, register and omission in representations of collective memory.
- Oral history uses first-person sensory memory and a sequence of sounds/actions to foreground social relationships.
- Catalogue note uses compressed institutional language, dates and categories to document formal use; it explicitly notes the absence of personal accounts.
- Neither source is automatically more truthful. Each is useful for different questions and has limits.
- Next step: explain how source form influences what can be remembered and recorded.

## Day 13 — Vocabulary and language
**Expected:** analyse metaphor, connotation, register and produce an accurate revision.
- “Beating heart” is a metaphor presenting the harbour as central and energetic.
- “Unlocked opportunity” is positive evaluative language implying that redevelopment enabled benefits.
- “Pushed familiar routines to the margins” suggests displacement or reduced importance, but requires evidence about which routines and whose experience.
- Source B records increased loading capacity and changed routes; it says informal trading and daily travel time are not quantified.
- Credit a revision that separates documented changes from interpretation and does not overclaim social impact.
- Next step: calibrate evaluative language to available evidence.

## Day 14 — Critical literacy
**Expected:** identify source purposes, evidence, limitations, projections and missing evidence.
- Authority statement lists proposed design features and projected benefits but provides no before/after waiting-time data.
- User submission records three access concerns but does not represent all passengers and lacks route measurements.
- Further evidence: timed boarding observations before/after opening; measured walking distances and route gradients; accessible-user testing; broader passenger survey; shaded-rest-point mapping.
- Credit other relevant evidence and qualified conclusions.
- Next step: use claim/evidence/source/limit/missing evidence for comparative evaluation.

## Day 15 — Writing and shaping
**Expected:** sustained critical essay with thesis, integrated evidence, evaluation, counterargument and reasoned conclusion.
- Forum summary records benefits and concerns but voluntary attendance means not all user groups are represented.
- Project review describes intended features and recommends future measurement; it does not contain post-opening results.
- A sound thesis may argue that success cannot be established from plans and voluntary comments alone; outcome data and inclusive access evaluation are needed.
- Credit counterarguments that recognise the potential benefits of improved facilities while requiring fair access and evidence of outcomes.
- Next step: plan a comparative thesis, integrate sources, qualify claims and revise cohesion.

## Pathway evidence
- Copper: identifies one accurate detail per source with model/frame and recorded prompts.
- Bronze: organises comparison using a source grid and paragraph frame.
- Silver: independently integrates precise evidence and evaluates limitations.
- Gold: synthesises competing perspectives and develops a nuanced, qualified conclusion.
