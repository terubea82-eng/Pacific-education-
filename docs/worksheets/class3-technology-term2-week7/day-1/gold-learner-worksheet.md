### Read this passage
The class wants a portable organiser for pencils, rulers and erasers. It must be light enough for a learner to carry, strong enough to keep its shape, stable when placed on a desk, and safe to handle. Litia considers a reused plastic container and a clean cardboard carton. The container may resist water but could have sharp cut edges if modified. The carton is easy to fold and label but may soften when wet. Litia writes design criteria before choosing: capacity for the items, a stable base, smooth edges and a handle that will not tear. She sketches two possible layouts, asks an adult to inspect the materials, and plans a test using the same set of school items in each model.

### Worked example
A fair comparison keeps the test conditions the same. If both models hold the same items and are carried the same short distance, the class can compare stability and damage. A design should be judged against its criteria, not just its appearance.
