# Form 7 English — Week 5 Teacher Marking Guide (Days 21–25)

**Status:** PENDING_TEACHER_REVIEW and PENDING_OFFICIAL_INDICATOR_VERIFICATION. Original practice sources only; not prescribed texts or examination materials.

## Common marking principles
- Assess critical synthesis, evidence precision, contextual understanding, consent, source limitations and sustained cohesion.
- Accept well-supported alternative interpretations and accessible oral, signed, AAC, dictated, Braille or written responses. Do not score accent, disability-related communication differences, handwriting speed or device access as errors.
- Record evidence, response mode, independence and prompting. Keep PENDING_TEACHER_REVIEW until reviewed.
- Pathways are flexible scaffolds, not permanent learner labels.

## Day 21 — Oral communication
**Expected:** reasoned recommendation, evidence from both sources, consideration of knowledge holders and a fair response to the creativity concern.
- Arts group statement reports retaining language and central message, adapting rhythm/staging and inviting elders to review words.
- Practitioner interview supports youth interest but stresses that some chants have specific contexts and that consultation should include relevant knowledge holders.
- Credit recommendations for context review, consent, consultation, explanatory notes or choosing a different performance format where necessary.
- Counterargument may explain that clear guidance can support learning rather than prohibit creativity; avoid treating one interview as a universal rule.
- Next step: distinguish adaptation, permission, context and representation.

## Day 22 — Close reading
**Expected:** compare perspective, imagery, structure and qualification.
- Personal reflection uses first-person memory, the tin box metaphor, pause and reflective question to connect inherited material with a new version.
- Museum label uses formal descriptive language and qualifies the account by noting local variations and differing views.
- Credit alternative readings of the new version when supported by precise details.
- Next step: explain how perspective shapes a reader's view of continuity and change.

## Day 23 — Vocabulary and language
**Expected:** analyse connotation and framing, identify audience and rewrite without overclaiming authenticity.
- “Authentic” may imply a single standard of genuineness; explain why this may flatten variation.
- “Ancient traditions come alive” can frame living practices as belonging only to the past.
- “Selected” and “participating” narrow the scope of the curatorial claim.
- Suitable rewrite: “Explore performances developed by participating community groups, reflecting selected contemporary interpretations and varied local practices.” Credit equivalent accurate wording.
- Next step: use language that recognises variation and living change.

## Day 24 — Critical literacy
**Expected:** evaluate consent, selection and representativeness.
- Organiser statement establishes that six participating groups approved their supplied descriptions; it does not establish that all families or groups were invited.
- Community network response raises concerns about exclusion and the risk of treating a small selection as representative; it does not quantify the number or views of those omitted.
- Further checks: publish selection criteria; record who was invited and how; provide accessible ways to contribute; obtain consent for descriptions and performances; label scope/variation accurately; invite corrections.
- Next step: distinguish consent to participate from authority to represent others.

## Day 25 — Writing and shaping
**Expected:** sustained recommendation with thesis, integrated evidence, counterargument, limitation analysis and practical procedures.
- Policy includes group proposals, review of descriptions and change requests but lacks selection criteria and disagreement procedures.
- Advisory note supports youth creativity while recommending consent, context notes, correction and an explicit statement about limited representation.
- Counterargument: too many procedural safeguards may discourage participation. A reasoned response can make safeguards proportionate, clear and supportive rather than restrictive.
- Credit procedures for transparent invitations, voluntary participation, consent, context review, correction/withdrawal and a fair disagreement process.
- Next step: revise thesis, source integration, ethical reasoning and practical conclusion.

## Pathway evidence
- Copper: identifies evidence from both sources with source labels, model and recorded prompts.
- Bronze: organises a comparison with a frame and reduces prompts.
- Silver: independently analyses context, consent and evidence.
- Gold: synthesises perspectives, qualifies principles and evaluates ethical implications.
