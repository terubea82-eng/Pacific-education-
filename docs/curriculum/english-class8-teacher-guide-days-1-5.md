# Class 8 English — Week 1 Teacher Marking Guide (Days 1–5)

**Status:** PENDING_TEACHER_REVIEW and PENDING_OFFICIAL_INDICATOR_VERIFICATION. PacEdu working guide only; not an official Ministry marking schedule.

## Common marking principles
- Assess the shared daily objective, not handwriting speed, speech fluency, accent, device access or disability.
- Accept oral, signed, Braille, AAC, dictated, pointed, drawn or written evidence where it demonstrates the same learning.
- Accept synonyms, equivalent wording and well-supported alternative interpretations.
- Record evidence, response mode, accuracy, prompting and next teaching step. Keep results PENDING_TEACHER_REVIEW until reviewed.
- Pathways are flexible scaffolds, not fixed ability labels.

## Day 1 — Listening and speaking
**Expected evidence:** a relevant summary and one accurate supporting detail, followed by a reasoned response about Ana's actions.
- Main message: listening and welcoming can help a new learner feel included.
- Accept details: Ana invited Mere to sit with the group, listened without interrupting, asked about her old home, or the display included both communities.
- Credit an explanation that listening showed interest and respect. Do not require exact model wording.
- Next step: model identifying the main message separately from a supporting detail, then reduce prompts.

## Day 2 — Reading and viewing
**Expected evidence:** contrast Litia's concern with Jone's view and infer why participation increased, supported by two details.
- Litia values familiar songs and games and worries change may weaken traditions.
- Jone believes traditions can remain meaningful while new voices are welcomed.
- Supported inference may mention compromise, shared ownership or inclusion. Evidence includes keeping the opening song and inviting families to contribute an activity.
- Next step: practise claim/detail/inference using a shorter paragraph if evidence is not yet linked.

## Day 3 — Vocabulary and language
**Expected evidence:** context-appropriate meanings for three words and a justified replacement for 'lively'.
- hesitant: unsure, reluctant, uncertain, not confident.
- reassured: comforted, encouraged, made to feel safe or confident.
- represented: stood for, symbolised, showed or expressed.
- Accept active, animated, energetic, busy or another context-appropriate synonym for lively; require a tone explanation if the replacement changes it.
- Next step: teach context clues by reading the sentence before and after the target word.

## Day 4 — Grammar and sentence craft
**Expected evidence:** two accurate revisions using contrast/cause and an explanation of the relationship.
- Example contrast: 'Although some students chose photographs, others chose drawings or objects.' Accept equivalent punctuation and clause order.
- Cause revision may explain that the teacher offered different formats because one format cannot represent every story; accept accurate alternatives.
- Credit correct capitalisation, clause structure and punctuation without insisting on one sentence.
- Next step: rehearse joining ideas with although, because, while or another suitable conjunction.

## Day 5 — Writing and shaping
**Expected evidence:** one coherent paragraph with a clear point, at least two relevant supporting details and a reasoned conclusion.
- Possible point: schools build belonging by allowing learners to contribute in different ways.
- Supporting details may include stories, drawings, song titles, family words, objects, home languages, sign, AAC, observation or trusted support.
- Reasoned conclusion should explain why choice and respect matter.
- Assess coherence, relevance and reasoning; accept a well-organised dictated, signed or AAC paragraph. Gold extension may compare approaches while retaining the same objective.
- Next step: use a point/evidence/explanation/conclusion frame, then gradually remove prompts.
