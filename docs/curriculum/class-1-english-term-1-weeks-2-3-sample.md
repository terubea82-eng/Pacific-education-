## Design and access rules

- Use familiar, locally checked words and objects. Learners may answer by speaking, pointing, choosing a picture, drawing, using an approved communication aid, or writing.
- The four versions are flexible task supports, not permanent labels: **Supported Start**, **Developing**, **Current Class Goal**, and **Extension**.
- Day 1 of Week 2 is a short check-in, not a new grade or permanent ability decision.
- A teacher checks student identity before attaching evidence to a student record. Do not put private learner information on a public worksheet.
- Keep the student worksheet separate from the teacher-only answer guidance. Teacher reviews any automated scoring before using it for important decisions.
- No device, paid material, food, or home resource is required.

## Week 2 focus — self, familiar words, and the short vowel /a/

**Learning intention:** learners listen and respond to simple questions about themselves, identify a familiar spoken word, practise the letter Aa and its sound where appropriate, and complete a short sentence.

**Teacher preparation:** check that examples and pictures are familiar to the class. Do not require children to disclose their age, family details, or other personal information; they may choose a school object instead.

### Day 1 — Check-in: listening, naming, and choice

1. Give one instruction: “Point to the book.” Repeat once if needed.
2. Offer two familiar pictures/objects and ask the learner to name or indicate one.
3. Show Aa and ask the learner to identify, trace, say, or select it from two choices.
4. Say “apple” and ask which sound they hear at the beginning. Accept an oral or picture response.
5. Invite the learner to complete “I am ___” with a safe choice such as a name, role, favourite colour, or school object.

**Flexible versions**
- **Supported Start:** model the instruction, offer two objects, and allow pointing or matching.
- **Developing:** repeat the instruction once and let the learner name or select the object.
- **Current Class Goal:** follow the instruction and respond to the short sentence prompt.
- **Extension:** give a second instruction or create another meaningful “I am…” sentence.

**Record:** attempted skill, support provided, response mode, and observed response. Use this evidence only to choose the next practice step.

### Day 2 — Hear and notice /a/ in a familiar word

**Teach:** listen to a word and notice its first sound. Use a clear teacher model; pronunciation can vary by accent, so focus on the agreed classroom sound model.

**Worked example:** apple begins with the short /a/ sound in the selected classroom model.

**Tasks**
1. Listen to “apple” and select its picture or word card.
2. Choose which starts with the target sound: apple / sun.
3. Trace or form Aa, or select the matching letter.
4. Say, point to, or draw another teacher-checked example if one is familiar to the learner.

**Flexible versions**
- **Supported Start:** choose between apple and sun after the teacher models both.
- **Developing:** choose the target word from two pictures without a model.
- **Current Class Goal:** identify Aa and select the matching initial sound from three teacher-checked choices.
- **Extension:** produce or draw another checked example and explain how the first sound matches.

### Day 3 — Match words to objects

**Teach:** a word can name something we can see or use. Match a spoken word with a picture or safe real object.

**Worked example:** teacher says “bag”; learner points to or selects the bag.

**Tasks**
1. Match “bag” to a picture/object.
2. Match “apple” to a picture/object, if the word and picture are familiar.
3. Hear the sentence “I have a bag.” Answer “What do I have?”
4. Complete orally, by selecting, drawing, or writing: “I see a ___.”

**Flexible versions**
- **Supported Start:** match one word from two choices.
- **Developing:** match two spoken words to two pictures.
- **Current Class Goal:** match three words and complete the sentence.
- **Extension:** make a new sentence using one matched word and show its meaning with a drawing or object.

### Day 4 — Listen and respond to simple questions

**Read aloud:** “Ari has a bag. The bag is blue.” Use a picture or a real bag if available.

**Tasks**
1. Who has a bag?
2. What colour is the bag?
3. Select or draw the object described.
4. Say, point to, or write one short sentence about a classroom object.

**Flexible versions**
- **Supported Start:** point to Ari and choose between two colour cards.
- **Developing:** answer both questions with one-word responses or picture choices.
- **Current Class Goal:** answer both questions and complete “The bag is ___.”
- **Extension:** add one detail of your own and identify which detail came from the text and which you added.

### Day 5 — Review and next-step choice

1. Identify Aa from two letter choices.
2. Listen and select the word that begins with the target sound: apple / sun.
3. Match “bag” to a picture or object.
4. Answer “Who has a bag?” after hearing the two-sentence text.
5. Complete “I see a ___” in an accessible response mode.

**Feedback routine:** state what the learner did correctly, model one next step, and allow one retry. Record independent / completed with prompt / not yet shown / not attempted. Do not turn this brief review into a high-stakes grade.

## Week 3 focus — following instructions, describing objects, and making a complete sentence

**Learning intention:** learners follow one- and two-step instructions as appropriate, describe a familiar object using a word, and understand a short spoken or read-aloud text.

### Day 1 — Follow a classroom instruction

**Teach:** listen until the instruction is finished, then do the action. Use safe actions only.

**Worked example:** “Pick up your pencil.” The learner may point to the pencil if picking it up is not accessible.

**Tasks**
1. Follow “Point to the door.”
2. Follow “Touch or point to your book.”
3. When ready, follow two steps: “Point to the bag, then point to the book.”
4. Tell or show which instruction was first.

**Flexible versions**
- **Supported Start:** one instruction, gesture, and two choices.
- **Developing:** one instruction without gesture, repeated once if needed.
- **Current Class Goal:** follow two familiar steps in order.
- **Extension:** explain or demonstrate the order of a two-step instruction, then give a safe instruction for a partner.

### Day 2 — Describe an object

**Teach:** describing words tell us more about a person, place, animal, or thing. Use observable features such as colour or size without judging a learner or their belongings.

**Worked example:** “The bag is blue.”

**Tasks**
1. Select a colour card that matches a classroom object.
2. Complete “The ___ is ___.”
3. Choose which sentence matches a picture.
4. Describe another familiar object using one suitable word.

**Flexible versions**
- **Supported Start:** choose from two colour cards and complete the sentence orally or by selecting.
- **Developing:** use one describing word in a sentence frame.
- **Current Class Goal:** independently complete a sentence about a pictured object.
- **Extension:** write or dictate two sentences and explain which word gives the description.

### Day 3 — Understand a short text

**Read aloud:** “Litia has a red cup. She puts the cup on the table.”

**Tasks**
1. Who has a cup?
2. What colour is the cup?
3. Where does she put the cup?
4. Put two picture cards in the order described, or explain the order orally.

**Flexible versions**
- **Supported Start:** answer who/what by pointing to a choice.
- **Developing:** answer two questions with words or picture choices.
- **Current Class Goal:** answer all three questions and order the two actions.
- **Extension:** retell the text in order and add one clearly identified detail of your own.

### Day 4 — Build a complete sentence

**Teach:** a sentence tells a complete idea. Model speaking the sentence before writing it. Capital letters and end punctuation are introduced according to the teacher’s current sequence.

**Worked example:** “The cup is red.”

**Tasks**
1. Choose words/pictures to complete “The cup is ___.”
2. Arrange word cards or dictate the sentence “The bag is blue.”
3. Identify the capital letter at the beginning when shown the sentence.
4. Create a sentence about a teacher-approved picture or object.

**Flexible versions**
- **Supported Start:** select a missing word from two choices.
- **Developing:** complete a sentence frame using a picture/word bank.
- **Current Class Goal:** say or write one complete sentence and identify its beginning.
- **Extension:** make two connected sentences and check that each communicates a complete idea.

### Day 5 — Review and demonstrate learning

1. Follow one spoken instruction.
2. Describe a familiar object using one suitable word.
3. Answer one who, one what, and one where question about the Week 3 text.
4. Complete or dictate one sentence about a picture.
5. Choose one task to retry after feedback.

**Next-step rule:** if a skill is not yet shown, offer a simpler choice or model and revisit it later. If shown with support, gradually remove the prompt. If independent, use a new familiar example before moving on.

## Teacher marking and evidence guidance

Use formative evidence rather than a fixed ability label. For each skill, record one of:
- **Independent**
- **Completed with prompt**
- **Not yet shown**
- **Not attempted**

Add the activity date, concept, response mode, support used, and one next step. A single short sample must not be treated as a complete measure of a learner’s ability. Verify identity before linking evidence to the student record. Keep teacher guidance private until the teacher releases it.
