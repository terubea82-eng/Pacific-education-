# Teacher-only guide — Class 2 English Week 6 (Gold)

**Days 26–30 | Shared reading and meaning**

**Matching learner file:** class-2-english-week-06-gold-learner.md

## Learning intention
Interpret a shared text by connecting details, vocabulary, and the central message. Support an explanation with evidence from the text.

## Preparation and access
Allow independent reading and rereading. Prompt the learner to justify an interpretation with a specific detail. Accept oral, signed, AAC, drawn, or written responses; do not require handwriting as the only evidence.

Read the exact passage in the matching learner worksheet. The passage is repeated here for teacher reference:

> A SHARED GARDEN, A SHARED RESPONSIBILITY
> The empty strip of ground beside our classroom did not seem useful, so the class planned a small garden. We loosened the soil and planted bean seeds in rows. Each morning, Mia checked whether the soil was dry, and Tom removed weeds that competed with the young plants for water. Several days later, green shoots pushed through the soil. Watching the change helped us understand the text's central message: living things grow best when people notice their needs and care for them consistently.

## Indicative answer guide
1. **Explain the central message of the text.**
   - Indicative answer: Living things grow best when people notice their needs and care for them consistently.
2. **Why might weeds harm the bean plants?**
   - Indicative answer: They compete with the young plants for water.
3. **What does “consistently” suggest about the class's care?**
   - Indicative answer: It happened regularly/steadily, not just once.
4. **Which two details show the class planned and shared responsibility?**
   - Indicative answer: They planted in rows; Mia checked soil; Tom removed weeds; any two relevant details.
5. **How does the final sentence connect the garden events to a wider lesson?**
   - Indicative answer: It explains that the class's actions show how regular care helps living things grow.

Accept accurate equivalent wording and evidence communicated through speech, sign, AAC, pointing, drawing, sequencing, or writing. Do not penalise spelling or handwriting when these are not the target skill. Give credit for the meaning demonstrated.
