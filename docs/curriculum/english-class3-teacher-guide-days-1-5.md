## Day 1 — Listening and speaking: A Place We Belong
**Learning intention:** identify a main message and support it with one detail.
**Worked answer:** Main message: people value different places for different reasons. Detail examples: Mere chose the community hall because her family meets friends there; Jone chose the football field because he practises with cousins; Ana chose the library because she can read quietly and borrow books.
**Pathway evidence:** Copper selects the correct main message and matches one character/place; Bronze uses the sentence frame and gives a respectful question; Silver independently states the message and one detail; Gold compares two reasons and asks an open question.
**Marking (4 points):** 1 main message; 1 accurate supporting detail; 1 relevant response/question; 1 respectful communication. Accept equivalent wording, signed/AAC/pointed/dictated responses and home-language explanation where meaning is clear. Do not score accent or speech fluency.

## Day 2 — Reading and viewing: The School Garden
**Learning intention:** state a central idea and identify two supporting details.
**Worked answer:** Central idea: Class 3 is caring for a school garden. Supporting details include planting bean seeds, watering them, checking soil, removing weeds and keeping a growth chart.
**Pathway evidence:** Copper selects the central idea and one correct detail; Bronze completes the sentence frame with two details; Silver states the idea and two details; Gold explains how the growth chart supports the work and distinguishes main idea from detail.
**Marking (4 points):** 1 central idea; 1 first accurate detail; 1 second distinct accurate detail; 1 explanation/link between details and idea. Accept paraphrase and direct evidence from the passage.

## Day 3 — Vocabulary and language: A Busy Market Morning
**Learning intention:** explain three words from context and use a suitable synonym.
**Worked answer:** lively = active/busy/energetic; vendors = people who sell goods; patiently = calmly or without becoming upset while waiting. Suitable synonym for lively: busy.
**Pathway evidence:** Copper matches the given definitions; Bronze completes the supported definitions and rewrites the sentence; Silver explains all three words and rewrites the sentence; Gold explains the effect of “patiently” and a nuance between synonyms.
**Marking (5 points):** up to 1 point for each accurate word meaning (3); 1 suitable synonym; 1 sensible rewritten sentence preserving meaning. Accept close synonyms with context-appropriate meaning.

## Day 4 — Grammar and sentence craft: Working Together
**Learning intention:** combine related ideas with a suitable conjunction and punctuate correctly.
**Worked answers:** “Litia carried the watering can, and Saki filled it with water.” “They watered the young plants, so the soil became damp.” Other grammatically correct combinations are acceptable.
**Pathway evidence:** Copper completes one supported “and” sentence; Bronze combines two pairs using “so” and another suitable conjunction; Silver combines two pairs independently; Gold writes two versions and explains how the conjunction changes the relationship.
**Marking (4 points):** 1 each for a complete combined sentence, suitable conjunction, clear meaning and correct capital/end punctuation. Award punctuation credit according to the sentence structure taught; do not penalise a valid alternative construction.

## Day 5 — Writing and shaping: A Place That Matters
**Learning intention:** write a focused paragraph with a clear main point and relevant supporting details.
**Worked example:** The sample paragraph in the learner worksheet models a topic sentence, supporting details and a closing idea.
**Pathway evidence:** Copper communicates a place and reason with the frame; Bronze produces 4–5 connected sentences with a plan; Silver produces about 5–7 sentences with topic sentence, three details and closing sentence; Gold produces a 7–9 sentence paragraph with precise details and varied openings.
**Marking (5 points):** 1 clear topic/main point; 1 relevant detail; 1 additional relevant details (up to 2); 1 logical sequence/cohesion; 1 closing sentence. Use the same objective across pathways; adjust prompting and quantity, not the learner's worth or access to the curriculum.

## Common accessibility and assessment rules
- Offer Braille, large print, screen-reader-friendly text, audio directions, sign, AAC, pointing, eye gaze, object choice, dictation, typing, scribing or handwriting as appropriate.
- Provide extra processing time, sensory breaks and adapted tools when needed. Do not treat a lack of speech, device access, handwriting or a missed/inaccessible item as proof of low ability.
- Record the evidence actually observed, the response mode, the prompt level and the next teaching step.
- Copper, Bronze, Silver and Gold are flexible evidence-based support pathways, not permanent learner labels.
- Accept valid equivalent answers and similar words where meaning is accurate.
